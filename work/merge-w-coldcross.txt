THE QUESTION, AND THE MEASUREMENT
---------------------------------
`w-gateperf` §11.1 declined worktree-independent case directories and
said, in its own words, that the decline rested on REASONING about the
lock's cold fallback and not on a measurement. This lane measured it.

  * `scripts/sweep_gen.py` writes all 19,556 cases in **0.65 s**.
  * `scripts/mode_cross.sh` takes its case-directory lock before that
    generation and releases it in an EXIT trap, so it holds it for the
    **entire run — 29 s warm, 347 s cold**. The exposure window is ~500x
    the hazard window.

Driven, both arms, N launched simultaneously, 3,000-cell stride, every
run printing `checked=2939 mismatches=0 graded=2919 ungraded=20`:

  naive  (one MUTABLE shared dir — the arrangement that was declined)
         N=2 -> FALLBACKS 1 of 2 · N=3 -> 2 of 3 · N=4 -> 3 of 4
  shared (content-addressed, IMMUTABLE, no lock)
         N=2/3/4 -> FALLBACKS **0 of N**

and at N=4 three of the four runs LOST the per-worktree lock and were
warm anyway. The lock is untouched and still fires; what changed is that
losing it no longer costs anything.

**`w-gateperf`'s inference was correct and its premise was replaceable.**
It priced a MUTABLE corpus, and on that premise the trade really is bad —
priced out, the naive share is a PERMANENT 3 x 347 s tax where
per-worktree pays 4 x 347 s ONCE. The prerequisite it named ("the
regeneration made non-destructive first") was the right one; the cheaper
form of it is immutability.

WHAT SHIPPED
------------
`scripts/corpus_dir.sh` — the corpus lives at
`<main-repo>/work/corpus/gen-<digest>`, the digest over `sweep_gen.py`
and every file in `sweep.d/` with RELATIVE names:

  * content-addressed, so two lanes with different corpora get different
    directories and cannot overwrite each other — this closes board
    #3249's hazard by construction rather than avoiding it by not
    sharing;
  * published by rename(2), so a reader never sees a half-built corpus
    and two racing publishers converge (driven, not argued);
  * immutable after publication, so NO LOCK IS TAKEN OR NEEDED;
  * VERIFIED ON EVERY RUN — each run still generates its own private
    corpus and adopts the shared one only if `diff -rq` finds all 19,556
    files byte-identical. 1.2 s including the generation.

That verification did not exist: both drivers regenerated and graded
their corpus with no check beyond `count > 0`.

A NUMBER IN THE DISPATCH IS WRONG, AND EVERYTHING DOWNSTREAM MOVES
-------------------------------------------------------------------
The dispatch, `w-gateperf` §2 and board #3265 all quote the cold cross as
**1,261 s** / "~19 minutes per new lane". **That is a `--jobs 4`
measurement**, and `w-gateperf` itself moved the standing gate flag to
`--jobs 16` (#3263). Re-measured at 16: cold cross **347 s**, cold sweep
fill **92 s**, whole cold gate **510 s**. 1,261/347 = 3.6x, the
concurrency ratio.

So the per-lane arithmetic the dispatch asked for is **73 %, not 94 %**:
against a 78 s warm gate, a twice-gating lane's lifetime is 588 s, of
which the cold excess is 432 s (73 %) and 381 s of that (88 %) is
shareable. **The lane removes 65 % of a twice-gating lane's whole gate
cost.** Still worth doing; the honest headline is 6 minutes, not 19.
