==============================================================================
WHICH GRID DECIDES THE *TRUNCATING* DIVISION
==============================================================================
  Q-QUOT and Q-QUOT-CEIL are the same rule up to rounding, and they
  only differ on a size the alignment does NOT divide.
    GRID-W /O1   separating cells   0   truncating right on 0, ceiling right on 0
    GRID-W all   separating cells   0   truncating right on 0, ceiling right on 0
    GRID-M       separating cells   4   truncating right on 4, ceiling right on 0
    GRID-M2      separating cells  18   truncating right on 18, ceiling right on 0
  GRID-W's `n` axis is built from EXACT multiples of the alignment,
  so it cannot see the rounding at all.  w-memcpy's already-paid-for
  cells are what settle it — they do not merely agree with the
  reading, they decide a part of it the whitebox lane's own grid
  could not.

==============================================================================
WHAT THE HOLD-OUT DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH
==============================================================================
  Both constants are recoverable from obj cells with no disassembler:
  the QUANTITY is picked out by a four-way search and the THRESHOLD
  by an exhaustive one, on either population, and each transfers to
  the other.  What the disassembly supplied was the SEARCH SPACE —
  `size / align, truncating` is not a quantity anybody enumerated
  before reading it (w-memcpy froze six rivals and none of them is a
  quotient).  That is navigation, and it is worth a `route:` row.
