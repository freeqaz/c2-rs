## 1. The result, in the order it should be read

> ### #822 states that **3,950 of 4,674** embedded-division sites *"need a magic-number multiply"*. At the workload's own optimization mode they need **none**. `mulhw`/`mulhwu` occurs in **0 of 478** `/O1` cells. The lowering is `li`/`lis`+`ori` and a real **`divw`**.

> ### The magic multiply is real, but it lives at `/Ox` — and it is a cliff on the **OPERATOR**, not on `k`. At `/Ox`, `%` takes it in **89 of 118** cells and `/` takes it in **0 of 118**. `a / 7` is `li r11,7 ; divw` at *every* mode graded here.

> ### The map is stated as a GENERATOR and graded by **byte-identity**: `rule.py` computes the 32-bit words from `k` alone and reproduces real `c2.dll` on **478 of 478** cells — including **78/78 held out**, **80/80 on the workload's own divisors**, and **204/204 at the workload's own exact flags**.

## 2. The map, at `/O1` and at the workload's own flags

Write `s32(k)` for `k` read as a signed 32-bit value, `fits16(k)` for
`-32768 ≤ s32(k) ≤ 32767`, `hi(k)` for the top halfword **read as signed**, and
`lo(k)` for `k & 0xFFFF`.

**MATERIALIZE(rD, k)** — `li rD, k` if `fits16(k)`; otherwise `lis rD, hi(k)`
followed by `ori rD, rD, lo(k)` **iff `lo(k) ≠ 0`**.

**QUOTIENT(k)**, from the dividend in `r3`:

| # | regime | signed | unsigned |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `k = 0` | `twi 7,r0,0` — **no division at all** | same |
| 2 | `k = 1` | nothing (`/` is the identity) | same |
| 3 | `k = -1` | `neg r3,r3` | — |
| 4 | `k = 2ⁿ` | `srawi r11,r3,n ; addze rQ,r11` | `rlwinm r3,r3,32-n,n,31` |
| 5 | `k = -2ⁿ` | `srawi r11,r3,n ; addze r11,r11 ; neg rQ,r11` | — |
| 6 | non-pow2, `fits16` | `li r11,k ; divw rQ,r3,r11` | `divwu` |
| 7 | non-pow2, wide, `lo ≠ 0` | `lis r11,hi ; ori r11,r11,lo ; divw` | `divwu` |
| 8 | non-pow2, wide, `lo = 0` | `lis r11,hi ; divw` | `divwu` |

For `/` the quotient goes to `r3`. For `%` it goes to `r11`, then
**MULBACK**, then `subf r3,r11,r3`:

* `k = ±1` — the whole body is `li r3,0`; no quotient is computed.
* `k = 2ⁿ` and `fits16(k)` — `rlwinm r11,r11,n,0,31-n`.
* `fits16(k)` otherwise — `mulli r11,r11,k`.
* wide — MATERIALIZE into **`r10`** and `mullw r11,r11,r10`. `r11` is taken, so
  the quotient moves: `divw r10,r3,r11 ; mullw r11,r10,r11`.
* unsigned `k = 2ⁿ` — the whole body is `rlwinm r3,r3,0,32-n,31`, a **mask**.
  There is no quotient, no `addze` and **no `simm16` cliff** (R9).

### 2.1 The two cliffs, and one of them is `/O1`-only

**The `simm16` cliff is real and it is on the multiply-back, not on the
division.** `a % 32768` at `/O1` materializes the constant and uses `mullw`;
`a % 16384` uses `rlwinm`. At `/Ox` the same `a % 32768` uses `rlwinm` — so this
cliff **does not exist at `/Ox`** and a lane that measured it there would not see
it. R6's predicate is exact over **192** multiply-back cells with **0**
violations in either direction.

**On powers of two, `fits16(k)` and `n ≤ 14` are extensionally equal and no cell
can separate them** — `2ⁿ` fits `simm16` exactly when `n ≤ 14`. The rule is
stated as `fits16` because that is the same predicate the `mulli`/`mullw` split
obeys on the non-power-of-two side, but **the two readings are indistinguishable
on this axis** and that is said here rather than hidden behind the choice.

### 3.1 Regime 8 was predicted before it was witnessed

The FIT set contains **no cell at all** in the `wide-nolo` regime — a
non-power-of-two `k` outside `simm16` whose low half is zero. Every wide `k` in
it (100000, ±32769, 2147483647) has `lo ≠ 0`, and 65536 is a power of two and
takes regime 4. So the rule *predicted* an eighth regime that no captured byte
had ever shown, `K_HUNT` was minted to hunt it — 196608, −196608, 3145728,
458752 — and **all 14 of its cells came back byte-identical to the prediction**.
`s-div-196608` reads `lis r11,3 ; divw r3,r3,r11`, three words, no `ori`.

R3 registered **7** with an accepted band of [6, 9] and the answer is **8**.

## 4. #644, aimed at this lane and hitting it three separate ways

The brief said it plainly: *"a magic constant is exactly a `lis`/`ori` pair, so
this trap is aimed at you."* R5 registered a single split and expected to lose,
because `w-divmod` had published `addis ; ori ; divw ; mullw ; subf` as
contiguous. It **hit three times**, and each is a different mechanism:

**(a) Split by the quotient chain, in a straight-line leaf** — `a % 32768` at
`/O1`:

```text
  srawi r11,r3,15 ; lis r10,0 ; addze r11,r11 ; ori r10,r10,0x8000 ; mullw … ; subf …
                    ^^^^^^^^^^                  ^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^
```

The `lis` lands after the chain's **first** instruction and the `ori` after its
**second**. Note also that the `lis` immediate is **0** — a naive *"emit `lis`
only when the high half is non-zero"* reader emits five words where `c2` emits
six.

**(b) Split by the loop's own induction update** — `(n-1)/100000` in a loop:

```text
  lis r11,1 ; addi r,-1 ; ori r11,r11,0x86a0 ; divw …
              ^^^^^^^^^ the loop's `n - 1`, between the two halves of the constant
```

This is the first time on this project that #644 has been witnessed **inside a
loop body**, which is where 99.5 % of the target population lives.

**(c) Two interleaved materializations at `/Ox`** — `a % 100000` reads

```text
  lis lis ori ori mulhw srawi rlwinm add mullw subf
```

Two constants — the magic multiplier and the divisor — built **in parallel**.
"The `ori` after the `lis`" is not a rule that survives this at all, which is why
`magicgrade.py` recovers the multiplier by **constant-propagation over the
register file** and never by an offset.

A fourth, weaker instance: the `srawi`/`addze` pair is itself split by the loop's
`addi(-3)` in three of the loop cells. **A producer is not one contiguous
instruction, and on this axis it is not even one contiguous *pair*.**

### 4.1 The `ori` placement rests on exactly ONE `k` in the whole 32-bit axis

Enumerated, not assumed (§8 of `work/w-magic/`'s own check): a signed power of
two needs an interleaved `ori` iff it is outside `simm16` **and** its low half is
non-zero. `2ⁿ` has `lo = 0` for `n ≥ 16` and fits `simm16` for `n ≤ 14`.

**`k = 32768` is the only value in the entire 32-bit signed axis that produces
that shape.** This project has five recorded single-cell traps; this clause is a
one-cell rule and is flagged as one. Anyone who ships it owes a wider witness —
`(a+b) % 32768` and other longer quotient chains are the obvious place to look,
and this lane did not build them.

## 5. The population's own shape: does the map survive a loop?

`w-divsplit` §8 measured the population at **4,649 of 4,674 `cflow-loop`**,
**74.3 % `calls-1`**, **99.8 %** carrying a pointer-difference dividend. A map
established in a straight-line leaf and *asserted* inside a loop would be this
project's twelfth refuted placement rule — `w-hash` §5.1 found the `twi 6`
placement differs exactly that way.

So the same `k` was put inside the population's own shape:
`work/w-magic/loopgrid.py`, three shapes (`ptrdiff`, `heap`, `call`) × 13 `k`.

```text
  /O1   cells 39 · CONTIGUOUS 33 · SCATTERED 6 · ABSENT 0 · capture-fail 0
  /Ox   cells 39 · CONTIGUOUS 18 · SCATTERED 21 · ABSENT 0 · capture-fail 0
```

**ABSENT is 0 at both modes.** The map's instruction sequence, with its immediate
fields, appears in every loop cell. Registers are not compared and that weaker
claim is stated in the script's own docstring so a green row is not read as a
byte claim.

**But two of the six scattered cells say something a lane must not miss.**

* **`ptrdiff` at `k = 3` is one word SHORTER than at every other `k`** — 15
  against 16. The loop's pointer stride is 3, so `c2` **common-subexpression-
  eliminated the divisor's `li r,3` with the loop's own stride constant**. A
  constant divisor's materialization is *not local to the division*: if any other
  constant in the body happens to equal `k`, one instruction disappears. An emit
  that always materializes `k` would be one word too long on exactly those
  bodies.
* **The `ptrdiff` loop emits a second `divwu` that has nothing to do with the
  user's division** — it is the **trip count**, `(q-p+2)/3`, feeding `mtctr`.
  So the loop lowering *itself* needs the constant-divisor map, independently of
  whether the body contains a division at all.

## 6. What this lane did NOT ship, and the count that decides it

**No emit. Nothing in `crates/`.** The count that decides it:

* The straight-line constant-divisor population is **4 of 4,674**
  (`w-divsplit` §8), and none of the four is a bare one-formal
  constant-divisor leaf — they are divisions embedded in larger bodies.
* **Conversions a constant-divisor leaf would buy: 0.** Stated plainly, as the
  brief requires, and pre-registered as R8 in that direction.
* Reaching *any* of them needs a `0x05`/`0x06` arm in `parse_expr_classed`,
  which `w-divsplit` deliberately did not add. **That widening is the exact
  mechanism of board #232** — *"the `26`-separator widening turned a clean
  refusal into a wrong emit"*, live on master for 255 commits. Buying **zero**
  conversions with that risk, against a hard `fnbyte-differs 0` bar, is a trade
  this lane declines and says so rather than shipping a second transcription.

The port refuses opcode `0x05` at parse time today, so **there is no live defect
here** — the map is a specification for the rung that will need it, not a repair.

## 8. What missed, and what is left open

**The one MISS is `rule.py`'s own, and the held-out design is what caught it.**
`fits16` was written as `-32768 ≤ k ≤ 32767` over the *arithmetic* value. That is
wrong for exactly one shape: `a / 4294967295u` emits `li r11,-1 ; divwu`, one
instruction, because **`li` sign-extends** — the predicate is over the 32-bit
two's-complement **pattern**. Two cells of 22 differed and **both were that one
`k`**. On the signed axis `s32` is the identity, which is why **156 fitted and
held-out cells could not see it**: the whole signed half of the grid is blind to
this error by construction.

The correction was then validated on `K_HUNT2`, minted afterwards and frozen
before compiling, which straddles the cliff deliberately: `0xFFFF8000` (`s32
-32768`, the last `li`) and `0xFFFF7FFF` (`s32 -32769`, the first `lis`+`ori`).
**16 of 16.** A correction validated only on the cell that forced it is not
validated.

**R4's generator was ALSO wrong the first time, and in the more dangerous
direction.** The Hacker's Delight `magicu` transcription derived **3 of 10**
unsigned cells, which reads as *"`c2` does not use the standard unsigned
magic"* — a false negative about the compiler, caused by a bug in the
instrument. Two errors: Python's unbounded integers do not wrap where C's
`unsigned` does, and the transcription's exit condition was wrong. Replacing it
with the **Granlund–Montgomery theorem stated directly** — smallest `p ≥ 32` with
`m = ceil(2^p/d)` and `m·d − 2^p ≤ 2^(p−32)`, taking the 33-bit `add` variant
when `m ≥ 2^32` — gives **89 of 89**. The lesson is the one this project keeps
paying for: *a negative result from an instrument you have not calibrated is a
statement about the instrument.*

Left open, deliberately:

* **`/Ox`'s `%` sequence is graded on its multiplier, not on its whole body.**
  R4 asked whether `M` and `s` are derivable and they are, 89/89. The
  surrounding fixup — `subf`/`rlwinm`/`add` in six distinct arrangements — is
  **not** stated as a rule here. A `/Ox` lane owes that, and `#763` says the
  modes differ in the body once there is more than one block.
* **Why `/` keeps a hardware divide at `/Ox` while `%` does not** is measured,
  not explained. 0 of 118 against 89 of 118 is a very sharp cliff and it has no
  mechanism attached to it.
* **The `ori` interleave clause has one witness in the whole axis** (§4.1).
* **The CSE finding is one cell wide too.** `ptrdiff` at `k = 3` is the only
  place a divisor collided with another constant in the body; nothing here says
  how general that is.
* **64-bit divisors are not on this axis at all.** `long long` was in
  `w-hash`'s grid and not in this one.
