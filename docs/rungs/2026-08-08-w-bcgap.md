[the prereg](_2026-08-08-w-bcgap-prereg.md) §0 says so **before** any probe. What
was actually missing is the other half, and its failure mode is why it is 500

> **The join key is a source-path string.** `w-emitp`'s pipeline spells
> `src/App.cpp` as `src__App.cpp` — that is literally how `work/w-emit/truth`
> names its per-TU files. Join those two spellings and you get **0 matches**, so
> every intersection is 0, and `|model ∩ B∧C| = 0` publishes as *"this model
> buys no reach"* when what happened is that the key was wrong.

This project has shipped that shape at least four times this week under other
names: a column that was zero by construction; 130,575 reader refusals published
under a codegen label; `noform-` keys where `None` was overloaded; a comment
asserting unreachability with 4,973 witnesses. So the join is a **reported,
refusable step**, and the refusal is graded (§4.3).

## 3. What shipped

`crates/c2-harness/src/gap/sets.rs` — **16 named sets, defined once**, over one
row type, reached two ways:

* **live**, from a scan — `GapReport::factor_rows()`;
* **offline**, from a `--factors-tsv` file — `sets::parse_factors_tsv()`.

`the_tsv_view_and_the_live_report_are_the_same_rows` round-trips the writer's own
output back through the parser and compares row for row, so the offline tool is a
**view** of the scan's measurement and not a second implementation of the
factorization that can drift from it. Without that property, an intersection
taken offline is an intersection with a *lookalike* of `B∧C`.

`crates/c2-harness/src/cli/factors.rs` — `c2rs factors`:

```
c2rs factors --tsv PATH [--check-metrics GAPLOG] [--list SETNAME] [--set NAME=PATH]...
```

**Gating decision, registered in the prereg and honoured**: the *listing* stays
exactly where #352 put it — opt-in, a file — because `gap` is also the engine
under `mode_lane.sh` / `mode_cross.sh`, where one row per graded TU is tens of
thousands of lines per lane. The *algebra over it* is *offline*: no toolchain, no
capture, no scan, milliseconds over a text file. So the answer to "every scan or
behind a flag" is neither — it is **zero marginal cost on every scan, because it
does not run on one**, and `gap`'s stdout gains nothing at all.

### 3.1 The sets, and why the derived ones are named

`factor-a` … `factor-e`, `d-or-e`, `b-and-c`, `a-and-b-and-c`,
`a-and-b-and-c-and-d`, `a-and-b-and-c-and-d-or-e`, `match`, `frontier`,
`frontier-if-a`, `projection-divergence`, and two that had never been named:

* **`reach-pool` = `B∧C ∖ A∧B∧C`** — *the* set board #213 prices at **+124**. A
  *partial* emit model buys `|model ∩ reach-pool|`, of which #213's +124 is the
  `model = everything` case. This is the generalization the project needed and
  did not have a name for.
* **`frontier-pool` = `frontier-if-A ∖ FRONTIER`** — #213's *other* arithmetic,
  **+122**. The two are different sets and their difference **is** the projection
  divergence, which is now an assertion in a unit test rather than a note.

### 4.4 A must-fail mutation SURVIVED, and that is the honest part of this lane

Four mutations, run against the suite:

| | mutation | first suite |
|---|---|---|
| **M1** | `frontier` forgets to exclude `match` TUs | **SURVIVED** |
| **M2** | `b-and-c` becomes `f[1] \|\| f[2]` | red |
| **M3** | the parser stops cross-checking the `letters` column | red |
| **M4** | the join silently applies `__` → `/` | red |

M1 is the single easiest way to make this module disagree with the scan, and it
survived because **the clause is inert**. The model's joint equals the match set
(`a-and-b-and-c-and-d-or-e` 11 = `match` 11), so every match TU is already
excluded by `!accepted()`. Measured on the workload: `A∧B∧C ∧ ¬(D∨E)` *ignoring*
the match clause is **16, of which 0 are `match`** — `FRONTIER` prints 16 either
way, and all 11 match TUs satisfy `D∨E`.

**A clause with zero witnesses being read as a guard is the same shape as a
column that is zero by construction.** It is kept — it is guarded by a
*different* control (`factor_control_on_match_tus` requires `D∨E` to have 0
violations over match TUs) and it is what keeps a byte-exact TU off the list of
TUs that need codegen work if that control ever goes red. So the state is now
**constructed in a fixture** rather than waited for, and M1 fails.

`|cand ∩ S|` over the 871 graded TUs, for every model w-emitp scored. Every join

| `JFP` | 132 | 124 | 129 | **122** | 24 | **98** | 97 | 15 | 9 |

Five things fall out of that table, and four of them could not be seen from a
per-TU exact rate:

1. **The alias channel is worth 0 TUs of reach.** `ORACLE ⊆ ALIAS_IN` (checked:
   true, +321) and `(ALIAS_IN ∖ ORACLE) ∩ B∧C = ∅` — verified directly, not by
   subtracting totals. `JFP → JFP_ALIAS` is +176 and also `∩ B∧C = ∅`. **A 3.1×
   move in the headline metric bought nothing the reach metric can see**, because
   every TU it added fails `B` or `C`.
2. **Proportional reasoning is wrong in both directions here.** For `ALIAS_IN`
   the naive `|B∧C| × 0.555 = 84` **under**-states the truth (134) by 50: on the
   145 `B∧C` TUs it can see, the model is exact on **134 = 92.4 %**, against
   55.5 % overall — a strong positive correlation, in the direction the prereg
   registered. For the *increment* it **over**-states: `321/850 × 124 ≈ 47` TUs
   of reach against a true **0**. w-emitp was right to refuse.
3. **`C` is the binding constraint, not `B`.** `ORACLE ∩ B` is 136 and
   `ALIAS_IN ∩ B` is 307 — the channel more than doubled the `B` intersection —
   and `∩ C` is **148 for both**. `B∧C` cannot move while `C` does not.
4. **The frontier is fully covered already.** All four models are exact on
   **15 of 16** `FRONTIER` TUs, and the 16th is outside w-emitp's corpus. So the
   emit predicate is *not* what stands between the port and the frontier;
   codegen is, which is what `FRONTIER` has always said.
5. **The `+124` is real but the gap to it is `C`-shaped.** A perfect predicate is
   worth 124; the best measured predicate is worth **110**, i.e. **89 % of a
   perfect one already**. The remaining 14 is not a modelling gap worth another
   channel hunt.
