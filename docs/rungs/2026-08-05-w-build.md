## 0. One line

I was sent to rank the frontier's construct inventories by **coverage** — which
operators appear across the most TUs — build the head, and measure. The ranking
is **refuted**, by the sharpest test available: rebuilt on the tree before the
one conversion this project has, it puts the operator `w-tu1` actually built
**30th of 31**. Depth put the same TU 11th–14th of 19; byte fraction put it 1st.

Refuting it took one arithmetic pass over another lane's committed file. What
took the rest of the lane is the instrument built to price the ranking's
entries — a **one-away screen** — which prices any operator's fall-through-ness
in a single scan, reproduces a prior lane's expensive decline, and inverts mass
completely. And what the *build* found, while probing its own allocation
neighbourhood, is a **`Port=Mismatch` on master `4d6aa58` in the accepted
straight-line class**.

### 3.2 Why it cannot work, from the instrument's own definition — board **#683**

`chain.py` sinks a blocker key and re-scans. **A chain step exists only because

fall-through key. So every operator in every inventory is, on every TU it covers,

an operator whose construction converts a frontier TU.** The two terminals it
can offer are both `op:23` — cmp-ge, in the relational family `w-cmp` measured at

## 5. What shipped — W70, the bitwise and shift six

Six IL opcodes with no production in the accepting parser at all: `09 <<`,
`0A >>`, `0B &`, `0C |`, `0D ^`. Register-register only, in a serial chain.
Three facts had to be right and each is a capture, not an inference.

### 5.1 The destination is the RA field — board **#685**

Every one of these is an X-form whose destination is **RA (bits 11–15)**, where
`add`/`mullw`/`subf` use **RT (bits 6–10)**. Encoding `and` through
`encode_add`'s layout produces a valid `and` with the destination and the left
operand **exchanged** — bytes that assemble, disassemble and fuzz-match, and
compute the wrong thing whenever the two registers differ. All 16 captured words
reproduce from `encode_logical_x`, and
`the_logical_destination_field_is_ra_and_not_rt` spells out what the wrong
layout would have emitted.

### 5.2 `>>` is TWO instructions from ONE IL byte

`sraw` (`7c632630`) over a signed left operand, `srw` (`7c632430`) over an
unsigned one, from the identical byte `0A`. The distinction is one nibble of the
operand TYPE, and `ValueClass::Int4` **collapses `int` and `unsigned` on
purpose** because every other modeled operator is identical across them.

Probed both ways round: **only the LEFT operand decides** —
`int f(int a, unsigned b)` is `sraw`, `unsigned f(unsigned a, int b)` is `srw`.

`parse_expr` therefore reads the signedness separately for this one operator and
refuses a mixed expression outright. **The `2C` arm feeds the same two flags, and
that line is what makes it safe rather than merely careful**: `is_int4_type`
admits both spellings, so `(unsigned)a >> b` is a conversion the parser
*already* accepts as the no-op it is — while it moves the instruction. Without
it that body would have parsed with every operand looking signed and emitted
`sraw`. It is `w70_bitwise_neg.cpp`'s `n_shr_cast`.

Measured cost: **819 workload functions** land on `expr-shr-mixed-sign`, plus 1
on `expr-shr-sign-late`.

### 5.3 The immediate axis is refused, and here is what it costs

| cell | c2 emits |
|---|---|
| `a & 1` | `clrlwi r3,r3,31` — a contiguous mask, so `rlwinm` |
| `a & 0xFF00` | `rlwinm r3,r3,0,16,23` |
| `a & -2` | `clrrwi r3,r3,1` |
| `a & 5` | `andi. r3,r3,5` — **record-form; it writes CR0. There is no plain `andi`** |
| `a & 0x12345` | `lis r12,1 ; ori r12,r12,0x2345 ; and r3,r3,r12` — **three instructions, through r12, not the r11 every other shape uses** |
| `a \| 0x12345` | `oris r3,r3,1 ; ori r3,r3,0x2345` |
| `256 >> a` | `li r11,256 ; sraw r3,r11,r3` |

Three instruction families and **two different scratch registers** across one
axis, selected by a predicate over the immediate's **value** — is the mask
contiguous, does it fit sixteen bits. Not a cell this rung gridded, so not a
cell it emits. (`w42_shift_mask.cpp` and `w43_cmp_shift_or.cpp` are the two
immediate cells this tree *does* model; both are folds recognized ahead of the
general expression path.)

### 5.4 Depth, and the guard that came out of it

`il_accum4.cpp` records that c2 decides accumulator-versus-descending **once for
the whole chain**, and that the candidate rules coincide at one intermediate —
which every three-leaf chain has. So the four- and five-leaf rows in
`w70_bitwise.cpp` are not more of the same shape; they are the first rows that
can separate anything. Measured at `/Ox`:

```text
  a & b & c & d       and r11,r3,r4 ; and r10,r11,r5 ; and r3,r10,r6
  a & b & c & d & e   and r11 ; and r10 ; and r9 ; and r3
```

A pure bitwise chain contains no addition, takes `select_text`'s existing
descending path unchanged, and is right at `/O1` and `/Ox` at four and five
leaves. **Mixing with `+`/`-`/`*` refuses** — §7.

### 6.1 Board **#687** — my own screen OVER-counts, and the reason is in the code

`op:0A`'s one-away read **1,433** and the build converted **0**. That is not
noise, and the screen's error has a mechanism: `expr-chain-sink-poison` is
raised **before** the pointer-arithmetic and one-byte-unsigned guards at the end
of `parse_expr_classed`. So a body those guards would still refuse reports
*poison*, and the screen counts it as one-away.

new signedness guards, which did not exist when the screen ran.

**So the one-away number is an upper bound with a named leak, and the fix is one
line** — move the poison below the guards, or count the guards separately.
Whoever uses this screen next should read every figure in §4 as a ceiling. It
does not change any verdict there: a ceiling of 1 is still 1.

## 7. Board **#688** — a LIVE WRONG-BYTES EMIT on unmodified master, and two instruments that could not see it

Not on this lane's brief. Found by probing the allocation neighbourhood of the
operators it was shipping, and reproduced on a detached worktree at `4d6aa58`
with nothing of this lane's in the tree:

```text
  int f(int a,int b,int c,int d) { return ((a + b) * c) * d; }

  c2   /Ox   add r11,r3,r4 ; mullw r10,r11,r5 ; mullw r3,r10,r6
  port /Ox   add r11,r3,r4 ; mullw r11,r11,r5 ; mullw r3,r11,r6
                                   ^^^   Port=Mismatch @ offset 541
```

`select_text`'s rule is `chain_has_add`: at `/Ox`, a chain containing any
addition puts every intermediate in r11, otherwise they descend r11, r10, r9.
That rule came from an enumeration of **11,664 four-leaf chains** and is right
about every one of them. This chain contains an addition and descends anyway.
`(((a + b) * c) * d) * e` is the same defect one leaf deeper.

### 7.1 Why nothing caught it — two blind spots, of different kinds

**`scripts/sweep.d/10-int-chains.py` enumerates THREE leaves.** `l1 o1 l2 o2 l3`
has exactly one intermediate, and with one intermediate every candidate rule
puts it in r11. The four-leaf enumeration `il_accum4.cpp` records was a
**one-off**; nothing carried the axis into the standing sweep. So the sweep has
reported `0 mismatch` over a shape it cannot represent — *"a grid that cannot
contain its own counterexample"*, and the generator's own axes are where it
happened.

**And `scripts/gate.sh` does not run the sweep at all**, so a fragment alone
would have left the regression outside the merge gate. Both are closed:
`scripts/sweep.d/12-alloc-depth.py` (72 cases) and the `w71` fixture pair.

> **My own fragment could not contain its counterexample either, and this is the
> correction rather than the omission.** Its first revision emitted
> `a o1 b o2 c o3 d` **unbracketed** and reported `mismatches=0` against the very
> master binary it was written to catch — C++ precedence reassociates
> `a + b * c * d` into `a + ((b*c)*d)`, a depth-3 tree the parser refuses
> outright. Caught by requiring the fragment to REPRODUCE the known failure
> before committing it, which is the only check that distinguishes a green
> instrument from an absent one. Parenthesized, it reads **1 mismatch against
> master and 0 here**.

### 7.2 The hypothesis, and why it is NOT shipped

All **23** measured cells — `alloc-Ox.cod` (9), `bits3-Ox.cod` (10) and
`il_accum4.cpp`'s records (4) — fit one rule:

> **An intermediate goes to r11 when its CONSUMER is an `add`, and takes the
> next descending scratch otherwise.**

It reproduces both `chain_has_add` misses *and* every case `chain_has_add` gets
right, including `(a & b) - c - d + e`, whose allocation is **r11, r10, r11** —
which no whole-chain rule can produce at all.

**23 witnesses is not 11,664.** Replacing a rule that survived an enumeration
with one that has not faced it is precisely how the per-chain accumulator bug got
in the first time — two rules that coincide on short inputs. So the divergent
region **refuses** under `expr-alloc-undetermined`, the hypothesis is written
down at `intermediate_alloc_determined`, and the fragment that would validate it
ships alongside. The next lane has a failing test to fix against.

The predicate is derived from the mechanism, not fitted: the two rules classify
intermediate *k* by different things, so they can only disagree where an add's
result feeds a **non**-add, and only with three or more operators (with one
intermediate the descending sequence *starts* at r11). **Exactly 1 of the
fragment's 72 cases is in that region**, and **0 workload functions** — the
`expr-alloc-undetermined` key does not appear in the final scan at all. The
guard costs nothing measurable; `w71_alloc_undetermined.cpp` re-grades 13
neighbours `Port=Match`.

## 9. Two claims of my own, corrected on the page

**`expr-ptr-bitwise` and `expr-int1u-bitwise` are UNWITNESSED.**
`w70_bitwise_neg.cpp`'s two rows were written to exercise them and do not:
`c2rs census` reports both as **`expr-convert-target-8641`**. C++ has no `&`
over pointers, so writing one needs a cast and the cast refuses first; and
`bool & bool` promotes both operands to `int`, which the IL spells as a `2C`
that refuses first too. The two guards are fail-closed defence at a position the
source language may not be able to reach, and they ship as that rather than as
measured rows.

**`intermediate_alloc_determined`'s doc listed `(a+1)*b` as accepted.** It is
`NotImplemented` — but on master too, under `expr-affine-pending-imm`, because
`(a+1)` leaves a pending immediate `Mul` has no lowering for. The predicate does
return `true` for it; the refusal is an older gate, and claiming it would have
credited this guard with a decision it does not make. Corrected in the source.
