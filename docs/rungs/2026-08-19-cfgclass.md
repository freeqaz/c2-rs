## 5. The promotion, priced TWO-SIDED — and DECLINED

The registry makes speakable what five lanes said they could not write down
(`w-rotate` §7, `w-sched2` §8, `w-subclass`, `w-blockir` §10, `w-cflowlabel`;
board **#761**): the port emits `cflow-if-n`, `cflow-if-2`, `cflow-loop` and
`cflow-multi-exit` bodies byte-exactly. Promoting any of them into
`PORT_CFG_CLASSES` — `Whole` or `CfgSub::Keys`-restricted — is a widening of a
**published claim** and is not taken here. The price, both sides:

### 5.1 Side A — what the refusal costs

`cfg_reach_bounds`' `⊤` on the live workload is **2 of 2** frontier TUs. So the
entire reach at stake — the whole gap between today's screen and the most
permissive claim anyone could make — is **2 TUs of *reachability*, and 0 TU
conversions**, because `cfg-reach-*` is *"an INSTRUMENT, never a gate"* and
licenses no emit.

And it is **2, not 1 + 1**: both frontier TUs need `cflow-if-n` **and**
`cflow-loop`. Promoting either class alone moves `cfg-reach-shipped` by
**zero**.

### 5.2 Side B — what shipping it costs, priced against the ORACLE on the population it applies to

Board #3270's rule. The population a promotion applies to is *the blocked
functions of the frontier TUs*, because `cfg_reach_with` reads only the crossed
(`class|key`) rows, which name blocked functions.

That population is **22 functions**, and `gap-metric frontier-codegen-reader` is
**22** — every one of them dies in the IL parser. `frontier-codegen-wrong 0`,
`frontier-codegen-refused 0`, `frontier-codegen-measured 0`.

> **So a promotion is wrong about 22 of the 22 functions it would newly claim,
> at a `match` and `fnbyte-exact` delta of exactly 0.** The screen would read
> `REACHABLE` — *"every blocked fn is a port CFG class"* — for two TUs whose
> every blocked function the port cannot **parse**, let alone lower.

This is `w-sizebracket`'s finding in this lane's units, at its maximum: 100 %
wrong in the unsound direction, and free in every metric anyone would have used
to choose it.

### 5.3 The restricted variant is **either inert or wrong**, with nothing in between

The `CfgSub::Keys` mechanism exists precisely so a partial claim can be
narrower. It does not help here, and the reason is structural rather than
numeric:

* a `Keys(K)` entry moves `cfg-reach-shipped` **only if** `K` appears in a
  frontier TU's crossed row — i.e. only if some **blocked** function carries it;
* every blocked function on this frontier is a **reader** refusal (§5.2);
* therefore any `K` that moves the number claims a body the port refuses, and
  any `K` that does not is **inert** — `cfg-reach-shipped` 0 → 0.

There is no key set for which the entry is both non-trivial and true. **P6 is a
HIT for a stronger reason than it was registered with**: not "the key sets
happen to be disjoint" but "they are disjoint by construction on this
frontier".

### 5.4 What the registry delivers instead, without promoting anything

`SHIPPED_CFG_CLAIMS` records, in `c2-core`, machine-checked and
census-graded, what the screen has never been able to say:

| class | claim | lowerings |

| `cflow-if-1` | **Whole** — reaches the screen | **4**: `call_seq`, `cond_pair`, `fp_store_diamond`, `pool_free_list` |
| `cflow-if-2` | *Partial* | **1**: `call_seq` |
| `cflow-if-n` | *Partial* | **8**: `call_seq`, `guard_chain_shared_tail`, `alloc_init_or_fail`, `osf_handle_guard`, `guard_ret_chain`, `close_call_chain`, `xlrc_create_guard`, `if_call_join` |
