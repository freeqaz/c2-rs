### 2.1 The selector is silent on 14 of 17, and the silence is structural

**Board #826.** `byte_fraction` is accepted `.text` bytes over total `.text`
bytes. It is therefore **exactly 0 for every TU where the port accepts
nothing** — and that is **14 of the 17** frontier TUs, printed by the scan's own
header. Eight of those fourteen have `emit-emitted == 1`, where the ratio is
`0` or `1` **by construction** and can never be anything else short of the
conversion itself.

So the ranking is a **partial** order, not a total one. Over the fourteen it is
alphabetical. **Both TUs the CFG screen calls REACHABLE — `Main.cpp` and
`xboxheap.cpp` — sit inside that blind block**, at ranks 4 and 15 out of a tie.

This is registered as **R2** in the prereg and it is a **HIT, stronger than
registered**: the claim was written about the 8 single-function TUs and the true
statement covers 14.

**This is not a rival ranking and must not be read as one.** Eleven selectors
have been refuted on this project and every one failed by proposing a different
*order*. This proposes none. It is a statement about where the sanctioned
selector has **no** order, which is checkable from any scan and which the
instrument's own printed header already contains — nobody had read it as a
limit.

### 3.1 The IL side — a chain WALKED, not guessed

`C2RS_SINK_CHAIN` (boards #660/#661) decodes one opcode at a time and
**poisons**: it pushes no `IlOp`, and a walk that used one refuses under

`work/w-next/chainwalk.sh`:

```text
step 0  spec=[<none>]                     -> expr-op-0x27
step 1  spec=[op:27]                      -> expr-op-0x32
step 2  spec=[op:27,op:32]                -> expr-op-0x4B
step 3  spec=[op:27,op:32,op:4B]          -> expr-op-0x4F
step 4  spec=[op:27,op:32,op:4B,op:4F]    -> expr-call-in-expr-data-addr-then-off-add-and-chain-bind-more
```

**Four opcode steps and then a structural refusal, against a census that reports
one key.** This reproduces board **#622**'s `0x27 → 0x32` and extends it by three
further steps — the first frontier TU whose chain has been published as a
**depth** rather than as a head. It is the direct demonstration of the standing
warning that closing a blocker key is set **substitution**, not removal.

The terminal is **composite**: `data-addr` receiver form, blockers `off-add`
**and** `chain-bind`, suffix `-more` — which `mcall.rs`'s own key grammar defines
as *"MEASURED: both together are still not enough"*. A name that looks like one
fact is three facts and an admission that there are more.

## 4. The grid — 34 cells, an anchor that reproduces the TU, and three refutations

`work/w-next/ctorgrid.py`, graded against real `c2.dll` at the workload's own
flags. **34 of 34 graded, 0 failed.**

> ### The anchor control is the finding that made the rest possible: a **twelve-line synthetic** reproduces `xboxheap.cpp`'s twenty words **byte for byte**. The class is reachable by a grid, which is why everything below is measured rather than argued.

**Checked on the raw bytes, not on the listing** (`work/w-next/anchorcmp.py`):
both objs carry exactly one non-empty `.text` COMDAT, both are **80 bytes**, and
the two are **identical**. A disassembly listing can agree while bytes differ, so
the claim is not made from the printed form.

**And the claim is scoped exactly as `FUNCTION_BYTE_MATCH.md` trap 6 requires.**
A `.text` COMDAT's raw data does **not** contain its relocations, and these two
bodies necessarily relocate to *different* symbols —
`?AllocatePageBlock@CXboxHeap@NUISPEECH@@AAAPAU_BLOCK_ENTRY@12@I@Z` against the
synthetic's own. So the statement is *"identical `.text` bytes"* and **not**
*"identical function"*. For an anchor control that is the right scope — it is
the schedule and the immediate fields being pinned — but it is stated rather
than left for a reader to assume.

### 4.1 The schedule is CONSTANT under the immediates, and re-plans under every structural axis

**Board #830.** Identical 20-word plan, only immediate and register fields
moving, across **ten** cells: `anchor`, `base` (a base class shifting every
offset by 8), `interior16`, `formal-a`, and `lit{1,7,255,32767,65536,−1}`.

Every structural axis re-plans the whole block: `interior0` (the `addi`
vanishes — 19 words), `order-monotone`, `order-interior-first`, `spell-direct`,
`n2`/`n4`/`n8`, `nocall` (no frame at all — 9 words), `call2`, `callarg-*`,
`callfree`, `formal3`, `virtual`, `field-char`/`field-short`/`field-ll`.

That is the `w-tu1`/`w-hash` recipe exactly — **draw the class narrowly enough
that the schedule is a constant, then grade the axes that remain free** — and it
is why this lane did **not** fit a placement rule.

### 4.2 Board #644 is LIVE in this class

**Board #831.** `#644` warns that a producer is not one contiguous instruction.
Measured here:

```text
  lit65536        lis stw addi stw stw mr stw stw stw          <- ONE-word producer, plan HELD
  wide65537       lis stw addi stw ORI stw mr stw stw stw      <- TWO-word producer, SPLIT
```

The `lis` lands at slot 0 and its `ori` at slot 4, with a `stw`, an `addi` and a
`stw` **between them**. Reproduced at `65537`, `123456`, `0x12345` and
`2147483647`. The class boundary is therefore *"a literal representable in ONE
instruction"* — `li` for `simm16`, `lis` for a clean high half — and not
*"a literal"*.

### 4.3 I refuted my own reading, and the grid is what caught it

**Board #832.** The prereg's §0.4 states, off the one cell anyone had looked at,
that **"the six stores are in exact SOURCE order"**. It is **false**:

```text
  anchor            source 16,0,20,4,8,12   ->  emitted 16,0,20,4,8,12   source order
  order-monotone    source 0,4,8,12,16,20   ->  emitted 0,4,8,12,16,20   source order
  order-lit-first   source 20,16,0,4,8,12   ->  emitted 16,0,20,4,8,12   NOT source order
  spell-direct      source 16,0,20,4,8,12   ->  emitted 16,0,4,20,8,12   NOT source order
  field-char        source 16,0,17,4,8,12   ->  emitted 0,4,16,17,8,12   NOT source order
```

Right on the cell it was fitted to and wrong as a rule — the single-cell trap,
sixth instance, caught by varying the axis the first reading held fixed.

### 4.4 Two source spellings emit BYTE-IDENTICAL `.text` from different IL

**Board #833**, and this is `w-hash` §4.2's finding reproduced in a second,
unrelated class. `order-lit-first` (the literal store moved to first in source)
and `spell-ptr` (`L* lh = &mListHead; lh->mNext = lh;`) each emit **all twenty

## 5. THE WALL — and it is not any of the fourteen

**Board #834, and it is the row worth reading.**

`crates/c2-core/src/codegen/alloc.rs` already models which register c2 gives each
producer of a store run: four clauses, `236/236` fit, **`250/250` holdout**, six
killer cells, and a preregistered exhaustive search over 52,416 rival allocators
that tops out at 179/236. It also refuses one population **by name**:

> *"**A run mixing constant and register-derived producers.** Clause 2 is
> measured only on the supplementary probe, never on the held-out partition, so
> it is not shipped."*

**`xboxheap`'s constructor is exactly that mix** — `addi r11,r3,8`
(register-derived, 2 uses) beside `li r10,0` (constant, 1 use). So the leaf half
of this body is outside the shipped allocator's domain before the call is even
considered.

### 5.1 The hypothesis, and its refutation in the grid built to test it

The obvious reading — and the one this lane held — is that the refusal is
**conservatism**: clause 1 (use count, descending) settles `xboxheap` on its own,
since 2 uses beats 1 and no tie arises. Clause 2 would only be needed for a tie.
If that were right, the next lane's rung would be small.

`work/w-next/allocgrid.py`, **10 of 10 graded**, anchor control passing:

```text
  anchor              addi 2 uses, li 1 use   ->  addi r11, li r10   both clauses agree
  diff-reg2-const1    addi 2,      li 1       ->  addi r11, li r10   both agree
  diff-reg3-const1    addi 3,      li 1       ->  addi r11, li r10   both agree
  diff-reg1-const2    addi 1,      li 2       ->  addi R11, li R10   <== CLAUSE 1 REFUTED
  diff-reg1-const3    addi 1,      li 3       ->  addi r10, li R11   <== CLAUSE 2 REFUTED
  tie-1-1             addi 1,      li 1       ->  addi r11, li r10   clause 2
  tie-2-2             addi 2,      li 2       ->  addi r11, li r10   clause 2
  tie-1-1-swapped     addi 1,      li 1       ->  addi r11, li r10   clause 2, order-invariant
```

> ### **`diff-reg1-const2` and `diff-reg1-const3` bracket a CROSSOVER that no shipped clause expresses.** At a use-count gap of **1** in the constant's favour the register-derived producer still takes `r11` (clause 1 wrong). At a gap of **2** the constant takes it (clause 2 wrong). The mixed-kind run obeys **neither** clause alone, and the threshold between them is **unexplained**.

**The obvious rule fits both cells — so it was taken to a holdout instead of
into the rung.** *"A constant needs a use-count advantage of ≥ 2 to outrank a
register-derived producer"* reproduces all eight cells above, and a rule fitted
to **two** cells on one side of one threshold is precisely how **P3** died
(`floor((N−1)/2)` fit three published cells exactly and failed at N = 5).

### 5.1.1 The holdout — 16 cells, 14 of them never fitted, 0 misses

`work/w-next/gapgrid.py` sweeps the full `(reg uses, const uses)` grid over
`1..4 × 1..4`. **The prediction is registered in the file's own docstring, above
the code that tests it**, and only `g1x2`/`g1x3` are the cells the rule came
from:

```text
  cell   reg const | addi  li  | winner  predicted | verdict  partition
  g1x1    1    1   | r11  r10  | reg     reg       | HIT      HOLDOUT
  g1x2    1    2   | r11  r10  | reg     reg       | HIT      fit
  g1x3    1    3   | r10  r11  | const   const     | HIT      fit
  g1x4    1    4   | r10  r11  | const   const     | HIT      HOLDOUT
  g2x3    2    3   | r11  r10  | reg     reg       | HIT      HOLDOUT
  g2x4    2    4   | r10  r11  | const   const     | HIT      HOLDOUT
  g3x4    3    4   | r11  r10  | reg     reg       | HIT      HOLDOUT
  …                                                            (16 rows)

  16 hit, 0 MISS, 0 out of regime, of 16 cells (14 holdout, 2 fit)
```

### 5.1.2 And ONE key reproduces both of `alloc.rs`'s clauses

`alloc.rs` notes that clauses 3 and 4 *"carry opposite signs inside one sort,
which is why the rule is not a priority function"*. Over the **mixed-kind** run,
clauses 1 and 2 collapse into a single priority key:

> ### rank by **`uses + (1 if the producer is register-derived else 0)`**, descending

That is clause 2 acting as **a bonus worth exactly one use** — which also makes
it the tie-break it already is, since a tie in `uses` becomes a `+1` win for the
register-derived producer. `work/w-next/unified.py` scores it over every mixed
cell both grids produced:

| key | cells | misses |
|---|---:|---:|
| **`uses + (register-derived ? 1 : 0)`, descending** | **24** | **0** |
| clause 1 alone (`uses`, descending) | 24 | **4** |
| clause 2 alone (register-derived first) | 24 | **4** |

**This is a candidate for the next lane to break, and it is not a rule and not a
licence.** Its domain is one register-derived producer beside one constant, use
counts `1..4`, **leaf** bodies, and one consumption pattern (the producer's value
stored, with the base folded into the object pointer). `alloc.rs` still refuses
the mix; this lane ships nothing. The honest summary is that a population
`alloc.rs` calls unmeasured now has **24 graded cells and a key that fits all of
them**, and that this is where a lane should start rather than where it should
stop.

**And three of the eight cells are the trap.** `anchor`, `diff-reg2-const1` and
`diff-reg3-const1` are cells where clause 1 and clause 2 **agree**, so they
discriminate nothing while looking like confirmations. A lane that built only
those — which is what "probe the shape I need" naturally produces, since
`xboxheap` *is* the agreeing case — would have come away certain that clause 1
settles the mix.

**So `alloc`'s refusal is load-bearing, not conservatism, and its own stated
reason understates it.** A lane that had read "clause 2 is untested, but clause 1
covers my cell" and shipped on that basis would have been right on `xboxheap`
and produced **wrong bytes** on `diff-reg1-const2` — a rule right on N−1 cells,
for the seventh recorded time.

### 5.2 The second wall, named so it is not rediscovered

Even with the allocation settled, `leaf_store.rs` states the other half plainly:
the store-run recognizer is `calls-0`, and *"the 832 bodies whose epilogue
follows a call need the general frame and stay refused."* `xboxheap` is
`calls-1` with `this` live across the call (`mr 31,3` … `bl` … `mr 3,31`). That
is a genuinely new emit class and it is **not** reachable by relaxing a gate.
