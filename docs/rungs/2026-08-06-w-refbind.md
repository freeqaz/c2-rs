## 1. The result, stated first

> ### **Board #839 is not a C++-level axis at all.** `L& q = s->inner;` makes `c1xx` emit a **`0x26` temp-bind statement** into the `.ex` body stream. Take the *same* binding to a sub-object at **offset 0** and the two `.ex` streams differ in **exactly one byte** — `0x60` against `0x00`, the displacement in that statement, at file offset `0x0aaf` — with **byte-identical `.sy`**. That one byte moves the emitted producer order and, for a `slwi` producer, the register allocation. There is no C++ spelling left in the explanation.

> ### And the allocation half of #839 was **one cell family**. Measured as a threshold per producer spelling: `self` 1→1, `addi` 2→2, `add` 2→2 are **unmoved** by the binding, and only the shift moves — from **2 to ∞**: with the bind present a `slwi` producer **never** takes `r11` against a 1-use constant, at 1, 2, 3, 4 or 5 uses. R2 registered that number as **3** and it is the lane's one wrong digit.

> ### **And then the axis turned out not to be the bind at all.** §5.2: a body with two pointer formals whose two store runs are **split across them** — *no bind anywhere* — schedules and allocates exactly like the bound one, while the same two formals with **one** base schedules like the unbound one. **The bind is one way to create a second store-base value and not the only one**, and a bind at offset 0 creates none, which is why R8 came out as it did. Every cell this lane classified is covered by that one description. It is **not shipped** — six discriminating rows is not a holdout — and #856 stands either way, because what one IL byte does is true regardless of why.

> ### **H-self (#837) is REFUTED** — 11 misses on **72** never-fitted graded cells of a holdout frozen and committed before a single cell was compiled. **Four of the eleven have nothing to do with #839**: `extsh` and a `lwz` load each take `r11` against a 1-use constant at 1 use, in **both** binding modes, which H-self forbids everywhere. Per the standing instruction the lane **STOPS** at the refutation and fits nothing on the cells that produced it.

### 2.1 R10 is the row worth reading

R10 was registered on the reading every prior grid supports: the binding is
*"the producer's stores address through a temp"*, so a binding the producer's
stores do not use should do nothing. It does the same thing. `Q3-ref-const-side`
binds `L& q = s->inner;`, stores the **constant** through it, and stores the
register-derived producer's value into `s->head` — a different sub-object,
spelled directly — and comes out ref-like on both spellings, against a
`Q3-none-const-side` control that is none-like.

So the axis is not *"how the measured stores are addressed"*. It is a property of
**the body**: does it contain a bind statement with a non-zero displacement at
all. R9 says the same thing from the other side — one scalar reference is enough,
and it need not be shared. Both misses point the same way, and neither was the
direction I expected. **R3, which scored a HIT, is right about its four cells and
is the wrong description of the axis**; that is recorded here rather than by
rewording R3.

**§5.2 then explains both misses in one line.** A scalar `int&` binds an address
that is not `r3`, and a bind serving the constant's store does the same — each
puts a **second store-base value** in the body, which is all the schedule reads.
R9 and R10 were not two separate surprises; they were the axis showing through.

### 3.1 The eight thresholds — R2

`T(spelling, binding)` = the smallest register-producer use count taking `r11`
against a **1-use** constant, searched over `1..5`.

| spelling | `T(·, no bind)` | `T(·, bind)` | registered | |

| `addi` — `u + 5` | 2 | 2 | 2, 2 | HIT |
| `add` — `u + v` | 2 | 2 | 2, 2 | HIT |

> ### #839's allocation half rests on the `slwi` producer and on nothing else in this grid — which is what §0.2 of the prereg read off w-alloc2's own committed outputs *before* measuring, and it holds. But the shift's behaviour is worse than "a shifted threshold": with the bind present it never wins at all, so there is no threshold to shift.

**And §5.2 corrects the attribution of this table's one moving row.** `basegrid`'s
`B2-split-cs-pt` shows the `slwi` producer losing at 2 uses against a 1-use
constant **with no bind in the body at all** — two store bases are enough. So
*"the shift never wins under the binding"* is really *"under two store bases"*,
and the bind is one way to get there.

### 3.2 The schedule — the refined statement R1 could not express

R1 was written pairwise so it could not be satisfied by an average, and it loses
on pairs where there is nothing to move. What the grid actually shows:

* **without a bind**, the producer emitted first is the one with **more uses**,
  ties to the constant — 28 of 28 cells of `bindgrid`'s `none` mode;
* **with a bind**, the **constant is emitted first**, and use counts stop
  mattering — **28 of 28** in `bindgrid`, **8 of 8** more on its `ptr`/`iptr`
  modes, **16 of 16** in `refprobe`, and **35 of 37** in the frozen holdout.
  **87 of 89 across the three grids.**

**Both exceptions are the `lwz` producer**, where the load of `s->f8` must precede
the store to `s->f0` because c2 cannot prove they do not alias. That is a
dependence, not a counterexample, and it is stated as a limit rather than
excluded from the count.

**ORDER and ALLOC are separable and were read separately in every cell.** They
disagree in 3 of `bindgrid`'s 28 unbound cells and in every `self`-spelling bound
cell, which is why the two columns exist.

### 3.3 And it pins the STORES, not just the two producers

`work/w-refbind/srcorder.py` re-reads the committed disassembly — it compiles
nothing — and asks a question `bindgrid.py` did not print. Every cell's source
store order is derivable from its name (constants to `s->f0, f1, …` at 0, 4, 8;
producer values to `s->inner.a0, a1, …` at 64, 68, 72), and every binding mode
uses the same offsets by construction.

```text
  emitted store order == SOURCE order:
    bound    36 of 36 cells
    unbound  20 of 52
```

Without a bind the producer's stores are **hoisted above** the constant's, and at
`cu ≥ 2` the two runs **interleave**: `P3-shift-none-r2k3` emits `0, 64, 68, 4, 8`
for a source order of `0, 4, 8, 64, 68`.

> **Scoped deliberately.** This is `bindgrid`'s shape and not a general rule.
> w-next's board **#832** records source order failing on a *constructor* grid
> whose cells **do** carry a binding — and those cells vary things this grid
> holds fixed, so the two results are consistent and neither generalises over the
> other. What #832 could not say, because it had no unbound cell, is that the
> binding is one of the inputs.

## 5. The IL — one byte

`work/w-refbind/ilcmp.py` / `.out`. c2's only input is the IL bundle, so two
spellings that emit different bytes **must** have different IL; that half is a
tautology and was not what R4 registered. Four cases with the **same struct name,
the same function name and the same formals**, because a name is *in* the IL and
comparing `bindgrid` captures by size would compare their names.

```text
  none       .db=705  .ex=2868  .gl=329  .in=439  .sy=68
  ref        .db=721  .ex=2877  .gl=328  .in=439  .sy=89
  head       .db=721  .ex=2877  .gl=329  .in=439  .sy=89     (bind at offset 0)
  unnamed    .db=705  .ex=2868  .gl=332  .in=439  .sy=68     (&s->inner)->aN
```

**`unnamed`'s `.ex` is byte-identical to `none`'s** (`faffd1b2`). `c1xx` folds the
address expression away, which is the whole reason `refprobe` graded that cell
none-like — an obj-level verdict with an IL-level cause.

`none` → `ref` is a **+9 byte** edit that opens the body with the bind:

```text
  none  …  b9 0c 0a 86 43 81 20 33 86 41 74 60 27 86 43 86 20 33 …   recompute s+96 per store
  ref   26 11 0a  b9 0c 0a 86 43 81 20 33 86 41 74 60 27 …           bind local 0x11 := s+96
        …  b9 11 0a 86 43 85 20 33 86 41 74 00 27 …                  each store LOADS the local
```

> ### And the decisive comparison is `ref` against `head`. Their `.sy` streams are **byte-identical** (`91f34404`) and their `.ex` streams differ at **exactly one offset**, `0x0aaf`: **`0x60` against `0x00`** — the displacement inside the bind statement. `ref` emits `li slwi stw stw stw` with the constant in `r11`; `head` emits `slwi li stw stw stw` with the producer in `r11`. **One IL byte, two schedules and two allocations.** Board **#856**.

That also names the mechanism without inventing one: at displacement 0 the bound
value **is** the incoming pointer, already live; at displacement 96 it is a
distinct value that the body carries — even though, in these cells, c2 folds it
back into the stores' displacements and **never materialises an instruction for
it**.

### 5.1 It is a FLAG, not a count — R11

`work/w-refbind/bindcount.py`, declared in addendum §9.5 before it existed.
**16 cells, 16 graded.** Across **1, 2 and 3** non-zero-displacement binds the
emitted producer ORDER and the relative ALLOC are identical, for all four
spellings; `n = 0` gives the unbound answer in every case.

```text
  self     n0 prod,prod  | n1 const,prod   n2 const,prod   n3 const,prod    flag
  addi     n0 prod,prod  | n1 const,prod   n2 const,prod   n3 const,prod    flag
  add      n0 prod,prod  | n1 const,prod   n2 const,prod   n3 const,prod    flag
  shift    n0 prod,prod  | n1 const,const  n2 const,const  n3 const,const   flag
```

> **The confound is stated rather than hidden.** An *unused* bind is deleted by
> the front end — `bindgrid`'s `ref-unused` is none-like — so the extra binds
> have to be **used**, and the use adds a `lwz` producer that takes a pool
> register. That is why the constant moves `r10 → r9` at `n ≥ 2`. These are
> therefore **not a pure count axis**. What R11 registered, and what is constant,
> is the **relative** order and winner between the two *measured* producers.

R11 mattering is the modelling question behind it: had a count moved either
readout, the bind would be a **producer-like entity competing for the pool**, and
`order::schedule` / `alloc::allocate` would need a pseudo-producer for it. It
does not, so today's reading is one bit of body state.

### 5.2 The axis is the number of store BASES — R13, and it corrects §5's own description

`work/w-refbind/basegrid.py`, declared in addendum §9.7 before its discriminating
cell was compiled. **10 cells, 10 graded, 0 out-of-regime**, all at ONE signature
(`void g(S* s, S* t, int u, int v)`) so the pool and the formal registers are
constant, and **four of the five bodies contain no bind at all**.

```text
  B0  both runs off `s`                    1 base   prod first, prod r11   none-like
  B1  both runs off `t`, `s` unused        1 base   prod first, prod r11   none-like
  B2  constant off `s`, producer off `t`   2 bases  const first            ref-like
  B3  constant off `t`, producer off `s`   2 bases  const first            ref-like
  B4  both off `s`, plus a bind            2 bases  const first            ref-like

  R13 rows (poles excluded): HIT 6 | MISS 0 | spellings that discriminate nothing: 0
```

`B1` is the discriminator: two pointer formals, **one** base, no bind — and it is
none-like, so *having* a second pointer is not the axis. `B2`/`B3` reach the
ref-like schedule **with no bind anywhere**.

> ### So the description that covers every cell this lane classified is: **the schedule pins to source order iff the body carries more than ONE distinct store-base value.** A `0x26` bind at a non-zero displacement *makes* a second one; a bind at displacement 0 does not (**R8**); `(&s->inner)->aN` is folded before c2 sees it; `int w = <expr>` names a value and not a base; `S& z = *s` names `r3` itself. Board **#865**.

**One rival is NOT separated by these cells, and is named rather than left
implicit.** *"More than one distinct store-base value in the body"* and *"the
constant's store and the producer's stores have different bases"* agree on every
cell here, because every cell has exactly two runs. A body with three runs across
two bases, where the constant and the producer happen to share one, separates
them and was not built.

**It is not shipped and it is not a rule.** It is a **post-hoc description**
registered before the one cell that could kill it was compiled, with **six**
discriminating rows — the same standing that H-self had before this lane's own
holdout killed it (§6). A successor lane owes it a fresh frozen population, not
another pass over these cells.

**R12 is how it was found, and R12 lost.** §9.6 registered that a reference
*formal* would carry a bind — it does not: its `.ex` is **2,851** bytes against
the direct spelling's **2,871**, twenty *shorter*, while the bound spelling is
`+9` and reproduces `ilcmp`'s cost exactly. A body with no bind that does not
schedule like the unbound cells is precisely what forced the base-count reading.
`formalprobe`'s own obj half is **UNGRADED by its printed marker** — its two
poles coincided, because its `none` pole had silently changed the store base too.
The instrument saying *"this discriminates nothing"* is what stopped that cell
being scored as a confirmation.

## 6. H-self on a holdout where the binding varies — REFUTED

Fresh axes: ten producer spellings H-self had never been shown (`sub`, `and`,
`or`, `xor`, `neg`, `nor`, `srawi`, `srwi`, `extsh`, and a **`lwz` load**),
self-referential producers at fresh addresses, and — the point — **the binding
mode**, which `opgrid` and `selfgrid` never varied at all.

### 6.1 The eleven misses are two families, and only one is about #839

```text
  (bind, reg 2 uses vs const 1)   sub and or xor neg nor srwi   ->  const takes r11   7 MISS
  (either mode, 1 use vs 1 use)   extsh, lwz                    ->  prod  takes r11   4 MISS
```

The second family is the important one: **`extsh` and a `lwz` load beat a 1-use
constant at 1 use with and without the bind**, which H-self forbids in every
mode. **H-self dies on both sides of board #839's axis**, so its refutation does
not depend on this lane's other finding.

**And it dies on its NEGATIVE side, which is worth stating precisely.** Every
cell whose producer H-self calls *self* came out the way it predicted: the
`&s->inner2`-into-`inner2` family is **6 of 6** across both binding modes — of
which 3 are the fit-shaped corner and 3 are never-fitted, counted once and not
twice. All eleven misses are cells where H-self
withholds the bonus — seven where it expected the producer to win on use count
alone and it lost, four where it expected the constant to win and the producer
took `r11`. So the finding is not *"the self bonus is not real"*; it is that
**`uses` plus a self bonus is not the whole key**, and the residue is #862's
opcode axis. That distinction is the reason this section reports the partition
and stops.

### 6.2 The (bind, 2-vs-1) outcome splits by producer opcode — DATA, NOT A RULE

Across `bindgrid` and the holdout, at the same point — a bind present, the
register-derived producer at 2 uses, the constant at 1:

```text
  takes r11 :  add   addi   srawi   extsh   addi(&q, self)   addi(&r, cross)
  loses     :  slwi  srwi   sub     and     or   xor   neg   nor
```

`slwi` and `srwi` are both `rlwinm`; `srawi` is a real instruction and it wins.

**And the split is not created by the bind.** `bindgrid`'s P3 cells separate the
same two spellings with **no** bind present: at `(ru 2, cu 3)` and `(ru 3, cu 2)`
the `add` producer takes `r11` and the `slwi` producer does not. So `add`'s
unbound allocation is *"prod iff `ru ≥ 2`", independent of `cu`* — which is
already not "use count, descending", since a 2-use producer beats a 3-use
constant — while the shift additionally needs `cu = 1`. The bind then takes the
shift's remaining win away entirely (#857).

**No rule is stated for any of this and none is fitted.** The standing instruction after
a refutation is that the miss is the deliverable and the cells that produced it
are not the cells to fit the successor on — which is exactly how w-next's key and
then H-self died. Recorded as board **#862** so the next lane starts from a
population rather than from this partition.
