## 2. THE RULE, and the three clauses that make it up

Everything below is read off objs this lane compiled with the real `c2.dll`
under wibo at the dc3 workload's own flags and cwd, and 30 of them re-compiled
at `/Ox` and `/O2`.

Write `s(d)` for the register destination `d` is filled from, and the **chain**
rooted at `A` for the forced move order

```text
   r11 <- A ,  A <- s(A) ,  s(A) <- s²(A) , … , s⁻¹(A) <- r11
```

### 2.1 SPLIT — the entry block ASCENDS, the call site DESCENDS

The call site's rule is the one this emitter already had for the *unguarded*
permutation (`moves_descending`, `docs/CODEGEN_FRAMED_CALLS.md` §3.2: *"highest
destination first"*). The entry block is its mirror. A chain can therefore be
laid out **only when its destination sequence is unimodal**, and the split falls
at the peak — which is not an extra clause, it is what makes the anchor clause
in §2.2 have a *reason*.

```text
   pk3up   g3(a1,a2,a0)   chain writes r3, r4, r5   ASCENDING   hoist 2, call 1
   pk3dn   g3(a2,a0,a1)   chain writes r3, r5, r4   descent at r4  hoist 1, call 2
```

`pk3dn` is board #1414's `[2,0,1]`, and it was **the whole of** the evidence for
this clause. Here the descent has **51 witnesses over 10 distinct register
triples** — one rotation of every 3-subset of the argument slots at arity 2–5 —
asserted by the generator before the grid was written.

**The literal slots confirm the descending half independently.** In the `lit`
cells the `li` takes its place at the call by destination order and not by
dependency, which nothing forces: `lit_n3_p102` emits `li r5,72` *before*
`mr r4,r11`, `lit_n3_p210` emits `mr r5,r11` *before* `li r4,72`. `?mmioGetInfo`
does the first of those at `0x34`/`0x38`.

### 2.2 ANCHOR — the guard's own scrutinee, and #1414 is refuted here

**The anchor is the register the guard compares**, when the chain rooted there
is unimodal; otherwise the cycle's minimum. Board #1414 says the minimum,
always.

| | grid 1 | grid 2 | grid 3 | all |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| in-class guarded cells | 127 | 277 | 428 | **832** |
| **R-MIN — board #1414's rule** | 113 | 149 | 132 | **394** |
| first-in-cycle (grid 1's fit) | 127 | 262 | 308 | **697** |
| scan (grid 2's fit) | 127 | 277 | 400 | **804** |
| scan + lowest (grid 3's fit) | 127 | 277 | 428 | **832** |

The cell that breaks #1414 smallest is `gtgt_n5_p20134_g2` — `g(a2,a0,a1)` with
the guard on `a2`:

```text
   #1414 predicts    mr r11,r3 · mr r3,r5    |   mr r5,r4 · mr r4,r11
   c2 emits          mr r11,r5               |   mr r5,r4 · mr r4,r3 · mr r3,r11
```

Same permutation, same production; only the guard moved. `w-clear`'s five cells
all guarded `a0`, whose home `r3` was also every one of those cycles' minimum,
so the two hypotheses were the same hypothesis there. **The generator asserts a
minimum of 20 cells where the guard's formal and the cycle minimum differ**, and
refuses to write the grid otherwise — grid 1 has 56.

`R-MAX` — "the cycle's highest register", the rival a grid that guards only the
maximum cannot rule out — scores **14 of 118** on grid 1 and is why grid 2's
middle-guard class exists.

### 2.3 COMPARE — two maps, and one of them is not the other

Once a park has run, a guard compares whatever register holds its formal. **The
first guard and the later ones do not agree about which that is:**

```text
   guard 0      its formal's HOME, unless the entry block overwrote the home —
                then wherever the value went
   guard 1..n   the LAST register the entry block wrote the value into, even
                when the home still holds a live copy
```

The separating pair is `gtgt_n4_p0312_g3` against `g2ord_n4_p0312_g23`:

```text
   gtgt   mr r11,r6 ; cmplwi cr6,r6,0     one guard, and it reads the HOME
   g2ord  mr r11,r6 ; cmplwi cr6,r5,0 ; … ; cmplwi cr6,r11,0
```

Byte-identical entry blocks, the same formal tested, and c2 picks a different
register. Both are correct code; the choice is not forced. **Scored over all
1,654 guards of the three grids: 0 wrong.** Under a single map, 327 are.

### 2.4 What the guarded and unguarded forms have in common

The two are **opposite rotations of one cycle break**, not two rules.
`gt_argperm.py::predict_pure`'s local-minimum model is the unguarded half: it
parks `s(m)` and ends by writing `m`, where `m` is the cycle minimum. The
guarded form parks `A` and ends by writing `s⁻¹(A)`. The fixture keeps `ctl`,
the unguarded swap, beside `pk2` precisely so the two breaks are visible in one
obj — `mr r11,r4` against `mr r11,r3`.

## 5. The emitter, and the 155 the differential caught

`call_seq_parts` now returns a third value, `SeqPark`, carrying the entry-block
words **and** the two register maps of §2.3; `call_seq_text` takes it as a
`park: &[u8]` emitted between the prologue and the first early return; and
`seq_early_emit_remapped` resolves each guard's compare register out of the same
`SeqPark` the moves came from — one place, so a compare cannot name a register
the moves above it did not write. Acceptance lives in the **IL parser**
(`park_in_class`) with `seq_entry_park` restating it as the backstop, which is
board **#139** and the rule `w-clear` gave for putting the *refusal* in both
places: with only the emitter deciding, the census over-claims and
`TuResult::fn_gate_refusals` fills up. Measured at the tip: **empty**.

**The first version of this emitter was wrong and looked right.** Split and
anchor correct, one map for the compare register — and `c2rs gap` returned
**155 `Port=Mismatch`** over the three grids, every one a single word: the
guard's `cmplwi` naming `r11` where c2 named a home register, or the reverse.
It is exactly the failure this project's one rule exists for, and it is the
second time in two lanes that a park emitter has produced plausible wrong bytes.
`work/w-mmio/cmpreg.py` then read the answer off c2 rather than off the model:
**three** disagreement patterns, all at guard index 0, and the two-map rule
above closed all of them.

`PREREG.md` §4 registered **OPTIMISTIC**, with the argument that this rung
*lifts* a composition floor rather than adding one, and that a floor-lifting lane
comes back optimistic because everything the floor was hiding is still

**Board #770 goes to twelve-of-thirteen optimistic**, and the useful part is
