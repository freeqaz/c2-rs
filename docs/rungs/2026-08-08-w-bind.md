## 2. What `26 11 0a` measured as

`w-f23` §5.1 priced it and this lane did not re-derive it — it read four more
instances of it, at the workload's own `/GR /O1 /Oi /EHsc` (board **#1112**),
one per grid cell, and the four together say what the byte *is*:

```text
  b_leaf_bind    26 fb 09  b9 f8 09 86 43 81 20  33 86 41 74 08 27 86 43 86 20  32 86 43 89 20 4b
  b_off0         26 fa 09  b9 f6 09 86 43 81 20  33 86 41 74 00 27 86 43 86 20  32 86 43 89 20 4b
  b_ptr_local    26 fc 09  b9 f8 09 86 43 81 20  33 86 41 74 08 27 86 43 86 20  32 86 43 85 20 4b
  b_target_bind  26 01 0a  b9 ff 09 a6 43 81 20  33 86 41 74 08 27 a6 43 8d 20  32 86 43 90 20 4b
```

* **`26 <tok>` is a store whose DESTINATION is a `.sy` automatic** — specifically
  a `TYPE_KIND_DATA_PTR` one, so the membership test is `SyView::ptr_locals`
  and it is **positive**. Prereg **P1**, hit.
* **The value is F2's address production**, read through `parse_addr_value`
  rather than a second decoder: designator, offset-add run, **no `30`**.
* **The `32`'s type is the LOCAL's own and does NOT restate the value's** —
  `86 43 89 20` for a `BE&`, `86 43 85 20` for a `BE*` over the same address,
  `86 43 90 20` for the target's `auto&`. So the type-restatement rule every
  other store makes cannot be applied here, and a positive 4-byte-pointer gate
  is drawn instead.
* **The first two lines differ in EXACTLY ONE BYTE** — the displacement, `08`
  against `00` — and their whole bodies emit differently or identically on that
  one byte. That is board **#856** ("#839 is a one-byte IL axis, not a C++
  spelling axis") reproduced from a second lane's captures.
* **`b_ptr_local` is a POINTER local, not a reference**, and `c1xx` spells it as
  the same statement. So the construct is not about references at all; it is
  about *a local that holds a formal's interior address*. New — nothing on
  record varied this.

## 4. The production, and the three things it refuses

### 4.1 It is refused BY NAME, and that was decided before it was written

`shape_to_function` returns `None` for `BodyShape::StoreRunBind`, with **every
field named** rather than dropped with `..`, so a future carrier has to come back
to the arm.

The alternative — widening `parse_store_stmt` so a bound base simply flows into
`BodyShape::StoreRun` — is a two-line change and it is the wrong one.
`StoreRun` and `StoreLeaf` **do** reach `codegen`, and #1128 measured that the
two source spellings emit different bodies, so a reader that resolved the local
to `this` would hand the emitter the *other* body's op stream. That is board
**#232**: a refusal that became an emit and sat on master for **255 commits**
while the workload scan read `mismatch 0` throughout.

The emitter's own `reg_of(<local>) == None` would decline as well. **That is a
second line and deliberately not the first** — "by construction" is the
reasoning that let #232 run, and this lane's prereg §1 registered the decision
before the production existed rather than discovering it afterwards.

### 4.2 The zero-offset bind is excluded, and the exclusion is a measurement

`b_off0` and `b_off0_ctrl` — the same body with and without the bind, at
displacement 0 — have **byte-identical `.text`**. So at offset 0 the bind makes
no second store-base value (boards #856/#865 confirmed), the two spellings are
one body, and giving such a bind its own base symbol would be a **wrong reading
of the schedule** even though nothing emits.

It is excluded for the reason F2 excludes the zero-offset *address* (`w-f23`
§3.2), and prereg **P2** registered the exclusion before the cell that tests it
was compiled. The unit test mutates one byte of a captured segment — `08` → `00`
— which is the same one-byte difference the two captured cells have.

### 4.3 The dead bind is DECLINED, and this is the conversion this lane did not take

`b_dead` binds a reference and never reads it. Its `.text` is **byte-identical**
to `b_dead_ctrl`, the same body with the line deleted — so c2 deletes the
statement entirely, and a reader that dropped it would turn `b_dead` into an
ordinary `StoreRun`, which is `match`. **That is a cell that converts**, and it
is one line of code away.

It is not taken. One cell pair is not a population; six allocation keys are
already on record as refuted for being fitted to exactly that much, and the
seventh died on a frozen holdout. `collect_store_run` refuses a bind nothing
reads, and §8 names the frozen population a successor owes before converting it.

### 4.4 The one place the binding IS discharged, and why it is right there

The run's **overlap** gate. `listHead.mNext` and `this->mListHead.mNext` are the
same four bytes under two tokens, and c2 deletes the dead one of two overlapping
stores — so a gate keyed on the token alone would let a provably-aliasing pair
through. The gate now resolves through the bind before comparing ranges.

That gate is about **bytes**; the schedule is about **symbols**. Resolving an
address for an aliasing test changes nothing about what the op stream carries —
the ops keep `IlOp::Load(<local>)`, which is what keeps the two spellings apart.
The resolver is the identity when `binds` is empty, which is every pre-existing
caller.

### 4.5 Clause 1 does not apply to a bind-carrying run, and that is `w-f23` §3.3's lesson consumed

`collect_store_run`'s widened-literal battery opens with **ONE base symbol**. A
bind *is* a second base symbol — that is the whole content of #1128 — so the
clause would make this production unreachable on its own target. `w-f23` §3.3
recorded exactly that mistake (a defensible F2 gate that hid `xboxheap`) so it
would not be re-derived; it was consumed here rather than re-derived. The clause
protects `order::schedule` and `alloc::allocate`, **neither of which is reached**,
because `shape_to_function` refuses the shape by name. It still applies to every
run without a bind, so it only ever refuses more.

## 7. The registered loss, and where it landed

Prereg **P0** registered the loss as **dispatch order, not grammar**, and that is
where the lane spent — but not in the way it was registered.

`parse_segment_shape` dispatches on **the first byte of the body's first
statement**. A body whose bind comes first (`b_leaf_bind`, `b_bind_first`,
`b_use2`, `b_two_binds`, `b_ptr_local`, `b_const_ref`, `b_use_value_only`)
enters the `0x26` arm and **never reaches the store block at all**; a body whose
bind sits in the middle — which is `xboxheap.cpp`'s own spelling — enters through
the designator and reaches only the store block. **One dispatch site would have
covered half the shape and looked complete**, because the target is in the half
that works.

So the recognizer has **two dispatch sites and one body**, exactly as
`try_parse_store_run` and `try_parse_store_run_call` have two entries and one
`collect_store_run`. Both sites are non-committal, both are stated as such, and
the `0x26` one is placed **ahead of** `try_parse_assign_body_detail` so a
declining body still reports assign's blocker and no census key moves.

**What P0 got wrong** is its second clause: it predicted at least one grid cell
would be *swallowed* by an existing production and print the wrong key. None
was — placing the site ahead of `assign` rather than behind it is what avoided
it, and the prediction was made on the assumption of the opposite placement.

The other structural cost was `run_call_tail`, split out of
`try_parse_store_run_call` as a **pure code move**. Board #1129's regime gate,
#866's transfer gate, the four call locators and the multi-word-literal bound
now have one home and two tails. GRID2's `c2_ctor_bind_argsetup` is what shows
the inheritance is real rather than asserted.
