# w-llvm — LLVM can read our objs, and after 5,478,967 field comparisons it does not disagree with us once. The build was 190 seconds and was not necessary.

    Lane:      w-llvm, 2026-08-04, worktree `wt-w-llvm` off master `c303ad0`
    Prereg:    work/w-llvm/PREDICTION.txt, written before any measurement
    Ships:     tools/llvm/ only. NOTHING under `crates/`, `scripts/` or
               `fixtures/`. No fixture, no codegen, no census change.
    Status:    FINDINGS. TU match is 8 at both ends and this lane does not
               move it. Its output is diagnostic throughput.
    Acts on:   docs/PRIOR_ART.md §3.2, verdict ADOPT.

## The headline, in the order the numbers were earned

1. **The build is optional.** A stock distro `llvm-readobj` decodes these objs
   today, given a scratch copy of the obj with the machine word rewritten
   `0x01F2 → 0x01F0`. Verified: stock Arch `llvm 22.1.8` and the patched build
   produce **byte-identical decodes on all 155 objs / 5,478,967 field
   comparisons**. The patch buys convenience and relocation *names*; it buys no
   decoded field.
2. **The build is also cheap** — 190 s wall, 177 MB — so it was done anyway and
   is committed as `tools/llvm/ppcbe.patch`.
3. **Every PRIOR_ART §3.2 claim reproduced**, with two corrections and one
   sharpening (§3).
4. **The disagreement matrix is empty.** 155 real dc3 objs, 35 fields, 65,401
   sections, 194,297 symbols, 295,302 relocations, **0 disagreements** between
   `llvm-readobj` and each of `tools/coffdump.py`, `scripts/gt_dump.py`, and
   `crates/c2-obj`.
5. **Two defects were found anyway, both outside the compared field set** —
   `scripts/gt_dump.py` does not resolve `/NNN` long section names, and three
   entries of its relocation-name table are the **x86** table. One of the two is
   live on `/Z7` captures today. Neither is fixed here: `scripts/` is another
   lane's seam.

### Correction 1 — `.debug$T` is not in our objs, and `.debug$S` is two records

At the workload's flags (`work/dc3-workload/flags.txt`, no `/Zi`, no `/Z7`),
across the **153** workload objs:

* **`.debug$T`: 0 sections.** There is nothing for that half of the decoder to
  read.
* **`.debug$S`: 153 sections** — exactly one per obj — 140–204 bytes each
  — the size is `len(output path) + 80..83`, i.e. it varies with the embedded
  output path and its record alignment padding and with nothing else —
  **containing exactly two records each: `S_OBJNAME` and `S_COMPILE2`. 306
  records over 153 objs, 0 decode errors.** `S_COMPILE2` carries `Machine:
  PPC604 (0x42)`, `FrontendVersion: 16.0.11886`, `BackendVersion:
  16.0.11886`, and the flag `NoDbgInfo`.

So "fully decodes `.debug$S`" is true and "a free `.debug$S`/`.debug$T`
decoder" overstates its present worth by a wide margin: **on today's corpus the
decoder reads two records per obj and nothing else.**

**But it is a real asset the moment we capture debug info.** Two control
captures at `+/Z7` (`work/w-llvm/z7/`, this lane's only non-workload
compilations) produce `.debug$T`, and LLVM decodes **4,756 CodeView records
with zero errors** — `LF_MFUNCTION` 918, `LF_POINTER` 678, `LF_ONEMETHOD` 621,
`S_UDT` 207, `S_GPROC32` 10, `S_FRAMEPROC` 10, `S_REGISTER` 13, `S_REGREL32` 2,
and the rest. That is the claim's actual size: **conditional on a capture mode
we do not currently use.**

### Correction 2 (sharpening) — the x86 register name, named

`S_REGISTER { Type: CriticalSection* const, Seg: BL (0x4), Name: this }`, from
`work/w-llvm/z7/CritSec_z7.obj`. Register **4**, holding `this`, which on this
ABI is **`r3`** — printed as x86 `BL` because LLVM carries CodeView register
tables for x86/ARM/ARM64 only. The number is right; the name is not; and LLVM
labels the field `Seg`, which is its own trap.

## 4. The disagreement matrix

**155 real dc3 objs** compiled by the real `c2.dll` under wibo at the workload's
own flags via `work/w-frame/refobj.sh` — 28 from a deterministic every-37th
sample plus `App.cpp`/`Licenses.cpp`/`Shader.cpp`/`CampaignPerformer.cpp`, 125
from every-7th, 2 `/Z7` controls. `work/capture-cache` was never touched.

Compared with `tools/llvm/xcheck.py`, which decodes each obj with `llvm-readobj`
**and** with each of our three readers and diffs the structured results:

| our reader | field instances compared | disagreements |
|---|---:|---:|
| `tools/coffdump.py` | 3,099,499 | **0** |
| `scripts/gt_dump.py` | 2,379,158 | **0** |
| `crates/c2-obj` | 310 | **0** |
| **total** | **5,478,967** | **0** |

Corpus actually walked: **65,401 sections, 194,297 symbols, 295,302
relocations**, 12.1 s wall.

**35 distinct fields**, so this is not a three-field agreement:

| level | fields |
|---|---|
| header (7) | machine, section count, timestamp, symbol-table pointer, symbol count, optional-header size, characteristics |
| section (9 + count) | resolved name, **raw 8-byte name field**, VirtualSize, VirtualAddress, RawDataSize, PointerToRawData, PointerToRelocations, RelocationCount, Characteristics |
| symbol (6 + count) | name, value, section number, reconstructed type word, storage class, aux count |
| aux section-def (6) | Length, RelocationCount, LineNumberCount, Checksum, Number, Selection |
| relocation (3 + count) | offset, **raw type word**, symbol index |
| `crates/c2-obj` (2) | the full `section_names()` sequence, the full `text_comdat_functions()` sequence |

The last row is small in count and large in weight: those two lists are what
`gap.rs` turns into the `emit-sec-*` keys of factor C and into the emitted-
function denominator. LLVM's independent decode reproduces both, in order, on
every obj, when the same COMDAT-leader rule is applied to LLVM's symbol table.
`ObjImage` **refused 0 of 155**.

### Why "0 disagreements" is a claim and not a silence

`xcheck.py` prints its comparison count and **exits non-zero if it is zero**.
Beyond that:

* **The obvious control is wrong, and it was tried first.** Corrupting a byte
  in the obj (`sec[3].rawsize 16 → 17`) produced **0 diffs**, correctly: both
  readers read the same corrupted file. A differential harness cannot be
  validated by corrupting its shared input.
* `--selftest` perturbs **one field on our side only**, after decode, and
  requires exactly one diff per section: *"perturbed `sec.rawsize` on the c2-rs
  side of 65,401 sections; comparison reported 65,401 `sec.rawsize` diffs and
  65,401 diffs total → PASS."*
* Fields that failed to parse would show as `None != int`, i.e. as
  disagreements, not as skips.

### Which one is right, where they agree

They agree, so the question is what the agreement licenses. It licenses this:
**for every structural field of a c2-emitted obj at the workload's flags, our
three readers are correct, because an implementation with no knowledge of this
project reaches the same numbers.** It does **not** license anything about
fields no c2 obj exercises — which is where the two defects below live.

## 5. What LLVM found that the compare did not: two defects in our own readers

Both are in `scripts/gt_dump.py`, which is **another lane's seam**. Neither is
fixed here. Both are recorded so the owning lane can act.

### 5.1 `gt_dump.py` does not resolve `/NNN` long section names — latent

`crates/c2-obj` calls `/NNN` one of *"three chances for a second reader to
disagree with this one"* and ROADMAP §10.14 records a session lost to exactly
that. No c2 obj at these flags carries one: **0 of 65,401 sections** have a
`/`-prefixed name field, all 14 distinct names are ≤ 8 characters. So the branch
is unreachable in production and a corpus sweep can never catch it.

`tools/llvm/longname_probe.py` builds the input that does reach it — a real obj
with one section's name field rewritten to `/NNN` and the long name appended to
the string table — and asks everyone:

| reader | answer |
|---|---|
| `tools/coffdump.py` | `.averylongsectionname$probe` ✓ |
| `crates/c2-obj` | `.averylongsectionname$probe` ✓ |
| `llvm-readobj` | `.averylongsectionname$probe` ✓ |
| **`scripts/gt_dump.py`** | **`/23196`** ✗ |

`gt_dump.py:66` is `h[0:8].rstrip(b"\0")` with no `/` branch. **This is a probe
of the decoders, not a claim about c2's output.** Severity: latent. If a future
capture mode ever emits a long section name, `gt_dump.py` silently reports the
offset as the name.

### 5.2 Three entries of `gt_dump.py`'s relocation-name table are the **x86** table — and one is live today

Relocation type words actually present across the 155 objs:

| type | count | where |
|---|---:|---|
| `0x0002` ADDR32 | 60,600 | `.rdata`, `.pdata`, `.data`, `.text`, `.xdata$x`, `.CRT$XCU` |
| `0x0006` REL24 | 60,358 | `.text` |
| `0x0010` REFHI | 41,789 | `.text` |
| `0x0011` REFLO | 45,349 | `.text` |
| `0x0012` PAIR | 87,138 | `.text` |
| `0x000B` SECREL | 34 | `.debug$S`, `/Z7` only |
| `0x000C` SECTION | 34 | `.debug$S`, `/Z7` only |

No modifier bit (`NEG`/`BRTAKEN`/`BRNTAKEN`/`TOCDEFN`) is ever set, and
`REFHI + REFLO = 41,789 + 45,349 = 87,138 = PAIR` **exactly** — every REFHI and
every REFLO is followed by a PAIR, on 155 objs.

`scripts/gt_dump.py:RELOC` says:

| word | `gt_dump.py` | MS PE/COFF rev 6.0 §5.2.6 | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| `0x000A` | `SECTION` | `ADDR32NB` | `0x0A = SECTION` is the **i386** table |
| `0x000B` | `SECREL_` | `SECREL` | right value, hedged name |
| `0x000C` | *absent* | `SECTION` | prints raw `0xc` |
| `0x0013` | `SECREL` | `SECRELLO` | wrong |

`0x000C` is **not hypothetical**: it is half of every `.debug$S` relocation on a
`/Z7` capture, so `gt_dump.py` prints `0xc` there today for anyone who captures
debug info.

**And this one does not rest on my transcribed name table.** The structural
evidence is independent of every table: in `CritSec_z7.obj`'s `.debug$S`,
`0x000B` and `0x000C` occur in **matched pairs four bytes apart, 18 of each**,
and 18 is exactly `6 S_GPROC32 + 2 S_GDATA32 + 4 S_LABEL32 + 6 Lines
subsections` — each of which carries one `(section-relative offset, section
index)` pair, and nothing else in the section does. LLVM's `--codeview` resolves
the first of each pair into `CodeOffset: ??0CriticalSection@@QAA@XZ+0x0` and
reads the second as `Segment`. Section-relative offset then section index is
`SECREL` then `SECTION`, from the record layout alone.

## 7. Corrections to the brief this lane was given

1. **"Build LLVM" was the expensive route to a capability we already had.** The
   brief read PRIOR_ART's "patch a scratch copy" as patching LLVM. Patching the
   *obj* — two bytes — gets the entire structural decode, the full CodeView
   decode, and correct big-endian disassembly (with `--triple`) out of the
   stock distro LLVM, at zero cost. The build is worth its 190 s for
   ergonomics, and it is committed; but the brief's framing would have led a
   slower lane to spend hours before discovering the decode was already
   available. The lane's premise — *we have no independent decoder* — was
   right; the estimated cost of getting one was wrong by roughly the whole
   budget.
2. **The rung doc is `_2026-08-04-w-llvm.md`, with the underscore.** The brief
   asked for `docs/rungs/2026-08-04-w-llvm.md`. `docs/rungs/README.md` and
   `crates/c2-harness/tests/rung_registry.rs` require every non-underscore file
   to declare at least one existing fixture and a unique `W`-tag, and to be
   present in the generated `INDEX.md`. This lane ships no fixture and no
   codegen. A non-underscore filename would have broken the portable lane and
   forced a regeneration of `INDEX.md` in a seam other lanes are editing.
   `_2026-08-04-w-skip-findings.md`, landed earlier today, is the precedent.
3. **`tools/coffdump.py` already existed and is a fourth reader** — the brief
   named `crates/c2-obj` and `scripts/gt_dump.py`. It is in this lane's own
   seam, it is the most complete of the three, and it is the one LLVM was
   compared against most heavily.
4. **The brief's "which one is right, and how you know" cannot be answered where
   they agree** — and the honest form of the answer is the field/obj/instance
   counts, which are given, plus the `--selftest` that shows the instrument can
   still fail.

## 8. Reproducing this

```sh
tools/llvm/build.sh                       # optional; 190 s
export C2RS_LLVM_BIN=$HOME/build/llvm-w-llvm/build-ppcbe/bin   # or omit entirely

work/w-frame/refobj.sh src/system/rndobj/Shader.cpp work/w-llvm/objs/Shader.obj
tools/llvm/xcheck.py work/w-llvm/objs/*.obj
tools/llvm/xcheck.py --selftest work/w-llvm/objs/*.obj

```

With no LLVM anywhere, every one of these prints a `SKIP:` line and exits 0 —
verified with an emptied `PATH`. `tools/llvm/README.md` has the environment
table.
