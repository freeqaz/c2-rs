### §1.3 The repair, and three decisions that are not in the sketch

`revert` un-applies every substitution from the working copy **in memory**,
compares the result with what `git checkout --` would restore, and refuses when
anything is left over. `--force` reverts anyway and names what it destroys
first.

1. **It compares against the INDEX, not `HEAD`.** #1380's sketch says `HEAD`.
   `git checkout -- <path>` restores from the **index**, so the index is exactly
   what survives and the working copy's excess is exactly what dies. With a
   staged non-hatch change, `HEAD` would refuse a revert that destroys nothing —
   a false positive, and **`w-one`'s `PARTIAL`-on-a-correct-tree is the recorded
   price of exactly that over-tightening** (#1355's own TAKE column). On a tree
   with nothing staged the two are identical, which is the tree #1380 was
   demonstrated on. An index that differs from `HEAD` is **reported**, never
   refused on.
2. **The comparison is on BYTES.** Reading through `text=True` applies universal
   newline translation, which would hide a CRLF difference — a difference
   `git checkout --` destroys as happily as any other.
3. **A POSTCONDITION after the destructive step.** `HATCH-RESIDUE` fires when
   the six files are not clean afterwards. A count, not a status (STATUS.md trap
   5).

### §1.4 The eight words, and why there are eight and not one

`apply` had **one** message, `HATCH FAILED — NOTHING WAS WRITTEN`, covering two
different defects: a needle that drifted, and a `RETIRED` entry whose
`paid_witness` vanished. Those are opposite facts — *the clause moved* versus
*the record of it being paid is gone* — and one string cannot be a red test for
both. They are split.

| word | in | fires when |
|---|---|---|
| `HATCH-DIRTY` | `revert` | the un-applied working copy differs from the index |
| `HATCH-UNREADABLE` | `revert` | a file in `FILES` cannot be read |
| `HATCH-UNTRACKED` | `revert` | a file has no stage-0 index entry to restore from |
| `HATCH-CHECKOUT-FAILED` | `revert` | `git checkout --` itself exited non-zero |
| `HATCH-RESIDUE` | `revert` | the files are still dirty after the checkout |
| `HATCH-FORCED` | `revert --force` | the escape hatch, naming what it destroys |
| `HATCH-DRIFT` | `apply` | a needle matched ≠ 1 times |
| `HATCH-PAID-MISSING` | `apply` | a `RETIRED` entry's `paid_witness` is absent |

### §1.5 The red harness, and the two traps it forecloses

`work/w-hatch/hatch_red.py`, **11 arms — 9 red, 2 green**. Verbatim output in
`work/w-hatch/hatch_red.txt`.

**Trap A — an early guard makes a later assertion unreachable.** Every `DIRTY`
arm is produced by writing a **genuine foreign edit into a real `crates/` file**,
never by breaking the un-apply machinery, so the quantities the earlier gates
read are held fixed and only the gate under test moves. The two guards that
stand *after* the destructive step are structurally unreachable on a well-formed
tree; they are fired through **one declared seam** (`hatch.py::_checkout`) and
are printed as **`[INJECTED]`**. A guard fired through a seam is weaker evidence
than one fired by a real defect, and the report says which is which rather than
letting the reader assume.

**Trap B — a shared prefix lets a later gate's refusal satisfy an earlier
case's expectation.** Three things, and the third is the one that catches it:

1. every refusal leads with its own word;
2. the harness asserts on the **leading whitespace-token of a line**, never on a
   substring anywhere in the output;
3. **every red arm also asserts that every other arm's word is ABSENT**, unless
   the co-occurrence is declared in that arm's own `also` set (only `F1 FORCE`
   declares one). A collapse back to a shared prefix fails **every** arm at once
   instead of silently passing two of them.

# item 1 — the guard, fired on purpose
python3 work/w-hatch/hatch_red.py                    # 11 arms, exits 0

`hatch_red.py` refuses to start on a dirty `crates/`, because every arm writes
to `crates/` and restores it — a pre-existing edit would be destroyed by the
very script written to stop that. It restores in a `finally` and fails the run
if the final diff is non-empty.
