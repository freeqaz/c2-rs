**HOW it went wrong — and it is none of the three usual suspects.** Not a
transcription slip, not an off-by-one against a table base, not a reading taken
from the wrong function. It is **two failures stacked**:

1. **A circular premise.** §1's table *names* `0x10b189b8` a "strictness flip";
   §2's constraint 4 then *uses that name as a premise* to fix the assignment.
   Three of the four constraints are satisfied by **both** candidate
   assignments; only constraint 4 discriminates, and it discriminates by
   assuming the answer. Both readings of `b8` are involutions fixing `{1,2}`, so
   the tables alone cannot separate them.
2. **A cross-namespace coincidence counted as confirmation.** Constraint 4 ends
   *"and lands it exactly on the IL's own `0x1F`..`0x24` order"* — but the IL
   opcode is a **different namespace**, and an assignment chosen to make the map
   a subtraction will land on that order **by construction**. The agreement was
   manufactured by the choice, then read back as independent evidence for it.

**Amended beside, never edited** — the `#3495`-on-`#3468` convention.
`WB_RELATION_FINDINGS.md` and `../rungs/2026-08-24-w-c7.md` keep their original
text; three banners were **inserted** into the findings file (at §1's table row,
after the §2 heading, and at §5) so a reader arriving at any of the three cannot
miss the correction. No original sentence was altered.
