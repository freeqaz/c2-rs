## 2. The one that matters: the encoder drives relocations

Registered: *"**Relocations are not emitted by `FUN_10bf9f15`.** The encoder
produces a word and nothing else"* (P4.3, and it was registered because
`WB_MIDDLE_INTERFACES.md` §5.6 says the relocation half of the emit seam is
**0 cells** and `READ_PLAN` §1 lists it as UNREAD).

**Refuted.** The encoder takes a fourth argument — a relocation sink at
`[ebp+0xc]` — and seven helper paths issue `REL24`, `IFGLUE`, `REFHI`+`PAIR`,
`REFLO`+`PAIR`, `SECREL16` and `ADDR32` through one indirect vector
`ds:0x10c433f8`. Every path is guarded by `cmp [ebp+0xc],0`; with a NULL sink
the word is byte-identical and nothing is requested. Board **#3377**;
`P_ENCODE.md` §6.

**Why nobody had it, which is the transferable part.** `WB_MIDDLE_INTERFACES.md`
§5.6 is exemplary about its own limit: *"`mvp_add3` and `mvp_two` have **zero**
`.text` relocations, so this lane observed the relocation/label half of the emit
seam **not at all**."* Both fixtures are frameless leaf functions with no
external symbol, so **no fixture in that lane could reach any of the seven
paths** — each needs a non-NULL sink *and* an external symbol. The absence was
a property of the fixture set. It sat in the tree for a day as a property of
the compiler, and the read-plan then ranked the thing as unread while the
answer was inside the function the plan was pointing at.

That is `docs/whitebox/ref/README.md` §2's *second* failure mode — "a claim can
be obj-checked, reproduce perfectly, and still be wrong as stated, because the
fixture lacked the structure that would expose the gap" — live, on the item the
read-plan ranked second of nine.

## 3. Three corrections to live documents

Each names the address, and none is a rewrite of a dated record.

1. **`READ_PLAN_2026-08-21.md` §4** asked for default-routed opcodes to be
   marked *"not encodable by this path"*. The default **is** an encoding — the
   base word with no fields, correct for 24 operand-free instructions. The
   refusal is `0x10bfa81d` → ICE line 985, 19 opcodes. A spec written to the
   plan's shape would have refused 24 instructions c2 encodes fine and silently
   accepted 19 it ICEs on. Board **#3378**.
2. **The survey's tail measurement counted two targets, not the tail.** *"3
   call sites of `0x10bf983a` + 1 of `0x10bf98ec`"* is a true statement about
   those two symbols; the body has **15 call sites over 13 distinct targets
   plus one indirect**. The survey's actual claim — that
   `WB_MIDDLE_INTERFACES.md:644`'s "unbounded" is wrong — **stands and is
   strengthened**: the tail is 13 short named functions on one page of the
   image. Reported as a dispatch-brief inaccuracy because the brief carried
   the "4 call sites" figure verbatim.
3. **`WB_MIDDLE_INTERFACES.md` §3.3**'s *"`+0xa` is a SIZE, not a condition
   code"* is `[O]` and is not contradicted, but the wording is too strong:
   `t+0xa` is a packed 16-bit field whose **low 5 bits are the condition code**
   fed to `0x10bf983a` on branch tuples, and three arms read it three ways.

## 4. The controls, and the one that only a big corpus could run

**Control A — two independent derivations of the same constants.**
`crates/c2-core/src/codegen/encode.rs` is a black-box re-derivation of exactly
what `0x10c3a578` states plainly (`READ_PLAN` §2 says so, and the file's own
2026-08-22 banner says so). Every `encode_*` evaluated at all-zero operands
against `base_word[op]`: **82 of 89 identical**, **0** disagreements in a
primary opcode or an `XO`, 7 residuals all explained by a field the port bakes
that c2's arm supplies. It **went green**, which is the weaker outcome, and it
is reported because it was registered.

**Control B — the mask test, built to fail.** Each arm rule became a claim
about *which bits the arm owns*; the test asks whether
`word & ~armmask(form[op]) == base_word[op]` for some opcode, over every
non-padding executable word of 500 real `dc3-decomp` objs.

| reading | explained of 634,457 |
|---|---|
| **as read** | **99.3839 %** |
| `D` field 16 → 12 bits | 91.40 % |
| `RB` field 5 → 4 bits | 92.32 % |
| drop the `RA` field | 73.49 % |
| `SPR` unsplit | 95.66 % |

**The `RB` row is the finding.** On the 46-word purpose-built probe — which
was specified in the prereg *before it was built* to contain a non-zero
displacement, a set sign bit, a `.text` relocation and an `Rc`/`LK` bit,
precisely so it could not be a control that cannot fail — the `RB` mutation
changed **nothing**. No word in it uses a register ≥ 16, so 4-bit and 5-bit
`RB` are the same hypothesis there. Four named failure modes were not enough
to catch a fifth nobody named. A 46-word 100 % would have been quotable and
would have been worth less than it looked.

**And the limit of Control B, stated here rather than discovered later:** it
confirms which bits an arm owns, never which operand it read them from. A rule
with the right bits from the wrong operand passes every cell. That is why
`P_ENCODE.md` §5 stays `[R]` on the operand axis, and why closing it is
read R5's job.

## 5. What this does NOT license

* **It does not re-price I2.** A read produces a spec, not an implementation
  (`DECISIONS_2026-08-22.md` decision 1's own warning). `P_ENCODE.md` §9 lists
  six things I2 needs that this does not contain, first among them the tuple
  stream — read **R5**, 189 arms, unstarted. "The encoder is read" ⇒ "I2 is
  cheaper" is **#1767**'s error with a bigger denominator.
* **It does not decide decision 0.** "Funded for option 4" funded the reads
  before the choice; the 4a / characterization-only / Phase-0 branch stays
  open until R1–R3 all report.
* **It does not adopt anything into `crates/`.** No `DISCLOSURE.md` row is
  owed. `encode.rs` is unchanged, including the seven residuals of §4 — they
  are differences in *where a constant lives*, not in what c2 emits, and the
  port's words are the ones its fixtures grade.
* **It is not a complete emit seam.** §2's relocation cells are the *request*
  side. The sink `ds:0x10c433f8`, the record layout, and label placement are
  all still unread.
