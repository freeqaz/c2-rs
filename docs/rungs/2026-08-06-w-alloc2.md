### 2.1 R2 is the row worth reading, and it is w-next's R5 in mirror image

R2 predicted the right outcome and **named the wrong partition**, which is
exactly what w-next's R5 did one lane earlier. I registered the three-producer
grid as the place the key would break, on the argument that `alloc.rs`'s
preregistered exhaustive search proves the pure-run rule is not a priority
function and that only three producers can expose the tie tier. **F5 came back
14 of 14 clean.** The key broke somewhere I had registered as a *routine*
partition — F4, "other register-derived ops" — which I built to widen coverage
rather than to kill anything.

The general lesson is the one this project keeps paying for: **the reason a
fitted rule dies is usually not the reason you would defend it against.** R2a is
recorded as a MISS rather than folded into R2's PARTIAL, because the mechanism
is the part a next lane would act on.

## 3. The fresh holdout — 60 cells, 56 graded, 7 misses

`work/w-alloc2/freshgrid.py`, every cell compiled at the workload's own flags
through `work/w-frame/refobj.sh` and graded against real `c2.dll`. The partitions
were declared in `work/w-alloc2/PREREG.md` §1 before the file existed.

```text
  selected 60 | reached 60 | GRADED 56 | hit 49 | MISS 7 | out-of-regime 4
    F0   2 / 0 / 0     F1  12 / 0 / 0     F2   8 / 0 / 0     F3   4 / 0 / 2
    F4   5 / 7 / 0     F5  14 / 0 / 2     F6   4 / 0 / 0
```

**`reached` and `graded` are printed as separate counters** and an ungraded cell
is never scored as a pass (STATUS trap 5).

### 3.1 The seven, and why they are not a modelling failure

```text
  F0-g1x1        li 10,7  · addi 11,3,64   ->  addi takes r11   (the fitted spelling)
  F4-add-r1k1    li 11,7  · add  10,4,5    ->  li   takes r11   **MISS**
```

Two bodies with the same store targets, the same use counts (1 and 1), the same
source order and the **same emitted instruction order**. Only the producer's
spelling differs, and the registers swap. Reproduced for `add rX,4,5` (`u+v`),
`addi rX,4,5` (`u+5`) and `slwi rX,4,3` (`u<<3`), at 1-vs-1 and 1-vs-2 — and the
shift loses at **2 uses against 1**, which breaks clause **1** as well.

> ### The emitted ORDER is identical in the deciding pair, so this is `codegen::alloc` and not `codegen::order`. That was checked on the raw disassembly, because the two axes are separable and w-next's §4.1 is explicit that they are different inputs.

**All 24 of w-next's cells spell the producer `(int)&q`.** The refutation is
therefore about the *population*, not the key: the grid varied use counts across
`1..4 × 1..4` and never varied the one thing that mattered. It is the
single-cell trap raised one level — not one cell but **one axis** — and it is the
eighth recorded instance.

### 3.2 What DID survive, and it is worth stating

F1 (use counts 5 and 6, outside the fitted `1..4`) is 12/12. F2 — the reg stores
moved **first** in source, at and around the key tie — is 8/8, so R3 holds and
the bonus is not a disguised source-order effect. F5 (three producers, a full
`r11`/`r10`/`r9` ranking) is 14/14. F6 (pool floor) is 4/4. **Within the fitted
spelling the key is exact on 38 fresh cells**, which is why §4 asks what the
spelling is doing rather than discarding the key wholesale.

### 3.3 R4 came out weaker than registered

R4 predicted a two-word `lis`/`ori` constant beside another producer would be
**out of regime** (#644's split). Two of six were; **four graded cleanly and
hit**. So the split is real but does not fire at every use count, and the
registered claim was too strong. Recorded rather than reworded.

## 4. What the bonus actually attaches to — and it is not shipped

`work/w-alloc2/opgrid.py`, 21 cells, 21 graded, every cell at the decisive point
(`reg 1 use vs const 1..N`) where clause 1 ties and the bonus alone decides.
Each rival is killed by a cell that varies **only** it:

| rival | verdict | killed by |
|---|---|---|
| derived from **r3** (the first parameter) | KILLED | 8 cells |
| derived from the run's **store base** | KILLED | 5 cells |
| **pointer-typed** | KILLED | 6 cells |
| the **opcode** (`addi` with a displacement) | KILLED | 8 cells |
| **H-self** — the value is stored **into the object it points at** | **SURVIVES** | — |

The decisive pair: `&s->inner` stored into `s->inner.a0` takes `r11`; the *same
producer instruction*, stored into `s->f1` instead, loses to the constant. And
`&t->inner` off a **second** pointer, stored into `t->inner.a0`, takes `r11` —
so it is not about `r3` and not about the store base.

The bonus is a **magnitude**, not an override: the producer wins at 1-vs-1 and
1-vs-2 and **loses** at 1-vs-3 and 1-vs-4, which is w-next's 1.5 uses confirmed
on fresh cells.

### 4.1 The displacement confound, which nearly took the lane

In every bonus-carrying cell above, the winner's store also had the **higher**
displacement (32 against 0); in every losing cell the lower (4 against 0). So
H-self was indistinguishable from *"the producer whose store reaches further into
the object wins"*. `work/w-alloc2/offprobe.py` sweeps that offset over
4, 8, 16, 32, 44, 64, 128, 252 with everything else pinned: **16 of 16
constant**, for both an `add` and a `slwi` producer. `selfgrid.py` then lays the
inner object at a **low** offset so the two axes vary independently —
**10 of 10, four of them discriminating, H-self clean and displacement killed on
five**. Board **#838**.

### 4.2 The axis nobody has controlled — and it is in w-next's grid too

`freshgrid` and `opgrid` disagreed on one cell shape with identical use counts,
source order, formals and producer spelling. `work/w-alloc2/bisect.py` removes
the four remaining differences one at a time:

```text
  B1-no-second-ref   li slwi stw stw stw   const takes r11
  B2-no-ref          slwi li stw stw stw   prod  takes r11   <== FLIPS HERE
```

**The C++ reference binding `L& q = s->inner;` moves both the schedule and the
allocation.** `w-next`'s `gapgrid.py` and `allocgrid.py` declare it in **all 24**
cells and neither varies it; this lane's `freshgrid` inherited it. Board **#839**.

**§3's refutation does not depend on it** — `B-notself` and five `selfgrid` cells
hold the binding fixed and still separate — but H-self's one miss is exactly
there, which is why H-self is a candidate and not a rule.

### 4.3 Two instrument defects, both #644, both recorded

* A matcher keyed on `rlwinm` scored **four real cells OUT OF REGIME** because
  c2 prints the extended mnemonic `slwi`. Three of the four were misses: the
  instrument said *"I could not see it"* where the answer was on the line.
* A single-line matcher graded `(int)&q + 4` as a clean producer. c2 emits it as
  **`addi 11,3,8` … `addi 11,11,4` with the `li` between the halves** — a
  two-instruction producer, board **#644** — and the grader matched the first
  half, scoring the cell as a counterexample to H-self. The fix is a
  **definition count**: the register a producer is read out of must be written
  exactly once, or the cell is out of regime.

Board **#843**. Both are STATUS trap 5 inside a grid rather than inside a test
runner, and both were caught by a number being implausible rather than by a
status.

only on the supplementary probe, never on the held-out partition, so it is not
shipped"*. That understates it twice over:

1. **Clause 2 is refuted where it would apply.** `B-notself-1v1` is a
   register-derived producer at 1 use **losing** a tie to a constant at 1 use,

## 6. `xboxheap` re-priced at 16 — and the price method was wrong in the other direction

Full derivation in `work/w-alloc2/PRICE.md`. The IL chain was **re-walked**, not
copied, and reproduces w-next's five steps exactly (four opcode steps then the
composite terminal `expr-call-in-expr-data-addr-then-off-add-and-chain-bind-more`,
whose `-more` suffix makes the IL side a **lower bound**).

w-next's diagnosis was that *"a price derived from an obj is systematically blind
to facts the obj states as answers."* That is right, and it is **half** of it:

> ### An obj also states facts the port has **already paid for**. The `.pdata` unwind word `40 00 14 04`, the `$M2595`/`$M2596`/`$T2597` labels and the 96-byte frame are stated just as loudly as `li 10,0` — and `coff::pdata`, `coff::label` and `codegen::frame` already emit all three for every framed non-leaf the fixture gate matches. **The step neither price took is: for each fact the obj states, go read whether a `crates/` module already produces it.**

This lane made that error **in its own prereg**: three of the five rows R5
registered as new were already in w-next's fourteen (the callee-saved `r31`
choice was w-next's row 12, "and *which* one"; the producer kind was row 9; the
return-`this` epilogue was row 14). Reading the obj alone **under**-counts;
assuming every stated fact is unpaid **over**-counts. Board **#842**.

**16 = 6 IL + 8 emit-shape + 2 that w-next's fourteen did not contain**, the two
being the store-run producer allocation (w-next's own stated blind spot) and
#844 below.

### 6.1 The next stopper, named the way w-next named its own

> ### **`codegen::leaf::store` is leaf-only by construction, and `xboxheap`'s store run is in a `calls-1` framed body.**
>
> `scheduled_gpr_run_text` is the **only** place under `crates/` that consults
> `order::schedule` and `alloc::allocate`. It is reached only from
> `store_leaf_text`; `select_function` dispatches that as a **whole-body** shape;
> and it terminates by appending `encode_blr()` unconditionally
> (`leaf/store.rs:356`). `xboxheap`'s run sits between a `mr 31,3` and a `bl`.
> **There is no seam that emits a scheduled store run as the MIDDLE of a framed
> body** — the run and the frame are two emitters that do not compose. Board
> **#844**.

This **corrects which wall binds**, and that correction is the part a next lane
would act on. w-next left `codegen::alloc`'s mixed refusal as the stopper.
`xboxheap`'s run is `addi r11,3,8` at **2** uses beside `li r10,0` at **1**, so
**clause 1 alone settles its allocation**; H-self pushes the same way; the answer
was never in doubt. What blocks is the *blanket* refusal, which §3–§5 now show is
**right in general** — and behind it stands #844, which no relaxation of a gate
reaches.

#844 sharpens #835 from a statement about the parser into one about the emitter.

## 8. What this lane did NOT do

* **Did not convert a TU.** TU match is 10 at both ends.
* **Did not ship the key.** It was refuted on fresh cells and the standing
  instruction is that the miss is the deliverable.
* **Did not ship H-self either**, and that is the harder call. It is exact on 80
  of 81 and it is **fitted on the cells that produced it** — precisely where
  w-next's key stood before this lane killed it. Shipping a rule fitted on its
  own grid is what P3 did (`floor((N−1)/2)`, three published cells, dead at
  N = 5) and what #836 has now done again.
* **Did not change one emitted byte**, and proved it with a scan rather than an
  argument — including under a deliberately wrong rule (§5.1).
* **Did not re-derive the `.pdata`, label-counter or frame models** it found were
  already paid; it read them (§6).
* **Did not resolve #839.** The reference-binding axis is measured, isolated to
  one bisection step, and left open.

## 9. Found and not taken — the next lane's rung, bounded

1. **#839, the reference binding.** The smallest unexplained residual this lane
   produced, it holds H-self's only miss, and it has a two-cell bisection already
   (`B1`/`B2`). It contaminates **every** ALLOC and ORDER grid on record, so
   pricing it is worth more than one rule.
2. **#844, the framed store run.** The real stopper for `xboxheap` and a
   composition problem rather than a gate: the scheduled run and the frame are
   two whole-body emitters.
3. **H-self needs a fresh holdout of its own** — a grid that varies the
   consumption pattern, not the one that produced it. `opgrid`/`selfgrid` are its
   fit set.
4. **`slwi` may be a third regime.** At 2 uses against a 1-use constant it wins
   without the binding present and loses with it. Only #839 separates those.
