## 2. `B∧C` = 151 over 871 graded TUs

    PHASE 7 FACTORS … over 871 graded TUs
      A  emit set reachable   `.ex` segments == obj `.text` COMDATs      28  (27 LO-anchored)
      B  binding complete     every emitted symbol binds                338
      C  section shape        obj sections subset of the writer's 10     169
      D  per-fn acceptance    every emitted COMDAT in class               8
      E  whole-TU acceptance  a REGISTERED whole-TU recognizer takes it   2
      B and C jointly (the near-term ceiling, measured per TU — NOT a product of
      marginals, §8.6): 151
      A and B and C: 27 | A∧B∧C∧D (refuted): 6 | A∧B∧C∧(D∨E): 8 | match: 8

**151, measured per TU.** `gap.rs` has computed this on every scan since the
factorization landed; the figure the docs carried, **107**, was simply the last
one anybody copied out, and it was copied at `C = 114`. Nothing was recomputed
when the writer's vocabulary grew.

**Why 107 could not have been scaled.** `C = {TU : sections(TU) ⊆
PORT_WRITER_SECTIONS}` and the vocabulary only ever grew, so `C_new ⊇ C_old`
and `B∧C` is monotone: the true answer was **forced into `[107, 169]`** and
*any* number in that range would have been consistent with a naive
extrapolation. A product of marginals would have said `338/871 × 169 ≈ 66`,
which is not merely wrong but on the wrong side of the old figure — §8.6's
standing rule, demonstrated.

**The interesting quantity is the marginal.** C gained **55** TUs from w-r1c's
and w-sect's writer work; `B∧C` gained **44** of them. So **80 % of the
vocabulary's marginal TUs were already binding-complete**, and the inclusion
rate `B∧C / C` fell from `107/114 = 93.9 %` to `151/169 = 89.3 %` — the
predicted direction and very close to the predicted magnitude (§7).

## 3. What a perfect emit predicate is actually worth: **+124**, and it went UP

Board **#213** is the row that carries this. It states the value of a perfect
factor A twice, and published **one** number for both:

> *"perfect emit-set modelling (A true on all 871) takes `A∧B∧C` **25 → 107 =
> |B∧C|** and the FRONTIER **17 → 99**: **+82 TUs that become REACHABLE BY
> CODEGEN**"*

Re-measured on this tree:

| the projection, stated as… | published | **measured `c303ad0`** |
|---|---|---|
| **reach**: `B∧C` − `A∧B∧C` | 107 − 25 = **+82** | 151 − 27 = **+124** |
| **frontier**: frontier-if-A − FRONTIER | 99 − 17 = **+82** | 141 − 19 = **+122** |

**Direction: UP.** From **+82** to **+124** (reach) — an increase of 42, and a
1.51× increase in the size of the prize. This is the answer to the lane's
question and it is the *less* useful of its two answers, because a projection
that grew is a projection the project was under-quoting, which is the safe
direction to be wrong in.

### 3.1 The refutation: the two statements are not one number

**They differ by 2 on this tree.** They coincided when #213 was written, and the
row treats that coincidence as an identity. It is not one. The two agree exactly
when no TU inside `B∧C` fails A while *already* having an accepted route through
D or E. Two TUs now do:

    THE TWO DISAGREE by 2 — #213's single `+82` is refuted on this tree. The
    difference is exactly the TU(s) inside B and C that fail A and that the port
    ALREADY accepts (D or E): ["src/system/decomp_pch.cpp", "src/system/math/vec.cpp"]

Read plainly: a perfect emit-set model makes **124** more TUs *reachable*, but
only **122** of them are additions to the **codegen frontier** — two of the 124
are TUs the port's accepted class already covers, so modelling the emit set
would move them without any codegen at all. `+82` conflated "reachable" with
"on the codegen frontier"; the conflation was invisible while the two counts
were equal, and it is visible now.

**This is why the lane derived the projection in code rather than publishing its
halves.** `emit-predicate-worth` and `frontier-if-a` are both emitted by
`gap.rs` now (§5), and a unit test asserts on a fixture where the two
arithmetics deliberately disagree — so the identity cannot be silently
re-assumed.

### 3.2 The noun is still "reachability", not "conversions"

#213 already corrected its own title on this point and the correction stands:
**a perfect factor A converts zero TUs by itself.** Every one of the 124 is
still gated on codegen that does not exist. **TU match is 8 and this lane did
not move it, did not try to, and does not predict that it will move.**

## 4. The greedy ladder, in full, and why the whole ladder is the deliverable

    GREEDY LADDER — next section name to teach the writer, and the resulting C
    (3 steps from 169 to 871):
      +.rdata$r     C =   590   (+421)
      +.text$yd     C =   804   (+214)
      +.xdata$x     C =    871   (+67)

Three steps. The head is **`.rdata$r`** at **+421**, and it is the only row
anybody should act on today.

**Each step invalidates the ranking below it.** The ladder is greedy: it picks
the name with the largest immediate gain, then re-scores every remaining name
against the *new* vocabulary. So `.text$yd`'s `+214` is its gain **given that
`.rdata$r` has already been taught**, and if a lane taught `.text$yd` first it
would realize a different number and the rest of the ladder would re-rank
underneath it. **Publishing only the head would be publishing a schedule**; the
whole ladder is published so that a reader who deviates from step one knows the
remaining rows are void, not merely approximate.

The history makes the point concretely: this ladder was **seven** steps at
`C = 84`, **four** at `C = 114`, and is **three** at `C = 169` — and the step
sizes below the head are not comparable across any of those changes. The
`+421` head is stable across two independent trees today (`33cbdbe` and
`c303ad0`), which is the reproduction check, not a guarantee it survives the
next writer rung.

**Greedy is not proven optimal.** What the ladder establishes is an *upper
bound* on the length of the route to `C = 871`, which is the claim §10.19 makes
and the only claim it supports.

Section vocabulary confirming the ladder's inputs — **13 names, writer emits
10**, both re-measured:

| objs | in writer | name |
|---:|---|---|
| 676 | — | `.rdata$r` |
| 243 | — | `.text$yd` |
| 67 | — | `.xdata$x` |

(The other ten are all in `PORT_WRITER_SECTIONS`.) **`.rdata$r`'s 676 objs are
not `.rdata$r`'s 421 TUs of ladder gain**, and the gap is the point of measuring
the ladder rather than reading the vocabulary census: a TU carrying `.rdata$r`
enters C only once *every* name it carries is writable.

## 5. The durable fix: `gap.rs` now emits a machine-readable block

**The root cause w-book4 identified is correct and this lane confirms it.**
Factor C, `A∧B∧C` and the FRONTIER are printed by every scan and are still
*hand-copied* into `STATUS.md` and `BOARD.md`, because `scripts/status.sh`'s
collector has `sed` recipes for only the six `TuClass` counters. `B∧C` was the
worst case of the class: published once, then **silently invalidated by a
dependency moving**, with nothing in the pipeline that could notice.

`gap.rs` now prints, at the end of the factorization block:

     GAP-METRICS — stable `key value` pairs for scripts/status.sh; keys are an
     interface, do not rename. …
       gap-metric tu-total 878
       gap-metric graded 871
       gap-metric match 8
       gap-metric mismatch 0
       gap-metric codegen-gap 0
       gap-metric vocab-gap 863
       gap-metric port-error 0
       gap-metric capture-fail 7
       gap-metric factor-a 28
       gap-metric factor-a-lo 27
       gap-metric factor-b 338
       gap-metric factor-c 169
       gap-metric factor-d 8
       gap-metric factor-e 2
       gap-metric b-and-c 151
       gap-metric a-and-b-and-c 27
       gap-metric a-and-b-and-c-and-d 6
       gap-metric a-and-b-and-c-and-d-or-e 8
       gap-metric frontier 19
       gap-metric frontier-if-a 141
       gap-metric emit-predicate-worth 124
       gap-metric writer-sections 10
       gap-metric workload-sections 13
       gap-metric ladder-steps 3
       gap-metric ladder-head .rdata$r
       gap-metric ladder-head-c 590

Three design decisions, each with a reason that is a recorded failure here:

1. **A `key value` text line, not an extension of `--jsonl`.** `--jsonl` already
   carries a provenance record 0 plus per-TU rows, and a summary record there
   would need a JSON parser the collector does not have — `status.sh` is `sed`
   over a log. A `jsonl` summary record remains a reasonable *addition* later;
   it is not a substitute, because the metric has to be readable from the same
   log the collector already captures.
2. **Derived quantities are derived in code.** `emit-predicate-worth` is
   `b-and-c` − `a-and-b-and-c`, computed in `GapReport::metrics`, precisely
   because publishing two halves and letting a reader subtract is *how `+82`
   survived both of its inputs moving.*
3. **A closed ladder omits `ladder-head` rather than publishing `0`.** A
   collector that reads a missing key as `0` would announce a ladder reaching
   `C = 0`. That is trap 5 with the mask on, and there is an assertion for it.

### 5.1 The exact change `scripts/status.sh` needs — NOT made here

`status.sh` belongs to another live lane. The change is small and mechanical.
`collect_gap` already tees the scan to `$work_dir/gap.log` and parses it with
helpers of the form

    p_vocab() { sed -n 's/^ *vocab-gap  *\([0-9][0-9]*\).*/\1/p' "$1" | head -1; }

**Add one generic helper and the rows that use it:**

    p_metric() { sed -n "s/^ *gap-metric $2 \\(.*\\)\$/\\1/p" "$1" | head -1; }

and then, inside `collect_gap`, four new `emit` calls beside the existing
`emit workload …` — the four figures that went stale, plus the projection:

| new metric key | value string to emit |
|---|---|
| `factors` | `A <factor-a>, B <factor-b>, C <factor-c>, D <factor-d>, E <factor-e>, over <graded> graded TUs` |
| `joint_ceilings` | `B∧C <b-and-c>, A∧B∧C <a-and-b-and-c>, A∧B∧C∧(D∨E) <a-and-b-and-c-and-d-or-e>` |
| `frontier` | `<frontier> TUs; <frontier-if-a> if factor A were perfect` |
| `emit_predicate_worth` | `+<emit-predicate-worth> reachable (B∧C − A∧B∧C)` |
| `section_ladder` | `<ladder-steps> steps, head <ladder-head> to C <ladder-head-c> (writer <writer-sections> of <workload-sections> names)` |

Each must go through the existing `val_or_missing` so a renamed key prints
`NO-RESULT` rather than an empty string, and each needs its row added to the
`workload  yes  …` metric manifest near the top of the script so
`status.sh --check` proves the recipe without a toolchain. **The keys are an
interface** — that is stated in the printed header and in the `metrics()` doc
comment, so a future rename is a deliberate act rather than an accident.

### 7.1 What the prediction did NOT cover, and it should have — the refutation

**Nothing in the pre-registration anticipated that the projection is two
different numbers.** The prereg treated `B∧C − A∧B∧C` as *the* projection
because #213 states it as one, and the lane inherited the row's conflation while
setting out to correct the row. The divergence (§3.1) was found only because the
lane chose to compute `frontier-if-a` in code rather than infer it from the
delta — and the reason for computing it was stylistic ("derive, don't
subtract"), not a hypothesis. **The most valuable finding of this lane came from
a discipline rather than from a prediction, and the prediction that should have
been registered — "the two arithmetics still agree" — would have been
REFUTED.** It is recorded here as a miss.

### 7.2 The absence trap fired on this lane

The **first** scan of this lane exited **0** and printed exactly one line:

    SKIP: toolchain absent

A worktree's repo root has no `compilers/`, and the harness degrades cleanly by
design — correctly, and into a run that produced no measurement and returned
success. Had the lane grepped that output for `B and C jointly` and reported
"unchanged", it would have shipped an absence as a confirmation, which is trap
5's exact shape. It was caught because the rung requires the `capture-fail` line
to be quoted and there was no `capture-fail` line to quote. Fixed by passing
`C2RS_COMPILERS` and `C2RS_WIBO` explicitly; every figure in this rung is from a
run that printed `capture-fail 7`.
