## 3. What the arms actually found

### 3.1 The `expr-call-in-expr` family: 17,562 emitted functions relabelled, two recovered

`op:26` is a **head-token** sink and PREREG §3 says so before it ran: it consumes
`26 <tok>`, one token of the `26 … 4C` production. It moved 17,562 emitted
functions across 423 lost keys onto **sixteen** successors —
`expr-convert-no-value-0x2C` **+9,222**, `expr-op-0x99` +2,774, `expr-op-0x27`
+2,251, `expr-intrinsic-this-adjust` +2,154, `expr-op-0xBD` +1,060 — and
recovered **two** (1 poison + 1 `expr-empty-0x32`).

**This is not a refutation of the family**; it is a measurement of the head
token, and the successors name the rest of the member-call spine (`2C` decay,
`99` bind, `BD` call). The family's own `-whole` column, read at baseline, says
the same thing from the other side: of 36,751 emitted, **3,837** carry `-whole`,
813 `-whole2`, 119 `-whole3`, 22 `-whole4`, **10,490** bare and **21,470**
`-more`. And its largest `-whole` row, `…-recv-load-whole` (1,505 emitted /
18,310 bodies), is **already refuted** — ROADMAP §9.14.5 measures 99.5 % of it as
the frame class (792 bail at *body does not end at the call*, 711 at
*framed-result-not-consumed-by-a-literal-post-op*), category (6), *"a row that
looks like the find of the session and is a phase"*.

### 3.2 The intrinsic family splits in two, and only one half is worth anything

The `intrinsic` sink consumes the whole two-token unit

**The `+17,693` is board #1465's rename at the largest scale the project has
measured it, and the sink was one token short.** The 2113–2119 class-layout

With `op:66` added (§5), `expr-class-descriptor` delta goes to **0** and the

populations and in every category — and on a poison-only reading this arm bought
**nothing**. It is `expr-empty-0x55` that moved: **+341 emitted / +2,569
bodies**, and §3.3 is why that key is the same event under a different name. So
the class-layout half's whole recovery is **341 of 18,923 — 1.8 %**, which is
below R1's floor on both clauses (`< 750` **and** `< 5 %`) and declines as a
sub-family in its own right. **17,982 of the 18,323 that moved are a rename
replaced by a rename.**

### 3.3 The proxy is an upper bound, and here is its width

**The poison is not the sink's only terminal, and the second one is bigger than
the difference this lane was measuring** (board **#1538**). `parse_expr_classed`
checks `ops.is_empty()` **before** all three poisons, and the sinks push no
`IlOp` by construction — so a walk that consumed an expression *entirely* through
sunk tokens arrives with `ops` empty and reports **`expr-empty-0xNN`**, not the
poison. Both are the same event: *the sink set was the last thing in the way*.

Measured, not argued: ARM-B2 puts **341 emitted (2,569 bodies)** under
`expr-empty-0x55` and 5,021 (40,210) under the poison, and **the 341 are exactly
the class-layout half's entire recovery** — so a poison-only reading of ARM-B2
reports **0** for a population whose answer is 341, which is §3.2's headline
inverted. Boards #660, #1319, #1455 and #1465 all read the poison alone.
**Read `expr-chain-sink-poison` and `expr-empty` together, or neither.** The
reorder that would make the poison the single terminal is inert with the sinks
off and is filed as §7 item 4 rather than taken here, because it redefines a key
those four rows have published counts against.

Beyond that, the terminal is still an **upper bound on recovery, of unknown
tightness**, exactly as PREREG §4 registered it: it fires where
`parse_expr_classed` would have returned `Ok`, and a body with further statements
behind that expression is still refused while the census reports the *first*
blocker. Two things bound it:

* the poison never renders `:eof`. It is raised at the walk's `stop` byte, which
  is by construction mid-segment, so `Block::completeness` reads
  `PartialSegmentEnd` for every one of them and the suffix carries no
  whole-body signal. Quote `expr-chain-sink-poison:mid`, not a bare spelling.
* the `fn_dispatch` cross says **36,041 of the 40,210** bodies are
  `disp-plain-call` — one discarded call statement, where the expression *is* the
  body and the bound is tight — and **4,169** are `disp-assign`, where it is not.

## 4. Why R2(a) fails, and what it would cost to make it pass

The 5,362 is `memset` ∪ `0xDF` (5,021) plus the class-layout family's 341, and
the 341 declines on R1's own floor before R2 is reached (§3.2). Of the 5,021,
neither half is expressible as a parser acceptance:

* **`memset` (3,749 emitted).** `docs/IL_INTRINSIC_CALL.md`'s standing statement
  is that none of the family can be lowered yet *because the emission depends on
  the literal argument values, not on the id*. Board **#971** prices what #232's
  precedent demands of exactly this widening, and board **#1047** REFUTES its
  premise **in both halves** — the production was already read by `w-inl0`'s
  `shapes/no_effect.rs`, and the brief's 369 is `w-seq`'s count, not a price.
  Board **#1096** closes the family's residue at **nine**.
* **`0xDF` (2,031 emitted).** §3.5: a class-typed result consumed by an enclosing
  argument region. A parser acceptance without a lowering is a wrong emit, which
  is strictly worse than the gap (#232, 241 commits).

Accepting either needs an `IlOp` and a lowering in `crates/c2-core` — a
**codegen-side** production. **#139 forbids splitting it**: acceptance must live
in the IL parser or the census over-claims. So the honest statement is the price,
not a half-rung, and R4 says to publish it.

**What this lane deliberately did not do:** narrow to `memset` alone and ship a
reader for the shape to claim a number. That is R4's exact prohibition, and #1047
is the record of what it costs.

## 5. What DID ship: one width, and a null-lift control that proves it inert

`SkipForm::ClassDescr` and `0x66 => ClassDescr` in `chain_skip_form`.

* **It is not a new width.** The arm calls `mcall::eat_class_descriptor`, the one
  decoder for the token; `shapes/control_flow.rs`'s `0x66` arm already calls the
  same function. This is `0x5C`'s situation (board #1423) exactly — a width the
  tree already knew and this table could not spell — and not `0xBD`'s, which
  needed an enum variant for an *unspellable shape*. It does get one anyway,
  because the arity is a byte and the ids are LEB.
* **It cannot be an acceptance.** The chain sink is `C2RS_SINK_CHAIN`-gated,
  defaults OFF, pushes no `IlOp`, and poisons any walk that used it. This is
  board **#1319**'s row (`w-bd`) one opcode over, with the same evidence.
* **ARM-D is the null-lift control**: `C2RS_SINK_CHAIN=op:66` **alone** moves
  **0 keys and 0 counts** on both populations over 878 TUs. `0x66` is never a
  first blocker by itself, so nothing in ARM-B2 can be attributed to this row
  rather than to the intrinsic sink in front of it.
* **The binary control**: an 878-TU scan with the sink OFF, taken with the *new*
  binary, is **identical to master's** — same `class`, `fn_total`,
  `fn_in_class` and both blocker maps for all 878 TUs, and **199 of 199
  `gap-metric` lines byte-identical under `diff`**.
* `0x66` leaves `the_unpinned_opcodes_refuse_rather_than_guess_a_width` with a
  line naming why, per that test's own convention. `0x64` stays: it is `mcall`'s
  by-value-return materialize and no reader in this tree consumes it at a stated

## 7. Found and not taken

Ranked, with the frame axis applied.

0. **`expr-empty` shadows the sink poison, and the reorder is one line.**
   Moving `ops.is_empty()` below the three poison checks is **inert with every
   sink off** — all three flags are false, so the arm is reached on the identical
   population — and makes the poison the single terminal every future sink can be
   read off. It is not taken here because it redefines a key boards #660, #1319,
   #1455 and #1465 published counts against; that wants its own rung, which
   re-derives those four numbers on both sides. **Until it exists, a lane reading
   `expr-chain-sink-poison` alone is under-counting by an unknown amount** —
   341 emitted of 5,362 here, and 0 of 341 on the sub-family that mattered.
1. **The `expr-call-in-expr` family has never had a whole-production
   counterfactual, and `op:26` is not one.** ARM-A sinks the head token only. A
   sink that consumed `26 … 4C` entire would answer the largest family on the
   board for the first time. It is a real code rung in `mcall.rs` (a bracket
   walk, not a width row), and it is the single highest-value follow-on here.
   **Do not read ARM-A's `1` as that answer.**
2. **`expr-op-0x55` is now the successor of 18,324 emitted functions** under
   ARM-B2, up from ~972. It is already in `chain_skip_form` as `Type`, so it is a
   ladder step away — and #1463/#1465 are why nobody should take that step
   without scoring `stepped` and `load-bearing` separately.
3. **Selector 223 is 2,031 emitted functions with no name.** Naming it is a
   census refinement, not a widening, and §3.5 has enough to start: the result
   TYPE kind, the argument literal, and the STLport population.
4. **The proxy could be made exact.** Deferring the chain poison from
   `parse_expr`'s exit to the body's exit turns an upper bound into a count,
   changes no acceptance, and would let every future sink report `recovered`
   rather than `recovered-proxy`. It changes the meaning of a key other lanes
   have published against, so it wants its own rung and its own re-derivation of
   #660's and #1319's numbers — **not** a quiet edit.
5. **Not taken, and named so nobody re-ranks it**: `expr-intrinsic-this-adjust`
   (9,267 emitted) and `expr-intrinsic-base-member-addr` (6,466). They are the #3
   and #4 keys on the emitted board and their whole family recovers **341 of
   18,923 — 1.8 %**, below R1's floor on both clauses. Board #140's *"Schedule it
   at 434"* is now a row with a counterfactual behind it, and the counterfactual
   reads 341 for a family five times the size of the population #140 was written
   over.
