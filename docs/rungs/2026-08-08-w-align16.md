## §2 `8A`/`CA` measured, against c2's own obj

Two quantities, and confusing them is how this lane nearly filed four false
disagreements. The **tag's width field** is the object's *natural* alignment; the
**obj's `Characteristics` nibble** is the *section's* alignment, which is
`placement_align` of the max over the section's objects (Rule B1). `oracle.py`
applies the port's own model to the first and compares to the second.

```text
tag  masked  reads   c2 nibble  align   cells
82   82      1       1 / 4      1 / 8   char (T12) · char[256] · char[4096]
86   86      4       3          4       {int,int,int,int} at size 16
88   88      8       4          8       {double,double} at size 16
C8   88      8       4          8       __declspec(align(8)) + ctor
CA   8A      16      5          16      __declspec(align(16)), NINE shapes
CC   8C      32      6          32      __declspec(align(32))   -> REFUSED
CE   8E      64      7          64      __declspec(align(64))   -> REFUSED
```

**24 of 24 section nibbles and 25 of 25 symbol `Value`s confirmed, 0
contradicted**, at the workload's own `/nologo /wd4355 /wd4164 /c /GR /O1 /Oi
/EHsc`. Nothing below is inferred from the IL; the alignment is always c2's.

### §2.1 Bare `8A` does not exist, and the port ships that arm anyway (board #1151)

`w-align` saw only `CA` and reasonably attributed it to its two cells both being
`__declspec(align(16))`. **This lane built the shapes most likely to produce the
narrow form and they did not.** A bare scalar `__declspec(align(16)) int g;`
(`A01`) is `CA`. A type that is 16-aligned because a *member* is, with **no
attribute on the outer type** (`A05`), is `CA`. All twelve 16/32/64 cells are
wide, at all four profiles, and §6 finds **0 records** at `8A`, `8C` or `8E` in
the whole workload.

`align_of_type_tag` accepts bare `8A` regardless, because it masks the wide bit
off and reads the field underneath — `w-align`'s orthogonality rule, 21 of 21.
**That arm has zero witnesses and it is labelled as an extrapolation in the
function's own doc comment rather than counted among the 24 confirmations.** The
alternative — both forms at 1/2/4/8, only the wide form at 16 — is an
inconsistency the next reader has to rediscover, and a shape nothing emits cannot
mismatch.

### §2.2 The size-implied clause CAPS AT 8, and that is now measured

`placement_align`'s `implied` term was fitted over `n = 1 … 256`
(`OBJ_DYNINIT_SHAPE.md` §4.2) and its top clause reads naturally as "8 and
rising". **It is not.** `char g[4096]` (`A07`) is tag `82`, natural alignment 1,
and c2 gives its section nibble **4** — ALIGN_8, not 16. `A08` says the same at
256. `placement_align(65536, 1)` is pinned at 8 in the unit test.

**So everything above 8 arrives through the TAG and never through the size.**
That is what makes `w-align`'s trap load-bearing at 16 as well: a widening fitted
on natural *layout* rather than on the tag emits the wrong nibble for exactly the
`__declspec` cells, and here those are nine of the fourteen that convert.

### §2.3 Three adjacent readings, each with the cell that kills it

* **"the tag is the size"** — `A01` is **size 4** at `CA` and `A04` is **size 64**
  at `CA`. `w-align` killed this at 4 with size-8 and size-68 cells; `A01` kills
  it from the other side, an object **smaller** than its own alignment.
* **"a 16-byte object is 16-aligned"** — `A06` is `{int,int,int,int}`, size 16,
  tag `86`, nibble **3**. It is `A02`'s discriminating pair: same size, different
  alignment, and only the tag separates them. `A20` (`{double,double}`, size 16,
  tag `88`, nibble 4) is the third point on that line.
* **"big objects get big alignment"** — `A07` at 4096 bytes is ALIGN_8 (§2.2).

## §3 16 is NOT the ceiling — 32 and 64 exist, and are refused (board #1149)

The prereg registered this as a question, not an assumption, and it was right to.
`__declspec(align(32))` spells **`CC`** and c2 gives nibble **6**; `align(64)`
spells **`CE`** and nibble **7**. Both `Value`s and both section nibbles check.
**So the `0x80 + 2*(log2(size)+1)` encoding and the `log2 + 1` nibble law are
confirmed to 64 in `.gl`** — this refusal is not "we do not know what c2 does".

They are refused because **the grid stops**. At 16 this lane varied structure
nine ways; at 32 and 64 it varied one shape each, and the consumers that would
bite are exactly the ones a single shape cannot exercise — `bump_layout`'s cursor
and `section_nibble`'s max. Extending a table by `log2` to a value three cells
confirm and no cell *constrains* is the "mostly right" table the decline floor
(§8.1 F4) was written against.

`fixtures/cpp/wa16_data_align32.cpp` is the graded refusal, and it **inherits the
guard role from `walign_dyninit_align16.cpp`** — a boundary with no cell on its
far side is a boundary nothing checks, and 16 is no longer the far side. Price to
take it: **one more grid of exactly this shape.** Nothing else.

## §4 THE GRID FOUND A LIVE WRONG EMIT THAT WAS ALREADY ON MASTER (board #1148)

**Cell `A11` graded `mismatch`.** This section is the lane's most valuable
output, and none of it is about alignment.

### §4.1 It is section order, and it is not this lane's

`A11` is `static A g; A* p = &g;` at align 16. The first divergence is at file
offset 101 — inside the **third section header's name**. Alignment agrees
everywhere: `.bss` nibble 5 both sides, `.data` nibble 3 both sides.

```text
   extern:  .drectve .debug$S .XBLD$W(C2) .bss        .XBLD$W(C1) .data   <- Rule S1
   static:  .drectve .debug$S .bss        .XBLD$W(C2) .XBLD$W(C1) .data   <- real c2
```

**c2 moves `.bss` before both watermarks when the section holds an
internal-linkage object.** Post-hoc diagnostic cells (`work/w-align16/diag/`,
their own `SHA256SUMS`, written *after* the mismatch and labelled as such) settle
the provenance in one step:

| cell | shape | at master `bcd0b3be` | at this tip |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`D01`** | `static A g; A* p = &g;` — **align 4** | **`mismatch`** | codegen-gap |
| **`D02`** | the same at **align 8** | **`mismatch`** | codegen-gap |
| `D03` | `A g; A* p = &g;` at align 16 | codegen-gap | match |
| `D05` | the same at align 4 (extern control) | match | match |
| `D06` | `static A g; int q = 5;` | match | match — the static is **dropped**, 5 sections |
| `D07` | `A g; static A h; A* p = &h;` — mixed | — | codegen-gap |

**`D01` and `D02` are at alignments 4 and 8 — inside the incumbent's own modelled
range — and both graded `mismatch` against real c2 on a tree with `crates/`
checked out at `bcd0b3be`.** Wrong bytes, live, under a scan reading
`mismatch 0`: board **#232**'s shape exactly.

### §4.2 Why it had never been seen, and why "unreachable" was almost right

`fixtures/cpp/wsect_drop_static.cpp` records that an uninitialized *unreferenced*
static is dropped by c2 entirely, and `wsect_data_linkage.cpp` concludes from it:

> *"an uninitialized unreferenced static is DROPPED by c2 entirely, so mixed
> linkage is unreachable in a `.bss` of a functionless TU"*

**True of every cell that existed.** `D06` reproduces the drop — 5 sections, no
`.bss`. The route around it is to **reference** the static, and in a functionless
TU the only referrer available is a `.data` initializer holding its address:
`A* p = &g;`. One line of C++, and nobody had written it.

### §4.3 The same scope error hits Rule Y1 — and neither rule is refuted

`OBJ_DATA_BSS_SHAPE.md` §6.2's static and mixed-linkage `.bss` rows are **real
objs**. What this lane adds is that every cell behind them is a TU **with
functions** — which is what keeps *its* statics alive — while
`coff::data::emit_data_obj` serves **only functionless TUs**. It was applying S1
*and* Y1 outside every cell that fitted them.

`D07` is the first functionless mixed-linkage `.bss` anyone has compiled:

```text
  sym[ 5] .bss        sym[ 7] h  val=0 STATIC        <- the static, in the group
  sym[ 8] .XBLD$W     sym[10] __C2_11886
  sym[11] ?g@@3UA@@A  val=4 sec=3 EXTERNAL           <- the EXTERNAL, AFTER
                                                        the NEXT section's group
```

The external `.bss` symbol is not in the `.bss` group at all, and the static
takes offset 0 with the external at 4 — neither Y1's order nor Y1's walk.
**Whether Y1's mixed row still holds for a TU with functions is open and this
lane did not test it**; a writer for that shape must re-measure, not inherit.
Both doc sections now carry the correction as a **SCOPE** correction with that
sentence in it.

### §4.4 Fixed by REFUSAL, and deliberately not by reorder

`emit_data_obj` refuses any `.bss` holding an internal-linkage object. Two
pre-existing live mismatches become honest refusals; **zero matches are lost**
(the extern-only `.bss` is untouched, and the shape has no workload instances
because the drop removes it). `fixtures/cpp/wa16_bss_static_reloc.cpp` grades it
**at alignment 4**, so the closure does not depend on one byte of #1120.

The correct order is a **three-cell observation, not a rule** — nothing here
separates *"internal linkage moves it"* from *"a `.data` relocation into `.bss`
moves it"*, because every cell that survives has both. That is board #1152,
filed with the warning attached and the block marked "do not encode this from
the doc". Rule S1 belongs to #174 and its grid.

## §10 Two instruments, reconciled

Neither is the other's witness.

* **Instrument A — the production cursor.** `data_object_at` / `align_of_type_tag`
  themselves, reached through the differential: the 14 converting cells are
  byte-exact objs, which is the crate's reading graded end to end by real c2.
* **Instrument B — crate-free Python.** `work/w-align16/glread.py` re-implements
  the `.gl` record frame from the documented grammar and imports nothing from the
  crate; `oracle.py` applies the promotion model to it and reads the truth out of
  c2's obj.

```text
section nibbles   predicted == c2   24 of 24    0 disagreements
symbol Values     predicted == c2   25 of 25    0 disagreements
cells with no defined symbol                    1 (A15, capture-fail)
```

**Four apparent disagreements were found and all four were the comparison's, not
either instrument's.** `glread.py` prints the tag's *width* beside the *section's*
nibble, and those are different quantities: `char[4096]` is tag `82` in an ALIGN_8
section (the size-implied promotion), and `A13`'s one-byte `char` sits in the
ALIGN_16 section its neighbour forced (Rule B1's max). `oracle.py` exists
precisely to apply `placement_align` and Rule B1 before comparing, and the count
went 4 → 0 without either reading changing. Writing that down matters because the
lazy fix — declaring the four "expected" — is how a two-instrument check stops
being one.

`glread.py` is `w-align`'s file with two marked edits (it reads widths `82..8E`
instead of refusing above `88`, and it gained an `if __name__` guard so the other
two instruments import rather than fork it) — the brief's "extend, do not write a
third probe", applied to the second instrument as well as the first.

## §12 Corrections to the brief

1. **"three functions in `crates/c2-core/coff` sharing one promotion table"** —
   the count is right; the membership is not. The third is
   **`data::section_nibble`**, a second copy of the `log2+1` table. `bump_layout`,
   which #1120 named, takes **no textual change** — it changes behaviour instead,
   which is the harder thing to notice (§5.1).
2. **"measure — do not assume 16 is the ceiling"** — correct to ask, and **it is
   not**. `CC` (32, nibble 6) and `CE` (64, nibble 7) both exist and both work
   (§3).
3. **"expect no movement"** in `factor-c` — correct, and the reason is stronger
   than "one item of nine": **no workload TU carries an alignment tag at or above
   16 at all** (§6). The item could not have moved it.
4. **"the trap that is already known to be live here"** — respected, and the trap
   that actually fired was a **different** one, in the same function's
   neighbourhood: not a wrong alignment inferred from a type, but a wrong
   *section order* that the alignment widening made reachable and that was
   **already wrong at alignments 4 and 8** (§4). The brief's instinct — that a
   widening here turns refusals into wrong emits — was right; its guess about
   which field was not.
5. **"`crates/c2-il/tests/in_init_probe.rs` now carries `w-align`'s standing
   reading — extend it rather than writing a third probe"** — the probe was read
   and **not extended**, because this lane's reading is graded end to end by the
   differential on 14 byte-exact objs, which is a strictly stronger instrument
   than a record count. What *was* extended rather than forked is `glread.py`
   (§10). No third probe was written.
