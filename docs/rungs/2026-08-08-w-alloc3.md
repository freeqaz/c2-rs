## 2. THE HYPOTHESIS, and the check it passed before it cost a compile

> **RULE BIND.** When c2 inlines a callee `G` at the single call site of `F`:
>
> **BIND** — every GPR **source** field still holding one of `G`'s formals is
> rewritten to the register the caller's actual already lives in. No copy is
> emitted. Registers `G` uses that are not its formals are left alone.
>
> **TEMP** — the destination of the instruction producing `G`'s return value
> stays `r3` **iff** that value is `F`'s own returned value. Otherwise it
> becomes `POOL_TOP` = **`r11`**, and `F`'s trailing computation reads `r11`.

`work/w-alloc3/bind.py` is that statement as 90 lines of Python with a
**fail-closed** PowerPC field table, and its self-test runs with **no obj in
sight**. It reproduces, from the bytes `w-seq` and `w-splice` published:

| witness | callee | RULE BIND | published |
|---|---|---|---|
| the **123** (`?back@?$vector` ← `?end@`) | `80630004` | `81630004 386bfffc` | ✔ |
| the **286** (`?Release@Object@Hmx@@` ← `?Release@ObjRef@@`) | 7 words | all four `r3` source fields → `r4`, `r11`/`r10` destinations untouched | ✔ |
| `w-seq` `s01` — no trailing `mr r3,r11` | `38630001` | `38630001` | ✔ |
| `w-seq` `s03` — setup `mr r3,r4` | `38630001` | `38640001` | ✔ |
| `w-seq` `s11` — a 2-register permutation | `7c641850` | `7c632050` (`subf`, #843) | ✔ |

**5 of 5, 0 objs — board #1075.** This is why the lane was worth running: a
hypothesis that cannot reproduce the record's own witnesses is refuted before
it costs a compile, and one that reproduces all five and *still* dies on fresh
structure is exactly the `RULE W2` shape. Saying so needs both halves measured.

### 2.1 Why it is not a restatement of any of the six

`PREREG.md` §1.1's table, reproduced because it is the claim the brief demanded:

new is the claim that *this regime is that regime*. Cells where a comparator
could re-enter — two inlined results live at once — were registered as a
**refusal** (`D7`) before the grids existed, not modelled.

### 4.2 GRID-A, and the two refinements it forced

**38 cells, 33 graded, 33 HIT, 0 MISS** — after two narrowings registered in
`ADDENDUM-2.md` before GRID-H's column was frozen. The three raw misses:

```text
  A-two-SUM-10    g = 7c632214 (add r3,r3,r4)
     RULE BIND     7c641a14   add r3,r4,r3
     c2            7c632214   add r3,r3,r4        <- commutative canonicalisation

  A-arith-add1    g = 38630001 (addi r3,r3,1), site `g(x0) + 5`
     RULE BIND     39630001 386b0005
     c2            38630006   addi r3,r3,6        <- a CONSTANT FOLD
```

Neither is an allocation decision, and the matching non-commutative cell is a
**HIT**: `A-two-SUB-10` is `subf r3,r4,r3` → `subf r3,r3,r4`, which is
`w-seq`'s `s11` reproduced at the byte. So both were **refused**, not modelled:

> **D10 (COMMUTATIVE)** — a commutative operator whose two register sources are
> two *different* live formals takes the cell out of domain.
> **D11 (CONSTANT FOLD)** — in `arith` mode, a result produced by `addi`/`addis`
> takes the cell out of domain.

**D10 is stated with no free parameter**, on purpose. The cheaper form —
*"refuse only when the substitution REORDERS the pair"* — would have kept two
GRID-A hits and would have been fitted on the direction c2 happened to
canonicalise in one cell. As written it refuses the identity binding too and
costs those two hits. Its **indexed-form** members (`lwzx`, `stwx`, …) went in
**a priori**, before any cell containing one was compiled, on the ground that
`RA+RB` is a sum — registered in `ADDENDUM-2.md` so it cannot be read as a
post-hoc rescue. `H-idx` later graded **2 of 2 HIT** under it.

**§A2.5's registered re-grade number was WRONG and is scored as a loss.** It
predicted *"36 HIT / 2 D10 / 1 D11"*; the measurement is **33 HIT / 4 D10 /
1 D11**. The addendum's own prose one paragraph earlier says the clause
*"refuses the identity binding too, costs two GRID-A hits"* — so the
registration was internally inconsistent and the measurement agrees with the
prose, not with the arithmetic. Published rather than corrected, because
§A2.5's whole sentence was *"a different number is a finding and not a silent
correction"*.

Every RULE BIND answer above is **semantically correct** and every one is the
**wrong bytes**. `a - b + c` is symmetric in `a` and `c`, so c2 is free to pick
either, and it picks the other one on exactly the three permutations where BIND
does not. `H-spell-neg` is sharper: c2 folds `-x + 4` into a single `subfic`,
an opcode absent from the callee's body, so no field rewrite of any kind could
have produced it.

**So `w-seq` §10.2's caution is now measured rather than argued.** It warned
that *"the rename that produced `?back@?$vector` picked `r11` because a later
instruction needed `r3`, which is a register allocator's decision and not a
field edit — the measurement says what changed, not what decides it."* The 503
field diffs are a description of the output. A rule that reads them as an edit
is right wherever the caller's expression has only one encoding and wrong
wherever it has two.

## 5. WHAT SURVIVED, and it is the part a successor should keep

### 5.1 TEMP — `POOL_TOP` measured outside the store run (#1068)

Every ALLOC grid to date is a **store run in a leaf**. `H-wide` is a different
shape: the single value an inlined callee returns, consumed by one more
instruction.

```text
   int* g(V* v) { return v->b; }                        lwz  r3, 4(r3)
   int* f(int,int,int,int,V* x4) { return g(x4) - 1; }  lwz  r11,4(r7)
                                                        addi r3, r11,-4
```

**16 of 16** across caller formal counts 5, 6, 7, 8 × bound position {first,
last} × mode {ret, arith}, plus 10 more in GRID-A at counts 1–4. With five
formals live the **lowest** free volatile is `r8`; c2 takes `r11`. So the rival
*"the temp is the lowest free volatile"* is refuted on a population that is not
a store run, and the pool walk this module ships is confirmed from a third
direction after **#543** (`r12` never used) and **#605** (the descent reaches
`r7`).

`H-temp` adds the sharper case, **4 of 4**: a callee that already holds a temp
in `r11` and a caller whose result also wants `r11`.

```text
   int* g(W* w) { return w->p->b; }        lwz r11,0(r3) ; lwz r3, 4(r11)
   int* f(int,int,W* x2){ return g(x2)-1;} lwz r11,0(r5) ; lwz r11,4(r11)
                                           addi r3,r11,-4
```

`crates/c2-core/src/codegen/alloc.rs::one_producer_takes_pool_top_at_every_floor`
pins it: one producer takes `POOL_TOP` at **every legal pool floor**, and
refuses above it. No existing test in that module varies the floor, so nothing
stopped a future edit from inverting the walk and staying green.

### 5.2 P5 — the body-length stratification, and it is NOT a size effect

`docs/STATUS.md`'s `/QXSTALLS` lesson: a +76.25 pp read between two populations
was body length and nothing else. So the hit rate is printed per callee body
length before any of it is believed (`work/w-alloc3/score.py`):

```text
   callee body 1 w    HIT 48   MISS 1   rate 0.980
   callee body 2 w    HIT 18   MISS 4   rate 0.818
```

The rate does fall with length — **and it is not length.** The 2-word bucket
splits cleanly by *family*: `SUM3` (the reassociable expression) is 3 hit / 4
miss, while `LD2` is **4 of 4** and `IDX` **2 of 2** and `EXT` **2 of 2** at the
same length. And the 1-word bucket's single miss, `H-spell-neg`, is the
*sharpest* refutation in the grid. **The separating axis is whether the
caller's expression has a second legal encoding, not how big it is** — which is
what the misses say directly and what a bucket rate alone would have hidden.

### 5.3 D4 counts the return register as a temp, and it cost two cells (#1074)

`D4` requires every register the callee touches that is not one of *its own
formals* to sit strictly above the caller's formal high-water mark. A callee
with **no** formals — `int g(){ return 42; }` — writes its result into `r3`,
which `D4` reads as a temp colliding with the caller's first formal, and both
`H-noarg` cells refuse. **The clause should exempt the ABI return register.**

A refusal is never wrong, so this changes no verdict. It is recorded because
the next lane's holdout is two cells smaller than it needs to be, and would
otherwise re-discover it by rewriting the clause.

## 6. Process — the one thing that went wrong, reported rather than smoothed

### 6.1 A freezer that imported its own grader ran it at import (#1073)

`freeze_h.py` imports `run_grid.py` for `compile_cell` and `text_comdats`; the
alternative was a **second COFF reader**, which `docs/GAPS.md` §6 forbids and
which this repo has already paid for once. `run_grid.py` called `main()` at
module level **with no `__name__` guard**, so the import executed the grader
over `sys.argv` — which was GRID-H's — and printed the holdout's verdict before
the frozen column had been written.

**The verdict is unaffected and this is checkable.** It came from the program
frozen at `ccf208e3`, with no refinement between the freeze and the grade, and
re-grading under `--frozen` reproduces it exactly: 46 of 46 `sha256` re-checked,
the same **33 HIT / 5 MISS / 8 out of domain**, the same five cells. What was
lost is the ceremony of a committed prediction file *preceding* the grade, not
the independence the ceremony exists to buy.

**The general form**: an unguarded `main()` is a side effect that any `import`
fires, and the thing it fired here is the one step the whole protocol exists to
order. The guard now carries the note.

### 6.2 What "frozen" can and cannot mean for a rule of this shape

`w-refbind` and `w-ilx` froze a **prediction per cell** before compiling.
RULE BIND cannot be frozen that way: its right-hand side is *the callee's own
emitted body*, which is a compiler output. So what was frozen instead is the
**program** — `bind.py` and `gen_grids.py` committed, GRID-H's sources and
`sha256` committed at `22e30ad1`, the rule frozen at `ccf208e3` — and
`freeze_h.py` is structurally incapable of seeing the answer: `callee_only()`
drops every `.text` COMDAT named `?f@@*` before its caller can look. Stated
here as a **weaker** guarantee than w-ilx's, not an equal one.

### 8.1 The grid a successor owes #1070

Everything RULE BIND got wrong is c2 choosing between two encodings of one
value. The description in §4.4 fits eight cells and has **never predicted a
cell before it was graded**. The population that would decide it, named here
because #912 says naming it is the whole obligation:

1. **Commutative pairs at unequal register distances** — `(r3,r10)`, `(r4,r9)`,
   `(r5,r6)` — to separate *"lower register first"* from *"first formal first"*
   and from *"nearest pair first"*. GRID-H's six permutations only ever span
   `r3 … r6`.
2. **Three-operand commutative chains** (`a + b + c`, `a & b & c`), where a
   register order exists that no pairwise rule induces.
3. **The same pair reached through a temp** — one operand already in `r11` —
   which is where a *register-number* rule and a *source-position* rule must
   disagree.
4. **`subfic`'s own family**: `K - x`, `x - K`, `-x + K`, `K + (-x)` at both
   signs, because `H-spell-neg` says the rewrite is algebraic and one cell
   cannot say how far it reaches.

Frozen predictions first, and on cells none of these 84 contains.
