## 1. What it builds, in one paragraph

`BodyLayout` had exactly **one** production client when `w-item-d` counted it
(#3124), and the crate had **23** branch sites in **13** other lowerings that
computed every displacement themselves and patched at a fixed offset — *"the one
shape a re-layout cannot serve, because a fixed site has nowhere to grow"*. This
lane moved the ones that can move, and the mechanism is **one new fact**:

* **`FinishedBody::start_of(block)`** — where a placed block's run begins.
* **`at(block, k)`** — the checked spelling of `start_of + k`, refusing a `k`
  that reaches out of the block it names, `k == len` included.
* **`run_len(block)`** — the denominator, **excluding the terminator**, because
  the terminator word is `LabelMap`'s site and an `at` that admitted it would let
  a lowering overwrite a branch the fixup pass had already patched.

The start is bound from the same number, at the same moment, as the label:
`finish` writes `starts[id]` on the line beside `labels.define(label, &text)`. A
second count kept next to it is precisely the thing this removes.

## 2. The finding: the branch sites were never the whole problem

This is `P17`, registered at p 0.75, and it is what made the migration
tractable.

Every one of those thirteen lowerings also **publishes** offsets off the same
running `t.len()` — a `bl`'s `REL24` site, a float constant's `REFHI`/`REFLO`
pair, the prologue's length. **A lowering cannot hand its branch positions to a
layout while keeping those in a counter of its own, because both come off the
same vector.** So the migration is not "route the branches through the map"; it
is "the layout owns every position the lowering states", and the two halves stand
or fall together.

Stated as `start_of(block) + k`, the published offset's `k` becomes a constant of
**one block's own run** instead of a constant of the whole body — which is
exactly the property #3124 asks for, because a block-relative site grows with its
block. **One** fact serves branches and publications alike: no per-site `Mark`
type, no second fixup list, no `Form` variant, no `Terminator` variant.

### 2.1 What the migration deleted

Ranked by how much the constant was hiding.

1. **`if_call_join` carried a table of eight word indices into the whole body** —
   `I_BT_LT = 7`, `I_BT_EQ = 8`, `I_BT_INNER = 10`, `I_BL_HI = 11`,
   `I_B_JOIN = 12`, `I_BL_LO = 13`, `I_JOIN = 14`, `I_EXIT = 15` — and spelled
   its four branches as `(I_EXIT - I_BT_LT) * 4` and friends, with a `- 3` on
   each `bl` to convert an index back past the prologue. All eight encode the
   placement order. It is written once now, as the placement order, and both
   `- 3`s are gone: each `bl` is word 0 of its own arm block.
2. **`guard_ret_chain` and `close_call_chain` carried the literal `12`.** That
   `12` is not a fact about a guard. It is *"over the arm's one word and its own
   `b`"* — a fact about the two blocks between the branch and its target, which
   now lives in the only place it can be checked.
3. **`fp_store_diamond`'s `block_lengths` was the SOURCE of its two
   displacements**, so the `debug_assert` that graded it could only ever restate
   the formula it had just used. The displacements are the map's now and
   `block_lengths` is an independent prediction: that assertion does real work
   for the first time.
4. **`guard_chain_shared_tail`, `osf_handle_guard` and `alloc_init_or_fail`** each
   carried a `patch(site, target, …)` closure over three to five `*_site` indices
   into a flat `Vec`. The three `||` guards in `guard_chain_shared_tail` named
   one `BlockId` three times where the closure spelled `l_err − site` three
   times.
5. **`xlrc_create_guard`'s Class C epilogue was the registered stretch** and it
   composed without a new mechanism (§4).

### 2.2 One hazard, found by reading, that every such migration will meet

`guard_chain_shared_tail`'s `hi_off` was a `0`-means-not-yet-emitted sentinel.
That worked **only** because it was a *body* offset with a prologue above it. As
a rotate-**block** offset, zero is a legal answer — at `n = 3` the `REFHI` really
is the block's first word — so the sentinel became an `Option`. Nothing detected
this; the `debug_assert` it guarded would simply have gone vacuous at exactly one
arity. **A migration that converts body offsets to block offsets turns every
`0`-as-absent sentinel into a live bug**, and it is silent.

## 4. The stretch, and what it turned out to be

`xlrc_create_guard` was registered (`P18`) as a **condition, not a hope**: it
ends in `b __restgprlr_26`, an external branch with `LK = 0` and **no `blr`
anywhere in the body**, and the question was whether that composes without a new
mechanism.

**It does, and the answer is that it already was the mechanism.** An external
branch is a *relocation*, not a label reference (§3.3, board #191) — which is
precisely why `Terminator::TailCall` exists as its own variant instead of a third
`Form`: a placeholder plus a reported site. The epilogue's `addi r1,r1,144` is
the join block's run and the `b` is its terminator.
`FrameLayout::epilogue_gpr_helper` stays the **single** producer of both words:
it is asked again after `finish`, at the offset `tail_sites` reports, and the
`addi` it produces there is asserted against the one already placed.

## 5. The residue is a FENCE, not a budget — `P16`

Eight sites in five lowerings cannot reach a layout at all, and neither fence is
this lane's to move.

| lowering | sites | fence |
|---|---:|---|
| `ptr_walk_loop` | 2 | **`LabelMap` invariant 4** — a **back edge**. #746; peer `w-slots` holds it |
| `ptr_walk_chain_loop` | 2 | same |
| `json_utf8_copy` | 2 | same |
| `xtea_encrypt_loop` | 1 | same — and its *only* `reach::direct` site **is** the back edge |
| `pool_ctor_chain` | 1 | **no CTR terminator.** Its back edge is `bdnz`; `Terminator` has no variant and §6.3 declines the discovery that would justify one |

The invariant-4 fence is not per-site: **`finish` resolves *every* branch through
the one map**, so a body with one back edge cannot go through it at all — which
is why `ptr_walk_loop`'s *forward* entry guard is fenced off by its own body's
back edge.

**And that fence was measured at zero cost, correctly, three days ago.** `#3089`
priced `LabelMap` invariant 4 at *"0 conversions and 0 bytes to keep"* on the
grounds that **no caller routes a back edge through the map** — true then, true
now, and inert then only because the map had two clients. This lane took it to
nine, and fence A went from binding nothing to blocking **7 of the 8** residual
sites. See `#3144` and §8 item 4: **superseded by construction, not refuted**,
and no instrument here can see the difference. **`#3124`'s "migrate the 23 sites" is therefore not performable in
full until #746's fence moves**, and nothing here relaxed either fence:
`block_ir`'s `a_backward_branch_is_refused_by_the_label_maps_own_rule` still
reads `labels.rs`'s own words and is still the test that fires.

### 8.1 A negative result worth the line

**The obvious reading of #3124 would have migrated the branch sites and stopped,
and it would not have compiled** — eight of the eight lowerings publish an offset
off the same `t.len()` the branches came from, so there is no state in which the
branches are the layout's and the `bl` sites are not. That is the cheap version.
The expensive version is that an implementer who *worked around* it — by keeping
`t.len()` for publications while the branches went through the map — would have
shipped exactly the thing #3124 says is unserveable: a body with a movable
control-flow graph and a fixed set of relocation sites, which links, and whose
first relaxation puts every `REL24` on the wrong word.
