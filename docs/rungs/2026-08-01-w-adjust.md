### 1.4 Why E2 is not the row's size, and why it is not a discount either

The ceiling rule says that when a row's blocker is a class whose emitter already
exists, the ceiling *is* the estimate. Here the emitter arguably does exist —
`docs/.../ctor_dtor.rs` records that a base adjust is handed to codegen as
`[Load(this), Lit(k), Add]`, which `int_tail_call_text` has lowered since the
MVP. So E2 is **not** a discount on a ceiling; it is a guess at the ceiling
itself, and the ceiling is unknown because *no* completeness bit has ever been
computed for this row (its `Block` is `expr-intrinsic`, not `CALL_IN_EXPR`, so
`whole_body_is_one_value` never runs on it — which is exactly why §8.7 calls it
the largest never-measured row).

Three facts push E2 well below 8,790, and they are **not** one quantity at three
thresholds:

1. **61.7 % of the bodies are `calls-2plus`.** A body with two calls cannot end
   at the member call, so it cannot be a tail member call whatever the receiver
   is. (Independent of 2 and 3: it is a count of CALL tokens.)
2. **52.7 % carry an EH marker (`eh-state0/1/unknown`).** The plumbing those
   bodies carry is not in the accepted class at all.
3. **The argument vocabulary.** In the receiver-parses pool the two live
   refusals after "does not end at the call" are
   `tail-argument-not-in-the-operand-vocabulary` (82,438) and
   `framed-result-not-consumed-by-a-literal-post-op` (32,443).

1 and 2 are correlated by construction (an object with a destructor becoming
live usually means a second call), which is why the estimate is *not* the product
of the marginals — §8.6's rule. E2 is set at roughly the body-column anchor
(135,926 × 17.1 % production acceptance ≈ 23,000 bodies) discounted for the
`calls-2plus` excess and then converted at the row's own emitted rate
(8,790/135,941 = 6.47 %) — and then widened hard downwards *and* upwards, because
WR1's realized emitted number came in **4.2× LOW** against a body-column-derived
discount, and a discount derived on the body column does not transfer to the
emitted column.

### 1.5 What a decline looks like

For #128, a decline is earned if E8 lands high (the row is really a `type-ptr`
operand row wearing a receiver row's name), or if the realized conversion is far
under E6's floor, or if the 1,380 turn out to need a relocated data-symbol
address the port cannot yet place at the receiver position.

### 2.1 #127 — `expr-intrinsic-this-adjust` is worth **472 emitted functions**

**472 of 8,790 = 5.4 % of the row**, and **434 of the 472 (92.0 %)** are at
adjust offset 0, where the receiver's true operand stream is `[Load(this)]` and
no new codegen is needed at all. In §8.2's units: **+0.26 pp** of the emitted
census, 19.37 % → 19.64 %.

The row is a **receiver-designator** row and its name does not say so. 135,926 of
its 135,941 bodies decline at `eat_receiver_this` — the member-call production
bails non-committally, the assignment parser then runs `parse_expr`, and
`parse_expr` stops on the intrinsic. The census key names the *second* reader's
stop. That is why `whole_body_is_one_value` has never run on it (its `Block` ctx
is `expr-intrinsic`, not `CALL_IN_EXPR`) and why the row has never carried a
completeness bit — §8.7's "largest never-measured row" is a consequence of the
attribution, not of anyone's oversight.

**Where the other 94.6 % goes, measured on the row's own emitted rows** (joint,
per function, not a product of marginals):

| emitted rows of `expr-intrinsic-this-adjust` | count |
|---|---:|
| total | 8,790 |
| clean (`cflow-straight` ∧ `eh-none` ∧ `calls<2`) | **3,062** |
| `calls-2plus` ∧ `cflow-straight` ∧ `eh-state0` | 2,034 |
| `calls-2plus` ∧ `cflow-straight` ∧ `eh-state1` | 1,906 |
| `calls-2plus` ∧ branchy | ~1,300 |

So the realized 472 is **15.4 % of the row's clean stock**, and the clean stock
is 34.8 % of the row. A body that survives both filters still has to be a
*complete tail member call*: the argument vocabulary, the "does not end at the
call" gate and the codegen-class gates take the rest.

**The recommendation is to leave it, and the reason is not its size.** 472
emitted is a perfectly ordinary rung by this project's standards (WR1 realized
+505). What makes it a poor *next* rung is the shape of the remainder: at
`k != 0` the receiver reaches codegen as `[Load, Lit, Add]`, which only the
one-argument operand path can lower — `sym_addr_tail_call`'s slot list admits
`[Load]`, `[Lit]` and `[SymAddr]` and nothing computed — so those 38 emitted
functions split by whether the member call is nullary, which no current axis
reports. The 434 at `k == 0` are the honest, self-contained number, and they are
what a future rung should be scheduled at. **Not 8,790, and not the 10,469
`-whole` figure §8.7 already flagged as belonging to a different key.**

### 2.3 #128 — **+1,385 emitted**, and the `-then-type-ptr` half of its key was never a blocker

The row converts **100 %** — all 1,380 — and the same widening additionally takes
16 emitted functions from three neighbouring `recv-object` rows, against 11 that
re-file under a named codegen gate (`data-sym-not-extern`, `call-arg-sym-permuted`,
`call-arg-multi-sym`). Net **+1,385 emitted, +0.78 pp**, 19.37 % → 20.15 %.

**E8 was missed by 14×, and the reason is a finding about the instrument.** The
key says `-then-type-ptr`, i.e. "with the receiver form *and* a pointer-typed
operand admitted, the body is whole" — so the row reads as needing two widenings.
It needs one. The `-whole` measure's operand vocabulary is
`mcall::eat_int_operands`, which calls `eat_int_like`; the **shipping acceptance
path** is `eat_call_args` → `parse_expr` with the `55 <TYPE>` gate
`eat_int_like_or_ptr4`, which has admitted width-4 pointers since W22. So
`-then-type-ptr` here is an artifact of the census measure being *narrower than
the emitter*, and the control that establishes it is the measurement itself:
admitting only the receiver converted 1,380 of 1,380, which it could not have
done if the pointer operand were a real blocker.

**Any `-then-type-ptr` row is suspect for the same reason.** On the emitted
board that is `…recv-object-then-type-ptr-and-call-more` (5,663),
`…-and-deref-load-more` (1,462), `…-and-plumbing-more` (449) and
`…-and-op-more` (409) — 7,983 emitted functions whose keys name a second
construct that may not be a second construct. Repairing `eat_int_operands` to
share the acceptance path's type gate is a small instrument change with a re-key
behind it, and it belongs with board #110's `-whole{k}` over-count and §9.11's
lost-suffix under-count: three corruptions of the same ranking input.

**What the 1,380 actually are.** Four distinct mangled names, and three of them
are 1,379 of the total:

## 3. The ALARM this rung found, which is not this rung's

`fixtures/cpp/wadjust_obj_recv.cpp` mismatched at first build, on
`void l3() { gDbg.two(3, 4); }`. Bisected to a body with **no receiver in it at
all**:

```cpp
extern int gI;  void gs3(int*, int, int);
void b3() { gs3(&gI, 3, 4); }        // pure WR1: a data symbol as a call ARGUMENT
```

At mainline `33d0049`, with none of this lane's code present, that body is
**1/1 in class** and the port emits it **wrong** (`Port=Mismatch @ offset 545`).
Verified by checking out `33d0049 -- crates`, rebuilding, diffing, and restoring.

WR1's shipped rule was *"the `lis` is hoisted and the address `addi` is emitted
LAST, after every other slot's setup"*. c2's own `.cod` listing says the address
goes **SECOND** — after exactly one word of the descending non-address walk:

```
  void b3() { gs3(&gI, 3, 4); }
    3d600000  lis  r11,?gI@@3HA
    38a00004  li   r5,4          <- one word of the descending walk
    386b0000  addi r3,r11,?gI    <- the address, SECOND
    38800003  li   r4,3          <- …and the rest of the walk follows
    48000000  b    ?gs3@@YAXPAHHH@Z
```

**At one setup word the two readings are the same sequence.** WR1's fixture and
its generated sweep both varied the address's slot, its destination register, the
literal's value, the object's type and the mangled name's length — and never the
**count**. Eleven cells now pin the rule, every word read off c2's own listing
(`work/wadjust/probe/q1.cpp`, `q3.cpp`), covering walks of length 0 through 4,
the address at slot 0 and at a middle slot, literals and in-place formals in the
walk, and both a free and a member caller. It **subsumes** WR1's rule rather than
contradicting it: address-last is the n ≤ 1 special case.

Three things follow, and they are the point rather than the fix:

1. **This is §9.10's finding arriving one rung later.** WR1 landed ~1,500 lines
   including 150 in `coff.rs` and the `#[test]` count did not move; the standing
   rule that came out of it was "a rung that touches `coff.rs` must add a
   portable assertion for each ordering rule it establishes". The rule belongs to
   the **ordering rule**, not to the file: this one lives in `codegen/calls.rs`,
   had no unit test either, and went wrong the same way.
   `the_data_address_addi_is_emitted_second_not_last` is now that assertion, and
   it fails on the portable lane with no toolchain.
2. **The generated sweep found nothing because the axis did not exist.** This is
   another instance of "generated axes find what hand fixtures structurally
   cannot", with the twist that the generator has to *have* the axis:
   `53-data-symbol-addr.py`'s WR1 block emitted 70+ cases and every one had ≤ 1
   literal. The arity block appended to it now sweeps 2–5 literals with the
   address at every slot, plus in-place formals and a member receiver.
3. **A fixture's own blind spot is worth writing into the fixture.**
   `wadjust_obj_recv.cpp` says, in its header, that it cannot discriminate any
   slot-dependent rule because the receiver is argument zero by construction —
   the same sentence WR1's ALARM had to be discovered to produce.

## 4. What it admits, and what it refuses

Admits: `26 <sym> [2C <TYPE ptr4> b] 99 <TYPE ptr4> 00` standing where a member
call's receiver designator goes, when the body ends at that call. The address
enters the slot list as `IlOp::SymAddr` and takes WR1's `sym_addr_tail_call`
path; the object must be an undefined external, at offset 0, the only symbol in
the call, and every other slot must hold the formal already in that register or a
`li`-sized literal.

Refuses, by name, each in `wadjust_obj_recv_neg.cpp`: a formal that has to move
(`call-arg-sym-permuted`); a defined or static object (`data-sym-not-extern`); an
offset off the object; a second symbol in the call (`call-arg-multi-sym`); a
chain through the object; a literal post-op on the result
(`tail-object-receiver-is-not-a-tail-call`, this rung's own new key — the framed
and comparison productions rebuild the receiver as `IlOp::Load` from a token that
here names a data symbol, and threading a second operand form through two shapes
with no captures is not this rung); and a second statement after the call.

The discriminator against a **chain** — whose head `26 <m> 26 <x>` is
byte-identical for two whole tokens — declines with the cursor untouched, so a
real chain reaches the chain production exactly as before. Both directions are
unit-tested.

**4 hits, 4 misses, and three of the misses are worth more than the hits.**

* **E1/E2 together are the WR1 lesson repeating with the sign flipped.** The body
  estimate was 3.2× high while the emitted estimate was 2.1× high — both wrong in
  the *same* direction this time, because both were derived from the same
  body-column anchor. The emitted number was inside its interval only because the
  interval had been widened on WR1's precedent. **A body-column anchor is not a
  source of an emitted estimate even when it is transparently discounted.**
* **E4 is a registered control that was the wrong control, which is §9.9.2's
  lesson exactly.** The sink hands codegen `[Load(obj)]` where the true stream at
  `k != 0` is `[Load(obj), Lit(k), Add]`, so it *does* over-claim — and the
  `census/gate disagreement` counter cannot see it, because the port accepts the
  wrong stream and would emit wrong bytes rather than refuse. A gate-agreement
  counter separates "the census accepted something the port refuses"; it says
  nothing about "the census accepted something the port would get wrong". The
  control that *would* have worked is the one this lane ran for #128 and not for
  #127: build it and put it in front of the differential — which is precisely how
  the §3 ALARM was found.
* **E8's miss is the `-then-type-ptr` finding** (§2.3) and it re-prices 7,983
  further emitted functions.
* **E6's "hit" hides a wrong assumption.** The point estimate was right to 0.4 %,
  but the interval's ceiling was set at the row's own emitted count, on the
  reasoning that a row cannot convert more than itself. A widening is not scoped
  to a row: it took 16 functions from three neighbours. **A census row is not a
  unit of work.**
