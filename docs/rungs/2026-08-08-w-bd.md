## 1. The result

> ### **`0xBD` was not an unwitnessed opcode. It is the MOST heavily witnessed opcode in `crates/c2-il`, and it refused for a reason that has nothing to do with evidence: `SkipForm` had no variant that could SPELL its width.**
>
> `IL_CALL_GRAMMAR.md` §2.1 states the grammar and §2.2 pins the flags byte from
> a three-convention probe. **Three** readers in this crate already consume
> `BD <TYPE> <1 raw byte> <varint>` today — `mcall::eat_call_and_args` (the
> **accepting** parser, graded byte-exact by the differential), `mcall`'s
> call-form walk, and `control_flow.rs`'s `cf-call-fn-type-id` arm. Every
> `SkipForm` variant is one byte short of that shape or one field long, so `BD`
> fell through to `None` and `chain_skip_form` reported an artificial floor as
> if it were a price. **A width table that cannot spell a width it already knows
> refuses for the wrong reason, and the refusal is indistinguishable by reading
> from one that lacks a capture.**

## 2. The two independent confirmations

The standard is `w-divsplit` / board **#820**, and it is **two** confirmations:
a captured token stream graded against real `c2.dll`, and a workload-wide check
that the rival readings are excluded by the bytes. Both were met, and the second
is **758×** the size of the one that moved `0x05`.

### 2.1 Confirmation 1 — a capture taken at THIS master, graded

`work/w-bd/probe/bd_cc.cpp` rebuilds `IL_CALL_GRAMMAR.md` §2.2's `e19.cpp`,
which is not in this tree: four externals differing **only** in calling
convention, with a byte-identical `int` return type, so the flags byte is the
only field that can move. Captured with `c2rs capture` and read out with
`work/w-bd/probe/read_bd.py`:

```text

  BD  TYPE[3] 86 41 74     flags 0x00  id[5] 80 02 10 00 00  width 10  -> 0xB9   int __stdcall sc(int)
  BD  TYPE[3] 86 41 74     flags 0x04  id[5] 80 07 10 00 00  width 10  -> 0xB9   int __fastcall fc(int)
  BD  TYPE[3] 86 41 74     flags 0x40  id[5] 80 06 10 00 00  width 10  -> 0xB9   int va(const char*, ...)
```

**Graded**, which is what makes it a confirmation and not a reading:

`Port=Match` is the load-bearing row: the port reads a `BD` token at this width
and emits an obj **byte-exact against real `c2.dll` under wibo**.

### 2.2 Confirmation 2 — the workload, at 3,544,589 sites

`work/w-bd/bdwalk.py` over the `.ex` of **876 of the 878** workload TUs
(`capture_all.sh`; the two absentees are `FxSendPitchShift360.cpp` and
`FxSendSynapse360.cpp`, which do not capture). Sites are **anchored** rather
than byte-scanned, because §2.1 records that a raw `BD` scan of one TU finds
8,638 hits of which 10 are false: anchor **A** is the direct-call form
`26 <token> BD`, anchor **B** the member-call bind `99 <TYPE> <varint> BD`.

Each reading is judged by the same predicate — *does the byte it lands on open
an operand token?* — where the legal set is taken **arm for arm from
`control_flow.rs`'s own `operand()` and `step()`**, not hand-listed.

**A fixed-width reader is refuted by the same table**: a fixed 3-byte return

### 2.3 The control, and the positive question

*Would this control have gone red if the width were wrong in the most likely
way?* The most likely way is dropping the flags byte. **It goes red at 3,544,480
of 3,544,589 sites**, and the mechanism is visible rather than statistical: the
flags byte is `00`, `TypeVarint` consumes it as a whole short varint, and the
cursor stops on the `0x80` that opens the real id's escape — a byte no arm of
`chain_skip_form` **or** of `control_flow.rs`'s `operand()` accepts. That
mechanism is pinned as a unit test
(`dropping_the_call_flags_byte_lands_on_a_byte_no_arm_accepts`).

**The instrument was widened once, mid-lane, and saying so is part of the
result.** The first `LEGAL_OPEN` was hand-listed and omitted the EH-state
trailer family (`5C`/`5D`/`5E`), which `operand()` has had all along — so 10
perfectly good `BD` sites landing on a `5D` were scored as desyncs by the
instrument rather than by the bytes (W read 33 desyncs, not 23). The fix was to
take the vocabulary from the tree instead of from a hand list. **The check that
this did not defang the control is that R1's and R2's red counts did not move
at all** — 3,544,480 and 3,544,319 before and after — because `0x80` and `0x00`
are not in `operand()` either.

### 2.4 The residue, screened rather than asserted

111 of 3,544,589 sites are anomalous (88 undecodable + 23 desync), all but 8 of
them under the heuristic member anchor. A **non-circular** screen — does some
token starting *before* `j` span past `j`, which is a property of the bytes and
not of any reading of `BD` — accounts for 110 of the 111:

```text
  the 0xBD lies inside a neighbouring TYPE's LEB payload     94
  the 0xBD lies inside a neighbouring 4-byte token           11
  the 0xBD lies inside a `66 <n>` class descriptor's tokens    5
  UNEXPLAINED residue                                          1
```

The one residue is `src/system/hamobj/HamCharacter.cpp` @ 30935,
`… 4f 02 be 26 4f 02 >bd< 26 4f 01 22 …` — a run of `4F 02 <2 bytes>` records,
not a function body. Every one of the 111 is printed in full in
`work/w-bd/bdwalk.txt` so the claim is checkable rather than trusted.

### 3.1 "`0xBD` is unwitnessed"

Board **#1289** and the brief that followed it both read `expr-chain-noform-0xBD`
as *"an opcode whose payload width this tree has never pinned"*. That is what
the key **means**, and it is not what was true. The tree had pinned it three
times in `crates/c2-il` and once in a dedicated doc. What it had not done was
give `SkipForm` a variant of the right shape.

**The generalisation is worth more than the row**: `chain_skip_form`'s `None` is
overloaded. It means *"no evidence"* and it also means *"evidence this enum
cannot express"*, and the two are indistinguishable from the key. `0x4C` (§5) is
the second instance and it was found in the same afternoon.

### 3.2 "Seven of the seventeen"

Nine. Counted off `w-front3`'s own committed ladder JSON, and its own §2 table

* *"the width table is `crates/c2-il/src/func/body/shapes/control_flow.rs`"* —
  **wrong file.** `control_flow.rs`'s `operand()` has had a correct `0xBD` arm

## 4. What this does NOT settle

### 4.1 Whether the flags field is a raw byte or a varint — and the corpus cannot say

Every value the field takes is `0x00` (3,544,297), `0x40` (171) or `0x04`, all
below `0x80`, where a plain byte and a `read_varint` agree. So **R3 agrees with
W at 3,544,501 of 3,544,589 sites** and the corpus excludes neither.
`IL_CALL_GRAMMAR.md` §2.2 already carries this as UNKNOWN and this lane does not
close it.

It does not have to. **The width is the same under both readings at every value
in the corpus**, and a *step* needs only the width. The tie is broken by
matching the **accepting** parser, which reads a raw byte: a sink that read a
field differently from the acceptor would report a successor key the acceptor
can never reach, which is the one way this instrument could lie. That reasoning
is written into `chain_skip_form`'s `0xBD` row rather than left in this file.

**Where it would bite**: a flags value ≥ `0x80` would make the two readings
differ by four bytes. None occurs here. A new corpus must re-run
`work/w-bd/bdwalk.py` before inheriting this.

### 4.2 Anything about acceptance

The sink pushes no `IlOp` and a walk that used it refuses under
`expr-chain-sink-poison`. `mismatch` is 0 at both ends and `IlBundle::functions()`
is untouched. **Decoding is not accepting**, and this lane moved only decoding —
of an instrument that is off by default.

## 5. THE FLOOR UNDER THE FLOOR — `0x4C`, measured and DELIBERATELY NOT SHIPPED

All nine ladders now terminate at `expr-chain-noform-0x4C`. `0x4C` is the
CALL-END — `BD`'s own closing bracket — and it is absent from
`chain_skip_form` for precisely the reason `0xBD` was: `4B` is in the table,
`4C` is not, and `control_flow.rs`, `mcall::eat_call_args_region` and `codec.rs`
all consume it as **one byte** today.

Measured anyway, by `work/w-bd/cewalk.py`, over the **26,701** positions where a
`4C` is certain rather than guessed — the byte a zero-argument call's `BD` token
ends on, anchored by the width §2 just confirmed:

| reading | desync / 26,701 |
|---|---:|
| **P** payload-free | **0** |
| B1 `4C <one raw byte>` | 18,958 |
| T `4C <TYPE>` — does not even decode | 21,947 |
| K `4C <token>` | 14,564 |

**And it is not shipped.** The reason is the anchor, and it is trap 0 in its
most concrete form: *a green control is a statement about the population it ran
over.* Those 26,701 sites are **zero-argument calls only**. The `4C` that closes
a call **with** arguments — 1,081,061 of the 3.5 M `BD` tokens end on a `4C`,
and the other 2.46 M do not — is **not in this population at all**. A width
pinned on the unrepresentative half is exactly the failure this table exists to
prevent, and "the evidence was in hand and I was already in the file" is the
worst available reason to widen it.

**What the next lane needs, and it is cheap**: an anchor for the argument-closing
`4C`. It falls out of `mcall::eat_call_args_region`, which already walks that
region — or out of running `bdwalk.py` forward through the region under the now-
pinned `BD` width. Board **#1318**. Expect the same answer; get it over the
other 97 %.
