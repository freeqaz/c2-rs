## Headline

**READ §7 FIRST IF YOU ARE QUOTING A NUMBER FROM THIS DOCUMENT.** The review
found that this lane applied its own registered ship/no-ship rule to one
component and not to the other, that its named control's headline was vacuous in
the way the lane had diagnosed and fixed elsewhere in the same rung, and that its
deciding probe ruled out one explanation and never tested the other. All three
are fixed; two of them changed what the lane is entitled to claim.

The object plan — everything about the output obj that is **independent of the
instruction bytes** — is read off real `c2`'s obj on **870 of 870** graded TUs
and computed from IL, **without emitting**, on **854** of them. The extractor,
the two-producer design and the population are the durable deliverable, and the
predictor is forbidden by a source-level test from asking
`IlBundle::functions()` — the admission gate that returns `None` on 844 of 870.

**NEITHER COMPONENT SHIPS AS A CURVE.** Both `emitset-order` (control differs on
12 of 26) and `emitset-members` (2 of 26) are withdrawn under this lane's own
prereg §3 — *"a component whose control is red ships as `Unknown`, never as
`Differs`"* — applied uniformly. The first version of this rung withdrew the
first and shipped the second, which made the operative rule *"12 is too many, 2
is fine"*: a threshold invented after the measurement and registered nowhere.
Both predictors are still measured and published, as **characterization** keys.
What that costs is the headline; what it buys is that the prereg still means
something.

And the thing this lane actually measured **re-prices the rule the roadmap's
emit-set lane was going to be built on.** `C2_MAP.md` §3E's `0x20` bit at
`sym+0x4c` — seed set, closed under "referenced by an already-emitted function"
— is present on **331** names across the whole workload against the functions
`c2` actually emitted, and the seed is **EMPTY on 739 of the 854** TUs where the
reader answers. A closure over an empty seed is empty. §3E's cascade was
measured on a 20-function probe bundle; this is 870 real TUs.

The byte is not mis-decoded in the *uniform-zero* way, and that much is
established rather than assumed: bit 6 (`FN_FLAG_INLINABLE`) is set on **28,104
of 28,107** records and **zero** records read `0x00`. **But that discriminator
tests one explanation of the coverage gap and the first version of this rung
concluded from it as though it had tested them all.** The probe for the other one
was cheap, was not taken, and **overturns the number that carried board #3328**:
of the 28,107 records `gl_function_attrs` names, only **3,933** are functions
`c2` actually emitted — **2.4 %**, not 17.7 % — while a reader blind to the
record framing names **70,114** of them, **43.2 %**, over the *same `.gl` bytes*.
The shortfall is in the **walk**, not in the container, and *"a ceiling whichever
bit is chosen"* does not follow. §2.4.

## 1. The plan defect in the handed brief, and the fix

Deliverable 3 as literally briefed — *"REQUIRED-EXACT on the 26 matched TUs: if
the manifest is not identical on a TU we already reproduce byte-exactly, the
manifest is wrong"* — is **vacuous** if the port side of the manifest is
`observe(port_obj)`. On a `match` TU the port's obj **is** the reference's bytes
(`ObjImage::diff`, timestamp-normalized), so `manifest(port) == manifest(ref)`
follows from `port_bytes == ref_bytes` for *any* pure `manifest`. And on the
other 844 the port emits no obj at all, so the port side is **undefined** and
there is no curve. The naive reading delivers **neither** deliverable 3 nor
deliverable 4.

The fix, and it is the whole design: **two independent producers.**

* `c2_obj::ObjPlan::observe(&ObjImage)` — ground truth, off the **reference**
  obj. `Some` on **870 of 870** graded TUs.
* `c2_core::plan::predict(&IlBundle, &PlanInputs)` — the port's plan, computed
  **from IL without emitting**, and **forbidden from calling the emitter or
  `IlBundle::functions()`** by a source-level test.

### 1.1 The residual defect the control then exposed, which the plan did not anticipate

The control's rule (*"`Unknown` on a control TU is a failure — the port
demonstrably had the information"*) assumes `predict` **is** the port's emit-set
decision procedure. **It is not.** The 26 matched TUs are matched by whole-TU and
per-function recognizers that never consult the `0x20` bit. So `Differs` on a
control TU is not automatically a bug: on `TomCryptLicense.cpp` and
`ZlibLicense.cpp` it is *correct and informative*, and the instrument **names
the symbol** rather than leaving it to be re-derived by hand:

```
src/system/synth/tomcrypt/TomCryptLicense.cpp  emitset-members = differs
    — the seed does not carry `??__EsLicense@@YAXXZ` (1 name(s) missing here)
src/system/zlib/ZlibLicense.cpp                emitset-members = differs
    — the seed does not carry `??__EsLicense@@YAXXZ` (1 name(s) missing here)
```

Both are the `??__E` **dynamic initializer** — compiler-generated, carrying no
`.gl` function record for the seed to read — and both TUs are exactly the two
`IlBundle::dyninit_tu` converts, by a path that never consults the bit. The
hypothesis was *"probably the dyninit"*; the witness makes it a measurement.

`plan_control` therefore counts three things apart —
`plan-control-exact` / `-differs` / `-unknown` — and **only `differs` reds the
lane**. Folding them would have made the control unreadable the moment a
component was demoted, which is exactly what its first run caused (§2.2).

### 1.2 …AND THAT REASONING WAS THEN USED TO SHIP A COMPONENT WHOSE CONTROL WAS RED

**Fix round, and this is the review's first major.** Everything in §1.1 above is
true and none of it is a licence. The registered rule (prereg §3) does not read
*"`Differs` on a control TU is a bug"*; it reads:

> `Differs` on a control TU means the extractor or the predictor is wrong and
> the component **does not ship**. A component whose control is red ships as
> `Unknown`, never as `Differs`.

The diagnosis in §1.1 establishes exactly what that sentence says — **the
predictor is wrong on those two TUs**: the `0x20` seed cannot name a `??__E`
dynamic initializer because no `.gl` function record exists for it to read. The
rule fires. `emitset-members` does not ship.

It shipped anyway, as the headline, and the justification offered —
*"the port's emit path does not consult the `0x20` bit"* — applies **verbatim**
to `emitset-order`, which was withdrawn under the same sentence in the same
rung. The emit path does not consult `.gl` record order either. So the rule that
actually separated them was **"12 of 26 is too many, 2 of 26 is fine"**, and it
was registered nowhere. The instrument then printed `2 differs` on the same
screen as its own **"ONLY `differs` REDS THE LANE"**, on every scan, while the
lane reported outcome `instrument`.

**The repair is the registered rule, applied uniformly to both components**, and
it is deliberately the option that costs this lane its curve. The alternative
the review allowed — register a threshold as a dated, explicitly post-hoc
amendment and re-adjudicate both — was available and is declined: a threshold
whose value is chosen after seeing which side of it the lane's own component
landed on is not a rule, and a prereg amended that way once cannot be relied on
again. `emitset-order` and `emitset-members` are now both in `PLAN_COMPONENTS`,
both grade `Unknown` on every TU with a named reason, and both predictors are
published as characterization (`plan-emitset-seed-*`,
`plan-emitset-glorder-*`).

**Nothing measured is lost — what is withdrawn is the CLAIM OF DISAGREEMENT.**
And there is an independent reason it had to go, which #3329 already states in
its own words: *"an instrument must not publish disagreements generated by a
rule its own control has refuted."* §2.4 declares the `0x20` seed refuted at
workload scale. `plan-emitset-members-differs 821` was 821 disagreements
produced by that rule.

### 2.4 THE DECIDING PROBE RULED OUT ONE EXPLANATION AND CALLED IT DECIDED — AND THE OTHER ONE IS THE TRUE ONE

A number with two stories is not a measurement. This section said so, named two
stories, tested one, and concluded. **The review's third major, and the number it
overturns is the one carrying board #3328, the `W-OBJPLAN-1` DISCLOSURE
amendment and the recommendation not to dispatch the emit-set-closure lane as
briefed.**

#### What was measured originally, and what it actually decided

| key | value |
|---|---|
| `plan-glattr-names` | **28,107** `.gl` function records the reader names |
| `plan-glattr-bit6` | **28,104** — 99.99 % carry `FN_FLAG_INLINABLE` |
| `plan-glattr-zero` | **0** — no record decodes to `0x00` |
| `plan-glattr-seed` | **331** — 1.18 % carry `0x20` |

Bit 6 at 99.99 % and zero `0x00` bytes rule out the **uniform-zero** mis-decode —
the exact failure `gl_function_attrs`' own doc records having made once — and
that much stands. **They rule out nothing else.** They do not discriminate
against a walk landing on some other byte whose bit 6 is usually set, and, far
more importantly, they say nothing whatever about **coverage**:
`gl_function_attrs` advances `p += 1` past any position whose offset field is not
framed, **silently, with no refusal and no counter**, so a systematically low hit
rate is indistinguishable from a fact about `.gl`. That is board **#3237** inside
the probe built to prevent it — a lane whose whole design is a fence against it.

Worse, and this is arithmetic rather than epistemology: **"the map reaches
28,107 of 158,802 emitted functions — 17.7 %" was never a coverage figure.** It
divides the size of one set by the size of another without taking the
intersection. Both measurements are cheap and both were already in `grade`'s
hands.

#### The probe for the OTHER explanation

Two intersections against the reference obj's own emitted set, per TU, plus an
**orthogonal** reader — `c2_il::mangled_names`, which walks `.gl` symbol runs in
file order and **does not use `gl_offset_framed` at all**:

| key | value | of `plan-emitset-observed-size` **162,146** |
|---|---|---|
| `plan-glattr-names` | 28,107 | — |
| **`plan-glattr-in-emitset`** | **3,933** | **2.4 %** |
| `plan-glruns-names` | 876,555 | — |
| **`plan-glruns-in-emitset`** | **70,114** | **43.2 %** |

**THE 17.7 % WAS A FACT ABOUT THE SCANNER, AND IT WAS NOT EVEN THE RIGHT
NUMBER.** Of the 28,107 records `gl_function_attrs` names, only **3,933** are
functions `c2` actually emitted — the reader reaches **2.4 %** of the emitted
set, not 17.7 %. And over the **same `.gl` bytes**, a reader blind to the framing
names **70,114** of them: `.gl` demonstrably carries the name of at least
**43.2 %** of the emitted set, **eighteen times** what the attribute walk
reaches.

So the coverage shortfall is **in the walk, not in the container**, and the
conclusion that was drawn from it does not follow:

> ~~*"the map covers only 17.7 % of the emitted set — a ceiling on anything keyed

## 4. What the manifest CANNOT see — stated, because #3237

* Every instruction byte, and therefore `.text` section sizes, the COMDAT aux
  `CheckSum`, symbol `Value` for in-body labels, relocation `VirtualAddress`
  inside code, `.pdata` prolog/epilog fields, and any `.debug$S` subsection
  keyed to a code offset.
* The 8 `capture-fail` TUs. No reference obj → **not a row**, never a false row.
* Anything about **correctness**. `plan-*-exact` is **necessary but not
  sufficient** for `match`; a TU can be plan-exact and mismatch on every byte.
* One thing deliberately **retained and flagged** rather than dropped:
  `PlanSection::byte_len` for **non**-`.text` sections. `.pdata`'s length is

  a function of the *instruction bytes*, so both sit inside the
  body-independence invariant, but both are body-*correlated* in the weaker
  sense that a different emit set moves them. They live in `sections` and are
  graded in their own component, never folded into the emit-set curve.

**Denominator: TUs.** Option A's target is stated in TUs (870 of 878), so the
curve's denominator is the goal's. This sidesteps #3254 entirely — the `fnbyte`
denominator is 71.2 % bodies the shipped image never contains because `/Gy`

problem. A TU is a TU; there is no discard.

### 7.3 Two-sided prices for the two declines

**(a) The other three accessor agreements at workload scale** (finding 5).
*Cost of doing it:* `section_names`, `weak_externals` and `text_comdat_relocs_named`
are list-valued per TU, so a workload-scale TU-by-TU comparison needs either a
per-record listing in `--plan-tsv` (353,043 section rows + 4,009 weak rows +
1,323,456 relocation rows against today's 870) or three more summary counters
whose disagreement would be unattributable to a TU — the first is a ~40× file and
the second is the shape #3237 warns about.
*Cost of not doing it:* the three accessors are agreed on **3 synthetic objs**,
and `plan-obs-sections` / `-weak-records` / `-reloc-records` are published off
the new walk on that basis. **What is actually at risk is bounded**: none of the
three is used as a denominator by anything (only the emit set is, and that half
IS now covered at workload scale), all three are `Unobservable`-side inventory
that no component grades against, and a drift would show up as an inventory
figure moving with no code change — which the identity discipline already
catches. **Declined, and the decline is the cheaper side by a wide margin.** The
deliverable it owes is named instead: a section lane needs the section listing
anyway, and it should carry this comparison rather than a lane whose components
do not touch sections.

**(b) Automated coverage of the `--plan-tsv` CLI plumbing** (finding 8).
*Cost of doing it:* the only honest test is a subprocess `c2rs gap --plan-tsv`
over at least one real TU, which means a capture; `cli_flags`'
`scripts_invocation_roster` already costs **338.94 s** of a 117 s parallel suite
(board **#3308** measured it: 99.1 % of that binary is three subprocess tests),
and a row that captures adds to the suite's longest pole.
*Cost of not doing it:* the flag's *parse* is covered (`fence_count.rs` and the
`Spec` table), the *writer* and *parser* are covered by the round-trip unit test
on real `PlanRow`s, and `derive_metrics` vs `metrics()` is covered in-process
over the real key block. What is uncovered is exactly one line —
`std::fs::write(path, plan_tsv(&report.plan_rows()))` — and its failure mode is
loud (no file, or an unparseable one, and the offline check refuses fail-closed
rather than skipping rows). **Declined**: the uncovered surface is one `write`
and the fence is a suite-longest-pole subprocess.
