**F4a — the single-producer rule, and it fits 6 of 6.** *The producer is hoisted
to slot 0; a store issues in source order as soon as it is ready; a store whose
value was produced at slot `p` is not ready before slot `p+3`.* Derive `o1`: `li`
at 0, `a` ready at 1, `b` not ready until 3 so `c` takes 2, `b` at 3, `d` at 4 —
`li·a·c·b·d`, which is what c2 emits. Same derivation reproduces `o2`, `o3`,
`o4`, `o6`, `o8` and `w1`. **The latency bound is a positive check with a count:
across all fifteen producers the minimum producer→consumer gap is 3, it is
attained in six of them, and it is violated in none.**

**F4b — the multi-producer distribution, and F4a does NOT cover it.** `o7` has
three producers and they are at **0, 2, 4** — strided by two, each separated by
one non-literal store, with all three literal stores packed at the tail (gaps 5,
4, 3). `o5` is the same regime with two (gaps 4, 3). **F4a predicts 0, 1, 2 and
is wrong.** And the target function is a *third* regime: its producers are at
**0, 2, 5** (gaps 4, 5, 5), and — unlike `o5` and `o7` — its literal store is
**not** packed at the tail but left in source position at slot 4. `xboxheap`'s
three producers are three different kinds (a constant, an address bind consumed
**twice**, and a live range spanning a **call**); `o7`'s are three independent
`li`s. **Neither F4a nor the `o5`/`o7` stride describes `xboxheap`.**

So the ordering is **at least two independent decisions, and the second is
unfitted.** That is consistent with `w-pair` §4, which refuted six candidate
placement rules on this axis, and with `work/w-frame/…/leaf_store.rs`, which
records four *register-allocation* rules each refuted by another cell.

**F4a is a FIT and I am not shipping it.** Six cells is how this project ships
wrong bytes, and it is offered here only as the instrument that **refutes** the
one-decision reading — a rule that fits six cells and fails on the target is
evidence about the target, not a rule.

### 5.3 What they refuted

* **They refuted the widening I would have written.** `s5_addr_signed_off` sits
  at offset **32768** — the first offset a signed 16-bit `addi` displacement
  cannot encode — and `s7_bound_32764` at **32764** is the last one that can.
  Sink off, `s7` is `1/1 in class` via **`addr-leaf`** and `s5` falls through to
  `expr-op-0x27`. That reads exactly like *"`addr-leaf`'s bound is too
  conservative, relax it"* — the shape of the `!=`→`>` trap on record. **It is
  wrong.** `crates/c2-il/src/func/body/shapes/leaf_addr.rs:129` bounds `off` to
  `-0x8000..=0x7FFF` because `AddrLeaf` lowers to **one `addi`**, and one `addi`
  genuinely cannot hold 32768. Real c2 emits **two** instructions there —
  `addis 3,3,1 ; addi 3,3,-32768` — and 80000 is `addis 3,3,1 ; addi 3,3,14464`.
  Relaxing the bound would have shipped a wrong object. The sink's path is
  byte-exact **only because it is a different lowering**
  (`[Load, Lit(k), Add]` through the generic constant materialiser), not because
  the bound was wrong.
* **They confirmed the `Vocab::CallArg` correspondence guard, and confirmed it
  REFUSES rather than mis-emits.** `expr.rs:400`'s comment warns that widening
  `parse_expr` obliges `mcall::eat_int_operands`'s `Vocab::CallArg` to widen in
  lockstep *"or §9.14.6's correspondence guard goes red."* Measured: with the
  sink on, `s9_callarg_big` (**one** computed-address argument) enters class and
  is byte-exact, while `s10_callarg_two` (`g(&p->b, &p->a)`) and
  `s11_callarg_mixed` (`g(&p->b, x)`) refuse at **`call-arg-computed:eof`** and
  return `not_implemented`. So the coupled widening is what you would need to
  **gain** the multi-argument cases; it is not what stands between the promotion
  and a wrong emit. That is a **narrowing** of #364's cost estimate in the
  direction that makes it cheaper — while §4 makes the *payoff* very much
  smaller, so the ratio still points at decline.

   This is the cheapest of the five and it is worth **+0** on its own.
