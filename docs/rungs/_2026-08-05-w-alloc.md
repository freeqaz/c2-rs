## 1. Result

`docs/STORE_SCHEDULE.md` §4 registered the allocation as *"a SECOND INPUT and it
is open"*, and `crates/c2-il/src/func/body/shapes/leaf_store.rs` carried **four
fitted allocation rules, each refuted by one of the others**. All four are now
derived consequences of one rule with **zero free parameters**.

> **ALLOC.** Order the run's distinct producers by (1) **use count,
> descending**; (2) on a tie, **register-derived before constant**; (3) on a tie
> within the register-derived, **source order**; (4) on a tie within the
> constants, **REVERSE source order**. Hand out `r11`, `r10`, `r9`, … descending
> over the free volatiles minus the live-in formals. `r12` is never used.

| population | cells | in domain | ALLOC exact | **wrong** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| fit | 242 | 236 | **236** (100.0 %) | **0** |
| **holdout** | 284 | 257 | **250** (97.3 %) | **0** |
| killer cells (external) | 6 | 6 | **6** | **0** |

**The seven holdout cells not predicted are REFUSALS, not errors** — the model
returned `None`. They are exactly the preregistered structural holdout clause 4
(`nf ≥ 6`), the regime §6 of the prereg named in advance as *"where the pool
descends into freed formal registers"*, and that is what they do: `r5`, `r4`,
and in two cells **`r3` — the base pointer itself**, reused after its last use.
Scored over predictions actually made, the holdout is **250 of 250**.

The holdout was declared before `grid.py` was written, partitioned by the
generator, and scored only after ALLOC was frozen at commit **`8973ffc`**.
`search.py` **refuses** to open any path containing `holdout` — a raise, not a
convention.

## 2. The corroborating negative — the more valuable half

Preregistered in §5 and run **before** ALLOC was scored: an exhaustive search
over the class every textbook answer lives in — 4 scan directions × 3
assignment points × 2 pool walks × 2,184 signed lexicographic keys over 7 base
features = **52,416 priority-function allocators**.

```
CEILING 179 of 236 fit cells  (75.8%)

RESIDUAL by (shared producers m, distinct producers P):
  m=0, P=2      miss   0 of  10        m=1, P=3      miss   0 of  72
  m=0, P=3      miss   0 of  13        m=2, P=2      miss  13 of  34
  m=1, P=2      miss   0 of  23        m=2, P=3      miss  44 of  84
```

**Every miss is in a cell where two producers tie on the use count, and there
are zero misses everywhere else.** Clauses 3 and 4 carry **opposite signs
inside one sort**, and no lexicographic key over a fixed feature set can carry a
tiebreak whose sign depends on the value being tied on. So the answer was never
in the class four lanes had been searching — the same finding, in the same
shape, as `w-sched`'s 13,104-configuration result about its rule 2.

## 3. Two corrections to my own prereg, both made on FIT and both recorded

* **H1 was wrong about the axis.** It said *"SHARED producers in reverse source
  order, then SIMPLE producers in source order"*. That is the special case of
  clause 1 when every count is 1 or 2 — which is **every cell the 36-probe
  discovery set contained**, so it fitted perfectly and was still the wrong
  generalisation. `supp.py`'s unequal-count patterns (`00111`, `01111`,
  `011110`) refuted it: the value used *three* times takes `r11` over a value
  used twice, for **every producer kind**. The axis is the **use count**.
* **The producer KIND matters, on a tie.** `w-sched` §2 measured kind-invariance
  for the *order* and I carried it over to the register. It does not hold:
  `{a=1;b=2;c=1;d=2}` reverses (constants) and `{a=&x;b=&y;c=&x;d=&y}` does not
  (register-derived). Mixed-kind runs put the register-derived one in `r11`.
  **Clause 2 is shipped as a refusal, not a rule** — it is supported only by the
  supplementary probe and was never in the holdout.

**The guard is inert and that is the design.** The store emitter builds the
producer list, asks `alloc::all_in`, and refuses unless every producer lands in
`r11`. Today the parser admits only one-value runs, for which ALLOC says `r11`
— so nothing changes. The point is board **#232**: a parser widening that
turned a clean refusal into a live wrong emit and survived 255 commits. A
widening that reaches this guard now gets a refusal instead of a wrong register.

## 6. Found and not taken

1. **The store order when every store is produced** (board **#544**) — the
   biggest single item this lane opens. ALLOC plus the measured hoist (the first
   consumer of the **strictly** highest-count producer moves into the head, and
   nothing moves on a tie) gives **198/236 fit, 219/250 holdout** on the full
   sequence. The residual is structured but **not one family**: 35 of the 38 fit
   misses are counts `(2,2,1)` with no unproduced store, and the holdout adds a
   second family at counts `(2,1)` with two unproduced stores. Next probe: that
   second family is a `w-sched` regime, so run it on `w-sched`'s own grid shape
   with the registers recorded, which its `canon` threw away.
2. **The reuse choice** (board **#541**). The discriminating pair is already
   compiled: `{a=1;b=2;c=3;d=4}` reuses `r11`, `{a=u+1;…;d=u+4}` takes `r8`.
   One axis separates them and it is not producer count.
3. **The multiply regime** (`docs/ALLOC.md` §4) is *nearly* a rule already —
   one at a time, in `r11`, consumers grouped, groups in count-descending
   order. It fits every probed `M` cell. It is not shipped because no `M` cell
   was in the holdout; a small held-out multiply grid would close it cheaply.
4. **`r12`** (board **#543**) — never allocated, unexplained. Cheap to settle
   against `docs/whitebox/` if anyone is in c2's allocator already; that would
   need a `DISCLOSURE.md` row naming the address.
5. **`w-sched`'s `canon` discards the registers.** Its grid is 504 cells that
   could have answered this lane's question on the day, and did not, because the
   observation was summarised before it was stored. Recording the register cost
   nothing here and was the whole difference.
