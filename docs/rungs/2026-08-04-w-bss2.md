## The headline

`w-bss` closed with nine open items, and named §8.8 — *"§5's allocator is
validated on probes only"* — as the largest. It now is validated, on the **117
non-COMDAT `.bss` and 68 non-COMDAT `.data` sections in the workload that define
two or more symbols**: every real section where the allocator can be wrong.

**The result is not "the model holds at N %". It is that the question was in the
wrong place.**

> The allocator is a **plain bump with no free list** — exact on 110/117 real
> `.bss`, 68/68 real `.data`, and 38/38 probe cells. Hole reuse, pass-over and
> best-fit are not three allocators; each is a different story about the order
> the objects were visited in, and every one of them emits a layout that is a
> bump in *some* order.
>
> What is open is the **walk order**, and it is wider than §5.5 implied: Rule A1
> reproduces **85 of 110** real `.bss` sections and Rule A2 **45 of 68** real
> `.data`. It is **47 of 48** on two-object `.bss` sections and 38 of 62 above
> that.

§8.8 also turns out to have asked for the wrong thing. It proposed re-censusing
with the **raw section bytes** retained (board #178). The allocator's *output*
was already in `sections.jsonl` — every defined symbol's `Value`. What was
missing was its *input*: size, alignment, linkage and declaration order, none of
which is in the obj at all. They are in the IL `.gl` that c2 is handed, and
locating them (§5.6) is what made the grading possible. 102 MB of raw bytes
would have answered a different question.

## Closed

* **§8.8 — probe-only validation of §5.** Graded on real objs; see above.
* **§8.3 — Rule Y2 has no held-out confirmation.** The two fitted cells each had
  a single linkage, so neither could see a linkage split. The discriminating cell
  — four deferred objects, two extern and two static, one `.bss` — gives symbol
  order `d2 d1 p2 p1`, the `.gl` order with no split. N = 3, 5, 7, 9 all confirm.
  A fifth cell shows **Y1 and Y2 compose**.
* **§8.4 — `.tls$` walk order unmeasured.** Rule T1: one section, **two blocks,
  uninitialized first**, each walked **backwards** — reverse `.gl` for the
  uninitialized block, reverse declaration order for the initialized one,
  ascending object size within each. Ten cells. The mirror image of `.bss`/`.data`.
* **How a port obtains "declaration order"** (implicit in Rule A2 and never
  addressed): the `.gl` record id is assigned in declaration order and reproduces
  all three of §5.3's transcribed `.data` orders on TUs whose `.gl` *file* order
  is the permuted one.

## Not closed, and stated as a boundary

* **§8.1 — mixed-size allocation.** Reframed: the four probe failures and both
  §5.5 counterexamples (which reproduce byte-for-byte) are walk-order deviations,
  not allocator failures. The best registered walk scores 19/20 held out but
  misses both controls, **and the two controls need different mechanisms** — cell
  10 is reproduced by hole reuse and not by pass-over, cell 11 the other way
  round. No member of a 13-model zoo gets both. The registered decline outcome
  ("state the boundary instead") applies and was registered as acceptable.
* **The walk order itself** is now item 1 of §8 and the only thing between here
  and a writer. §5.7.1 characterises the 25 failing `.bss` sections precisely —
  the deferred clause is right in 24 of 25, the eager block is a *near*-`.gl`
  order (median 0.17 inversions, 9 of 25 a single adjacent transposition), and
  the transposed pairs share no size, alignment or linkage property.
* **Negative result worth its own line.** If c2 assigned a sequential id while
  consuming the `.gl` and walked its own `bucket = id & 0x3ff` table
  bucket-ascending (§7.4, lane `w-map`), the walk would be file order below 1024
  records and interleave above — which would have explained both the probe grid
  and the real-TU deviation at once. **It does not hold:** 1 of 12, chance. Do
  not retry it.
* **§8.10, new.** The `.gl` id is read by scanning **backwards**, which is right
  on small TUs and demonstrably wrong on some large ones. Every `.data` number
  above inherits that error bar; the `.bss` numbers, which depend only on file
  order, do not.

## Method notes for the next lane

* `c2rs capture` hard-codes `/Ox /GS- /c` and takes neither `--flags-file` nor
  `--cwd`, so it **cannot capture a real workload TU**. `work/w-bss2/cap.py`
  mirrors `Toolchain::capture_il_with` instead of modifying `crates/`.
* Front-end-only capture of all 871 TUs takes about two minutes at 16-way and
  writes nothing to disk. The 102 MB obj problem `w-bss` had does not recur.
* Two mechanism bugs each looked exactly like an allocator failure and each cost
  20 points before it was found: Rule A1's eager/deferred split simply not being
  applied (33 % → 56 %), and the dynamic-initializer marker having **two**
  spellings — a `??__E` thunk named after the object's *path* for a
  namespace-scope object but after its *whole decorated name* for a class static
  member (56 % → 76 %). Anyone grading a layout rule should score the
  bump-in-address-order test **first**, separately, before scoring any model.
