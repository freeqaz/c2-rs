## 2. THE RULE

> **Rule 1 — store order.** Walk the source statements in order and emit the
> earliest store that is *allowed*. A store whose value needs a new instruction
> to materialise it — a **produced** store — may not occupy store position 0 or
> 1. Stores through different base **symbols** may not be reordered past each
> other. If everything remaining is blocked, source order wins.
>
> **Rule 2 — producer placement.** The producers, in source order, are inserted
> immediately *before* the stores at store positions 0, 1, 2, … — one producer
> per store slot, from the top of the block.

The only free constant is the **2**.

## 3. THE NEGATIVE RESULT, which is half the deliverable

**It is not a machine scheduler.** Producer *kind* does not touch the
permutation. `li`, `addi` from the store's own base, `addi` from a different
base, `addi` from a formal, `rlwinm`, and **`mulli`** — several cycles slower on
this part — give the **byte-identical** order at every consumer position. Store
width does not touch it either: `stb`, `sth`, `stw` and `std` all emit
`P S0 S2 S1 S3` for the same statement list.

The preregistered family — forward/backward × producer→consumer latency 1..6 ×
a lexicographic priority key over up to three signed features from {natural
index, statement index, is-producer, is-store, depth, height}, with memory
dependences in the DAG, **13,104 configurations** — was searched on the fit
partition **before** SCHED existed:

```
best: 89 / 146     forward, L = 3, priority "defer producers"
per tier: t1 25/27   t2 21/21   t3 15/15   t4 0/48   t6 19/26   t7 9/9
```

> ### **The residual is EXACTLY the two-producer tier: 0 of 48.** Every one-producer tier is at or near 100 % and every two-producer cell fails. Rule 2 is an *insertion* rule — "the m-th producer goes before the m-th store" — and no priority function over a dependence DAG can express it, which is why a family that contains the right latency and the right direction still cannot reach the answer. **This is the arity result the brief asked for, and it is a negative one: the schedule is not in the hypothesis class four previous lanes were searching.**

**Prereg R7 (≥ 95 % from the declared family) is a MISS, and the miss is the
finding.** The wrong ones stay on the page.

## 4. `xboxheap` WAS NEVER A THIRD REGIME — a premise in my brief, refuted

My brief, `w-dclass`/B §3.4 and board **#402** all state that `xboxheap`'s three
producers sit at **0, 2, 5** and that this is a third regime neither F4a nor the
`o5`/`o7` stride describes.

**There are two store-value producers and they sit at 0 and 2** — the ordinary
two-producer slots rule 2 gives. The **5** is `mr r31,r3`, the live-range save
of `this` across the trailing `bl`. It is not a store producer; no store
consumes it. Counting it as one is what made the TU look irreducible.

Read off the **real obj**, compiled from `src/xdk/nuispeech/xboxheap.cpp` at the
workload's own flags — not off a rung's transcription of it:

```
li   r10,0        P0        mCount = 0
stw  r5,16(r3)    S0        mSize = size
addi r11,r3,8     P1        &listHead
stw  r3,0(r3)     S1        mFreeHead = this
stw  r10,20(r3)   S2        mCount
mr   r31,r3                 <- the live-range save, §5 of STORE_SCHEDULE.md
stw  r3,4(r3)     S3        mUsedHead = this
stw  r11,8(r3)    S4        listHead.mNext
stw  r11,12(r3)   S5        listHead.mPrev
```

SCHED predicts `P0 S0 P1 S1 S2 S3 S4 S5`. **Exact.** `crates/c2-core/src/codegen/schedule.rs`
carries it as `xboxheap_constructor_is_derived_not_fitted`.

And `o7` — F4b's declared-unfitted cell, where F4a predicted producers at 0,1,2
and measured 0,2,4 — is `P1 S0 P2 S4 P3 S1 S2 S3`, derived. **Prereg R2 HIT.**

## 5. `w-pair`'s six refuted rules died on an ARTIFACT

`w-pair` §4 ends on `F1`/`F2` — the same statement structure with the two
pointer parameters exchanged, both gap 1 — and concludes that *"every surviving
fit had to be stated in terms of a specific register number, which is the
signature of fitting a machine scheduler"*.

The axis is not the register. It is the **base symbol of the store's address
expression**, and a bound reference `B& l = h->lh` is its own symbol even though
it folds to `8(r3)`:

| cell | symbols | order | |
|---|---|---|---|
| `E2` | `h h h h` | reordered, gap 3 | one symbol — free |
| `E1` | `g h h g` | source order, gap 1 | two symbols — pinned |
| `F1`/`F2` | `l b b l` / `l a a l` | source order, gap 1 | two symbols — pinned, in **both** halves of the swap |
| **`D5`** | `l h h l` | source order, gap 1 | **two symbols through ONE machine base register** |

`D5` is the discriminating cell and it is why the register reading looked
plausible. Tier 6 isolates the axis positively: the same statement list with the
store destinations split `pppp` / `pqqp` / `ppqq` / `qppq` gives reordered /
pinned / pinned-after-one / pinned, exactly as the symbol count predicts, with
the **producer's own base held fixed**. And the axis w-pair never varied
cleanly — producer base — turns out to be **irrelevant**: tier 2's `A` (producer
reads the store's base) and `Q` (producer reads the other pointer) are
permutation-identical at every consumer index.

**Prereg R5 is a MISS.** I registered that the discriminating axis was "producer
base == store base" and predicted `g1` at gap 1. The producer's base does not
matter at all; the *store's* symbol does. The prediction was wrong and the
correction is the finding.

### 5.2 Constructed counterexamples — including one against a shape the port accepts

The load-valued store run is a **different regime**: real `c2` leaves the `lwz`
at its source position (it may alias the stores, so it cannot move) and sends
its consuming store to the **end** of the run — `ld0` is `lwz ; S1 ; S2 ; S3 ;
S0`, where SCHED would say `S1 ; S2 ; S0 ; S3`. So I asked whether the port
emits that shape. **It does not, and it refuses honestly**:

| probe | census | `c2rs diff` |
|---|---|---|
| `ld0`..`ld3` — a loaded value at each position of a 4-store run | `0/1 in class`, `expr-op-0x27` | **`Port=NotImplemented`** ×4 |
| `sm` — a literal mixed with a formal (F1) | `0/1 in class` | **`Port=NotImplemented`** |
| `sl3` — the all-same-literal run the port DOES accept | **`1/1 in class`** | **`Port=Match`**, `ReferenceReplay=ByteExact` |

**6 printed verdicts, 5 honest refusals and 1 byte-exact match** — a positive
count, not an absence. No live wrong emit was found on this axis.

**The guard that came out of it** (board #525). `leaf/store.rs` emits in source
order. SCHED says that is right for everything the parser admits today — an
all-formal run has no producer, an all-same-literal run has one producer shared
by *every* store so rule 1's fallback fires — and **wrong** the moment the
parser admits a mixed run. The emitter now builds the statement list, asks
`schedule::is_source_order`, and refuses when it disagrees. Inert today.
**#232 is the precedent**: a parser widening turned a clean refusal into a live
wrong emit that survived 255 commits with every standing instrument green.

## 6. What SCHED does NOT cover, measured rather than waved at

When the register allocator hands a producer a register that is still the data
source of a store the schedule has not emitted, the write-after-read
anti-dependence perturbs the order.

| distinct producers | allocation clean | conflicts |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 6 | **0** |
| 1 | 148 | **0** |
| 2 | 109 | 10 |
| 3 | 54 | 67 |
| 4 | 3 | 107 |

**SCHED is exact on 320 of 320 clean cells and 0 of 184 conflicted ones.** The
partition is decided from the register assignment alone, *before* the order is
looked at, so it cannot be tuned to make a miss look explained — that check
replaced a first version which merely asked "does *some* anti-dependence exist",
and which scored `0 unexplained` on everything. **That first version was
absence-read-as-success wearing a different hat, and it is recorded here because
it very nearly shipped.**

**With 0 or 1 producers it never happens: 154 of 154.** `xboxheap` has two
producers and is clean.

**I did not fit the allocator.** `leaf_store.rs` carries four refuted allocation
rules; a fifth fitted here would be exactly what this lane was sent to stop
doing. What can be said is bounded and positive: the conflict is a
register-pressure phenomenon that needs ≥ 2 producers, and the schedule is a
function of the IL **plus the allocation**, not of the IL alone. That is the
honest arity: **SCHED is complete for the IL; the allocation is a second input
and it is open.**

## 7. `xboxheap` re-prices 5 → 3, and #269 stops firing

`#401` priced it at **5**: F1 (a literal-valued store mixed into a store run),
F2 (a member's address as a stored value), F3 (a call after a store run), and
**F4a + F4b, the schedule, as two unmodelled decisions.**

F4a and F4b are now **one modelled fact**. The count of independent *unmodelled*
constructs is **3**, and board #269's clause — *a frontier TU at ≥ 4 independent
unmodelled constructs is not a target* — **no longer fires**. `xboxheap` is the
only frontier TU in that position: `w-dclass` §4's reprice put the next cheapest
genuinely-buildable member at 4, and C's independent third count put
`negate_test` at 10.

**It does not convert here and I did not attempt it. Prereg R10 is a HIT.** What
remains is three parse-layer facts plus **one instruction-order fact with
n = 1** — where the `mr r31,r3` live-range save goes. It sits at the slot rule 2
would give a *third* producer whose slot index were 3 rather than 2. One
instance. Recorded as a hypothesis with a mechanism, in `STORE_SCHEDULE.md` §5,
and explicitly **not** as a rule; it needs its own grid (a call with a live range
across it, crossed against the number of store producers).

## 10. Found and not taken

1. **The load-valued store run is a third schedule regime** — the producer is
   pinned at its source position by aliasing and its consuming store goes to the
   **end** of the run, not to position 2. Four cells, all refused by the port
   today, so nothing is wrong; but a lane that widens `expr-op-0x27` into the
   loaded case inherits a rule this lane did not measure. §5.2.
2. **The `mr r31,r3` placement**, n = 1. §7.
3. **The register allocator's choice of producer register.** It descends from
   r11, skips registers whose formal is dead, and in at least one measured cell
   (`t4_ll_0_1`, n = 6) takes a **live** formal's register while two dead ones
   are free. Four refuted rules already exist for this; I add no fifth, and I
   did not spend the one-shot gate on it.
4. **The one-shot Part-1 gate is UNSPENT.** SCHED did not need it: the holdout
   is out-of-sample by construction and was scored against a frozen, committed
   model, and every cell is graded by real `c2` bytes rather than by a model
   score. I am the lane the eighteen declines were pointing at and the answer is
   still no — a quarantine guards against a fitted model with free parameters,
   and this model has **one** constant and is graded on exact permutations.
