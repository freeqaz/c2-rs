## 1. The finding, and it is a reading of two lines rather than a measurement

Board **#746** fences two things. `w-slots` (**#3148**) settled which is which:
**fence B** is `label_slots -> None` in `c2-il`, **fence A** is the backward-
reference refusal in `c2-core`. `#3089` priced fence A at *"0 conversions and 0
bytes"*, was correct, stayed correct, and was made binding by `w-layout`'s own
success — **#3144**, *superseded by construction*.

This lane's finding is that fence A was never doing the job it cites, and that
it says so itself in three places:

* **The stated rule** (`labels.rs` module header, rule 2) quantifies over
  **bodies**: *"every body with a BACKWARD intra-section branch charges the
  compiler-label counter >= +1, 11 of 11"*.
* **The enforcing line** — `LabelMap::resolve`'s `if target <= r.at` — quantifies
  over **references routed through this map**.
* **Four shipped byte-exact classes live in the gap.** `ptr_walk_loop`,
  `ptr_walk_chain_loop`, `json_utf8_copy` and `xtea_encrypt_loop` each emit a
  backward `bc` through `reach::direct` / `encode_bc`; the map never sees one.
  The module's own 2026-08-08 correction records the fact — *"neither carrier
  routes through this map"* — and does not draw the consequence.
* **`labels.rs` reason 3 states where the rule belongs**, and it is not here:
  *"the precondition is TU-level and this map is per-body … that is where a loop
  rung's relaxation belongs; it is not here."*

So the enforcing line has **never** enforced its stated rule. What it did, from
`w-layout` onward, was block **7 of the 8** residual sites at a counter benefit
of **zero**.

This is `H1`, registered at p 0.80 before the first `crates/` change, with the
lane declining every arm if it were false.

### 1.1 It is a FOURTH failure mode, and three of the four are one disease

The generalization is worth more than the lift, so it is stated as a rule and not
as a fact about `labels.rs`.

| # | the item | how the prose and the code came apart |
|---|---|---|
| **#3114** (`w-ir-g`) | `CFG_SHAPE.md` §6.2 item **G** | the item's **sentence** ("band 3 or refuse") would have refused a **shipped byte-exact class**; its **title** ("per accepted shape") is the half that bound |
| **#3119** (`w-item-d`) | §6.2 item **D** | the **sentence** ("this must be in the fixup pass") put a **re-layout** where `resolve(&mut [u8])` structurally cannot hold it — a slice cannot grow |
| **#3144** (`w-layout`) | board **#3089** | a measurement that was **correct, stayed correct**, and was invalidated by a **peer lane's success** |
| **this row** (`w-fencea`) | `LabelMap` invariant 4 | the **enforcing line never matched its own sentence, from the day it shipped** |

**#3144 is its own disease** — a population changing underneath a number that
never stopped being true. **The other three are one disease: the prose and the
code quantify over different sets.** In every case the doc-comment states a rule
over one population and the predicate below it decides a different one, and **the
gap is invisible to every instrument this repo owns**: `board_audit.sh` checks
that cited numbers have rows and that anchors resolve, and cannot compare a
module header's quantifier with a predicate three hundred lines down; the gate
grades bytes, and bytes are identical on both readings; the 878-TU identity diff
grades outcomes, and the outcome of a rule nobody's corpus exercises is nothing.

The cheap general fix is the one this lane happened to use, and it is worth
copying: **make the rule's own text name its population, and then write the test
so it reads the OTHER crate's predicate rather than a copy of its number.**
`every_admitted_class_has_a_registered_control_flow_surcharge` is that test —
it goes red if `c2-il` moves and this crate does not, which is the only kind of
drift a doc comment cannot catch by being well written.

## 2. What replaces it: a closed admission, graded against c2-il's own gate

`LabelMap::new()` is unchanged and is `BackEdge::Refused`, so **all nine of item

### 4.1 The one that is worth reading: `ptr_walk_chain_loop`'s SAME regime

Registered as a decline because the emitter is 638 lines of variable arity. It
needed **no** new mechanism, and the reason is a **scope correction to `#3142`**
rather than a fault in it.

`w-layout`'s `P17` — *"all thirteen lowerings also **publish** offsets off the
same `t.len()`, so not one of the eight could have moved its branches alone"* —
was that lane's load-bearing prediction and is why `FinishedBody::start_of` had
to exist. **It is false of `ptr_walk_chain_loop`, and of nothing else in this
residue.** That class publishes **nothing**: no `bl`, no pooled constant, no
prologue length, every branch self-relative. So there the branches really *were*
the whole problem. `#3142` holds on twelve of the thirteen it surveyed; what this
adds is that **"a lowering that publishes nothing" is a real category with
exactly one member**, and for that member the migration is cheap.

The decline itself was registered off the emitter's **line count**, which is the
same shape of estimate that produced `#3144`'s own `P19` miss — a row written
from a grep rather than from the emitters. It cost nothing here because the arm
was attempted anyway, and it is filed because *"638 lines"* is not a fence.

What it did need is a **block split**: in the SAME regime the preamble jumps
*into* the record form, which `S2` puts at slot `M + 1`, the body's last. So the
body is two blocks and the JUMPIN `b` names the second, in place of
`4 * (M + 2)` counted off the body's own length. `M6b` is the mutant that says
the split is load-bearing, and its separating control is `wvl_chain3` — the same
class in the **TWO** regime, green under it.
